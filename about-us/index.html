From Dhading to Namaste Hiking Trek

Lal Gurung grew up in Dhading and built his career carrying packs and guiding travelers through Nepal’s mountain regions. Over more than 20 years, his work has taken him along the trails of Everest, Annapurna, Manaslu, Mustang, and Langtang.

Those years shaped the kind of company he wanted to build: a local team where travelers could speak directly with people who understood the route, and where the work of guides and porters was treated with care and respect.

Namaste Hiking Trek launched in 2026 as the next chapter of work developed through Nepal Intrepid Treks, whose history began in 2016. We bring that earlier experience into a company focused on personal trip planning, local knowledge, and the relationships that make a mountain journey possible from the guide walking beside you to the family preparing your evening meal.

Today, Lal remains involved in itinerary planning, pre-trek briefings, and coordination with our mountain team. Together with Kamal and our guides, he helps travelers prepare for their journey and supports the practical decisions that arise along the way.

"Our purpose is to make Himalayan travel personal, well organized, and considerate of the people and places involved. We want mountain communities to benefit from the journeys we organize and future travelers to experience the landscapes and cultures of our mountain regions."

Safety and Support in the Mountains

Mountain travel requires preparation and decisions that respond to the conditions. We discuss the demands of your route before booking and include acclimatization days in our high-altitude itineraries.

On the trail, your guide checks on how you are feeling and has the authority to adjust the pace, change the route, arrange descent, or stop a journey when conditions or someone’s health make that necessary. Tell your guide promptly if you feel unwell or have concerns about continuing.

Our Kathmandu team helps coordinate medical and transport support when needed. The available response and its timing depend on the circumstances, access, and assistance available.

Insurance Requirement: Travel insurance is required for your journey. Before traveling, check the planned activities and highest point on your itinerary against your policy, including its medical treatment and evacuation provisions. Ask us for itinerary details to share with your insurer.

Respect and Care for Our Mountain Crews

Porters, guides, and camping staff make these journeys possible. Their welfare is an essential part of how we plan and operate every trip.

  • Manageable Loads: We allocate bags for up to two trekkers per porter with strict weight caps.
  • Warm Winter Gear: Appropriate mountain clothing, sturdy footwear, and high-altitude equipment provided.
  • Medical & Evacuation Insurance: Comprehensive policy covering medical care and emergency extraction.
  • Fair Payment: Living wages paid promptly, respecting national industry standards and fair trail treatment.

Please follow the luggage allowance confirmed for your trip. If you need extra equipment or have questions about how your bags will be carried, tell us during planning so we can discuss the staffing required.

Responsible Travel in Daily Practice

How we protect mountain ecosystems, support local villages, and honor sacred Himalayan traditions on every trail.

Spend Locally

We use family-run teahouses and mountain lodges and encourage meals made with locally grown ingredients. Accommodation, meals, guiding, and porter work connect your trip directly with the households and businesses along the route.

Reduce Waste

Our teams carry non-biodegradable packaging to appropriate disposal points and encourage water refills to reduce disposable plastic bottles. Ask your planner about water arrangements and what filtration to bring for your chosen trek.

Respect Local Life

Our guides explain customs around monasteries, sacred sites, village life, and photography. They help you understand the Sherpa, Tamang, Gurung, and other communities you encounter and approach visits with consideration for local expectations.

How We Handle Real Trail Crises: The Lukla Weather Protocol

During monsoon transition periods, Lukla mountain flights can be grounded for days by thick cloud cover. When flight cancellations hit the airport, foreign agencies often leave trekkers stranded in hotel lobbies.

At Namaste Hiking Trek, our Kathmandu logistics desk immediately activates pre-planned overland contingency: dispatching private 4WD jeeps via the Salleri/Tham Danda route or coordinating group helicopter shuttles from Phaplu. Because we operate our own vehicles and field crew, our guests bypass gridlock and preserve their trekking schedules without losing valuable acclimatization days.

Booking Essentials and Changes to Your Plans

We ask for a reservation deposit to secure your trip. Contact our Kathmandu team for the deposit amount, payment instructions, balance deadline, and cancellation conditions for your chosen itinerary before making a payment.

For Nepal treks, the remaining balance is payable on arrival in Kathmandu. We accept credit card, bank transfer, or cash; ask about the accepted currency and any payment charges when arranging your booking.

If illness or flight changes affect your plans, contact us to discuss your booking and the options for a later trip or a transfer to another traveler. Ask for the written deposit-credit, transfer, cancellation, and refund conditions that apply to your booking.

If a Lukla flight or another connection is disrupted, we discuss the available alternatives and their implications for your route, time, and costs.
