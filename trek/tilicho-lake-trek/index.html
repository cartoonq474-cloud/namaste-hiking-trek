Annapurna Circuit Trek with Tilicho Lake (16 Days)

A legendary 16-day high-altitude journey combining the classic Annapurna Circuit with an unforgettable excursion to turquoise Tilicho Lake (4,919m), crossing Thorong La Pass (5,416m), and scenic flights from Jomsom to Pokhara and Kathmandu.

The Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek weaves two of Nepal's most revered mountain adventures into one continuous 16-day Himalayan voyage. Trek from the lush subtropical valleys of the lower Marsyangdi through traditional Gurung and Tibetan-influenced Manangi settlements, make a dedicated ascent to the turquoise waters of Tilicho Lake (4,919m) under the Annapurna Great Barrier, and conquer the iconic Thorong La Pass at 5,416 meters before descending into the trans-Himalayan desert of Mustang and holy Muktinath. Unlike itineraries with long bumpy return drives, this 16-day program includes panoramic domestic flights from Jomsom to Pokhara through the Kali Gandaki gorge, and from Pokhara to Kathmandu. This ensures maximum trail time, superior acclimatization, and smooth air returns.

Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek Overview

The Annapurna Circuit Trek with Tilicho Lake (16 Days) represents the ultimate mountain odyssey in the Nepal Himalaya. Combining the world-renowned full circuit around the Annapurna massif with an extraordinary excursion to turquoise Tilicho Lake at 4,919 m (16,138 ft)—one of the highest accessible alpine lakes on Earth—this itinerary delivers unmatched geographic, ecological, and cultural variety.

Beginning in the lush subtropical valleys of the lower Marsyangdi River, the trail winds upward through terraced rice paddies, cascading waterfalls, and fragrant pine and rhododendron forests. Beyond Chame and Upper Pisang, the terrain opens into the wide high-altitude plateau of Manang, where ancient Tibetan-Buddhist gompas and spinning prayer wheels frame the towering peaks of Annapurna II, III, IV, and Gangapurna.

From Manang, the route makes a dedicated westward divergence into the wild Khangsar Valley, reaching the remote staging settlement of Tilicho Base Camp. A pre-dawn ascent reveals the frozen or shimmering turquoise waters of Tilicho Lake resting dramatically beneath the towering 7,134-meter north face of Tilicho Peak and the Annapurna Great Barrier. Trekkers then rejoin the main circuit at Yak Kharka and cross the legendary Thorong La Pass at 5,416 m (17,769 ft) before descending into the sacred Hindu and Buddhist sanctuary of Muktinath and the wind-swept Kali Gandaki valley.

Annapurna Circuit Trek with Tilicho Lake 16 Days Itinerary

Our comprehensive 16-day Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake itinerary follows an optimal, medically sound acclimatization progression. Featuring Kathmandu arrival, a scenic drive to Jagat along the Marsyangdi River, gradual pedestrian ascent through Chame and Upper Pisang, crucial acclimatization in Manang, a high-altitude detour to turquoise Tilicho Lake (4,919 m), a triumphant crossing of Thorong La Pass (5,416 m), exploration of holy Muktinath, and two included domestic flights (Jomsom to Pokhara, and Pokhara to Kathmandu).

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu

1,355 m / 4,445 ft

Hiking / Travel: 20-25 minutes
Distance: 6 km (Transfer)
Lodging: Hotel
Meals: Breakfast Included
Highlight: Airport greeting & private hotel transfer • Expedition briefing with lead guide • Gear check in Thamel • Welcome dinner with cultural performance

Your Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek begins with your arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport in Kathmandu, located at approximately 1,355 m.

A Namaste Hiking Trek representative receives you at the airport and coordinates your transfer to the hotel. After an international flight, this first day is intentionally kept light so you have time to settle into your accommodation, recover from travel, and prepare for the mountain journey ahead.

Once at the hotel, you can organize your luggage and separate the belongings that will be required during the trek from items that can remain in storage. This is also a useful opportunity to check your trekking boots, clothing layers, personal medication, documents, and other essential equipment.

The evening can include a short group meeting and introduction to the trekking guide and other members of the group. The guide can provide an initial overview of the 16-day journey, including the route through the Marsyangdi Valley, the Manang acclimatization day, Tilicho Lake, Thorong La Pass, Muktinath, and Jomsom.

Travelers can also use the evening to become familiar with the Kathmandu environment. A short walk through Thamel can provide an introduction to the city's restaurants, shops, and trekking atmosphere before the journey moves toward the mountains.

This is not a strenuous day, and no trekking is required. The main objective is to arrive comfortably, organize your preparation, and rest before the long journey toward Jagat the following morning.

Day 2: Drive from Kathmandu to Jagat via Besisahar

1,300 m / 4,265 ft

Hiking / Travel: 7-8 hours
Distance: 286 km (7–8 hrs drive)
Lodging: Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
Highlight: Scenic Prithvi Highway drive along Trishuli river • Besisahar gateway to Annapurna Circuit • Entering Marsyangdi River valley • Overnight in Jagat teahouse

The second day begins the journey toward the Annapurna region with a long overland drive from Kathmandu to Jagat via Besisahar.

The route covers approximately 286 km and normally takes around 7 to 8 hours, although actual travel time can vary because of traffic, road conditions, weather, and local circumstances.

As the journey leaves Kathmandu, the landscape gradually changes from the urban Kathmandu Valley toward the middle hills and river valleys of central Nepal. The road provides a visual introduction to the environments that will become more important during the trekking section.

Besisahar serves as an important gateway toward the Annapurna Circuit. From there, the journey continues toward Jagat and the Marsyangdi River valley.

The landscape becomes progressively greener as the road enters the mountain environment. River valleys, terraced hillsides, villages, forested slopes, and waterfalls begin to replace the urban surroundings.

Although this is a road day rather than a trekking day, it plays an important role in the itinerary. It allows the trekking journey to start from a relatively low elevation while gradually moving toward the higher Annapurna region.

Jagat is located at approximately 1,300 m, making it a useful starting point for the gradual ascent ahead.

Upon arrival, you settle into the local accommodation, enjoy a meal, and prepare for the first full trekking day.

The evening is also a good time to make sure your daypack is organized with water, rain protection, snacks, warm layers, and other items you may need during the trail.

Day 3: Trek From Jagat to Dharapani

1,960 m / 6,430 ft

Hiking / Travel: 5 hours
Distance: 12 km (5 hrs walk)
Lodging: Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
Highlight: Rocky trails through Chamje and Tal village • First village in Manang district • Crossing Marsyangdi suspension bridges • Arrival in Dharapani

The actual trekking journey begins on Day 3 as the route follows the Marsyangdi River valley from Jagat toward Dharapani.

The day's walking distance is approximately 12 km and takes around 5 hours under normal conditions.

The landscape during this section remains relatively green compared with the higher stages of the trek. Forests, agricultural terraces, mountain streams, river crossings, waterfalls, and traditional villages form much of the scenery.

The trail follows the changing contours of the valley, with a mixture of gradual climbs, descents, stone paths, suspension bridges, and uneven mountain terrain.

This first trekking day provides an introduction to the rhythm of the Annapurna Circuit. Rather than immediately climbing into high altitude, the route progresses gradually while giving trekkers time to adapt to repeated mountain walking.

The villages along the way provide opportunities to observe local architecture, agricultural practices, and everyday life. This section also gives travelers their first sustained experience of walking through a Himalayan river valley.

As the route approaches Dharapani, the surrounding mountains become increasingly prominent.

Dharapani sits at approximately 1,960 m, representing a significant increase from Jagat but still well below the highest parts of the itinerary.

After reaching the village, trekkers can settle into the teahouse, recover, eat dinner, and prepare for the longer uphill stages ahead.

Good habits established on this first trekking day, particularly controlled pacing, regular hydration, and careful foot management, can make the remaining trek more comfortable.

Day 4: Trek From Dharapani to Chame

2,630 m / 8,628 ft

Hiking / Travel: 6 hours
Distance: 15 km (6 hrs walk)
Lodging: Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
Highlight: Climb up to Timang village with Manaslu views • Walking through pine, fir and rhododendron forests • Administrative center of Chame • Natural hot spring views along river

Day 4 continues the gradual ascent from Dharapani to Chame.

The route covers approximately 15 km and generally takes around 6 hours.

The landscape changes noticeably as the altitude increases. The lower valley environment gradually gives way to cooler mountain forests and more open terrain.

The trail passes through settlements and forest sections while following the Marsyangdi Valley deeper into the Annapurna region.

Mountain views become increasingly impressive as the surrounding peaks rise above the valley.

The route contains uphill sections as well as descents and uneven surfaces, so travelers should continue maintaining a steady pace rather than attempting to complete the day too quickly.

Chame sits at approximately 2,630 m and is one of the major settlements on the Annapurna Circuit.

The village provides useful trekking facilities and a comfortable stopping point before the route moves to higher elevations.

As altitude increases, trekkers may begin to notice that uphill sections require more controlled breathing and slower movement. This is normal and is one reason the itinerary uses gradual elevation gain.

After arriving in Chame, travelers can rest, enjoy a warm meal, organize their equipment, and prepare for the next day's trek toward Pisang.

Day 5: Trek From Chame to Pissang

3,300 m / 10,826 ft

Hiking / Travel: 6 hours
Distance: 13.9 km (6 hrs walk)
Lodging: Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
Highlight: Curving rock wall of Paungda Danda (Swarga Dwar) • Entering Buddhist cultural zone of Upper Pisang • Spectacular views of Annapurna II and IV • Traditional stone-walled village & gompa

The trek from Chame to Pisang is another major stage in the gradual progression toward higher elevation.

The route covers approximately 13.9 km and takes around 6 hours.

As the trail climbs, the surrounding landscape becomes cooler and more distinctly Himalayan.

Forested sections give way to more open mountain terrain, while the views toward the Annapurna range become increasingly expansive.

The route follows the Marsyangdi Valley through changing terrain and traditional settlements.

Pisang is located at approximately 3,300 m, making this an important point in the altitude progression.

Travelers are now entering an environment where hydration, nutrition, pace, and rest become increasingly important.

The higher elevation can make familiar physical activity feel more demanding. Rather than compensating by walking faster during easier sections, travelers should maintain an even rhythm throughout the day.

Pisang is also notable for its traditional settlement environment and mountain scenery.

The village provides an excellent opportunity to observe the transition between the lower valley communities and the more distinct high-altitude culture that becomes increasingly visible around Manang.

After arriving at the teahouse, the evening should focus on recovery and preparation for the following day's longer journey.

Day 6: Trek From Upper Pissang to Manang

3,500 m / 11,482 ft

Hiking / Travel: 6.5 hours
Distance: 16 km (6.5 hrs walk)
Lodging: Guesthouse (Private Bathroom)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
Highlight: High balcony trail via Ghyaru and Ngawal • Panoramic vistas of the entire Annapurna range • Centuries-old Buddhist monasteries and stupas • Arrival in the historic trading hub of Manang

Day 6 takes the route deeper into the Annapurna Circuit toward Manang.

The day's distance is approximately 16 km, with a walking time of around 6.5 hours.

The route provides some of the most impressive scenery of the middle section of the circuit.

The mountain environment becomes increasingly open, dry, and expansive as the trail climbs.

The higher route around Pisang and the approach toward Manang provide opportunities for wide mountain views and traditional Himalayan landscapes.

As the trek continues toward Manang, the surrounding peaks become increasingly dominant.

Manang sits at approximately 3,500 m and is one of the most important destinations on the entire itinerary.

It is not only a major trekking settlement but also the principal acclimatization point before the route continues toward Tilicho Lake and the higher stages of the Annapurna Circuit.

The increase from Pisang to Manang is moderate in terms of altitude, but the walking distance makes the day substantial.

Arriving in Manang marks the end of the main continuous ascent from the lower Marsyangdi Valley.

The following day provides an important opportunity to slow down, explore, and adapt to the elevation.

Day 7: Manang Exploration and Acclimatization Day

3,500 m – 3,800 m / 11,482 ft – 12,467 ft

Hiking / Travel: 2-3 hours
Distance: 4–6 km (Hike)
Lodging: Guesthouse (Private Bathroom)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
Highlight: Active acclimatization hike to turquoise Gangapurna Lake • Chongkor viewpoint with glacier and avalanche views • Himalayan Rescue Association (HRA) altitude lecture • Rest and hydration in ancient Manang

Day 7 is intentionally different from the surrounding trekking days.

Instead of continuing immediately toward greater elevation, the itinerary includes an exploration and acclimatization day in Manang.

At approximately 3,500 m, Manang provides a suitable environment for spending additional time at elevation before the route moves toward Tilicho Base Camp, Tilicho Lake, and eventually Thorong La.

The day can be used for short walks around the village and surrounding landscape rather than complete inactivity.

Light activity allows travelers to remain mobile while avoiding the unnecessary exertion of a full trekking day.

The guide can recommend suitable walking options based on weather, group condition, and individual fitness.

Manang also provides an excellent cultural experience.

Travelers can explore traditional stone-built structures, Buddhist cultural features, agricultural areas, prayer flags, monasteries, and the distinctive architecture of the high Himalayan settlements.

The village is surrounded by dramatic mountain scenery, including views toward the higher peaks and valleys of the Annapurna region.

The acclimatization day is also an ideal time to evaluate how the body is responding to the altitude.

Travelers should pay attention to hydration, appetite, sleep quality, headache, unusual fatigue, dizziness, nausea, or other symptoms.

The guide can monitor the group and provide practical advice.

The day also provides an opportunity to prepare for the Tilicho section.

Travelers should organize warm clothing, water, snacks, trekking poles, headlamps, and other essential equipment before moving toward the more exposed high-altitude environment.

Day 8: Trek From Manang to Tilicho Base Camp through Khangsar Village

4,150 m / 13,615 ft

Hiking / Travel: 5-6 hours
Distance: 19.1 km (5–6 hrs walk)
Lodging: Guesthouse (Private Bathroom)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
Highlight: Branching off the classic circuit toward Khangsar village • Stunning views of Tilicho Peak and the Great Barrier • Traversing steep alpine scree slopes with caution • Arrival at isolated Tilicho Base Camp lodge

Day 8 begins the dedicated Tilicho Lake section.

The route leaves the main Annapurna Circuit around Manang and continues toward Khangsar Village before ascending toward Tilicho Base Camp.

The total distance is approximately 19.1 km, making this one of the longer and more demanding days of the itinerary. Walking generally takes around 7 to 8 hours.

The landscape changes significantly as the route moves farther from the main circuit.

Vegetation becomes increasingly sparse, while the terrain becomes more rugged and exposed.

Khangsar provides an important cultural stop before the route enters the higher environment leading toward Tilicho.

The trail includes uneven and rocky sections, and careful footing is required.

As the elevation increases toward approximately 4,150 m, the reduced oxygen availability becomes more noticeable.

This is where the acclimatization day in Manang becomes especially important.

Travelers should maintain a slow and steady pace, drink regularly, eat properly, and communicate with the guide if they experience unusual symptoms.

The objective is to reach Tilicho Base Camp without exhausting the body before the following day's high-altitude lake excursion.

Tilicho Base Camp provides the overnight stop before the journey to Tilicho Lake.

The surrounding landscape is markedly different from the green lower Annapurna valleys. The mountains are more barren, the air colder, and the environment more exposed.

After arriving, trekkers should focus on hydration, food, warmth, and preparation for the following day's climb.

Day 9: Hike to Tilicho Lake and Return to Tilicho Base Camp, then Trek to Shree Kharka

4,919 m / 16,138 ft (Lake) → 4,060 m / 13,320 ft (Siri Kharka)

Distance: 15 km (6–7 hrs walk)

Highlight: Early morning 4:30 AM ascent beneath starry Himalayan skies • Reaching turquoise Tilicho Lake (4,919m) beneath Great Barrier • Unforgettable photos and hot tea by the glacial waters • Descent back to base camp and onward to Siri Kharka

Day 9 is one of the major highlights of the entire Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek.

The day's objective is to reach Tilicho Lake at approximately 4,919 m before returning toward the lower section of the route and continuing to Siri Kharka.

The complete day covers approximately 15 km and generally takes around 6 to 7 hours.

The route begins from Tilicho Base Camp and climbs through a high-altitude environment characterized by rugged slopes, exposed terrain, sparse vegetation, and dramatic mountain surroundings.

The ascent is demanding because the trail is already at high elevation.

The cold and reduced oxygen availability mean that trekkers should maintain a deliberate pace.

The approach toward the lake is a major physical achievement.

As the lake comes into view, the landscape changes into a striking high-altitude setting surrounded by massive mountain slopes.

Weather conditions influence visibility and the appearance of the lake. On a clear day, the combination of blue water, surrounding rock, snow, and mountain peaks can create an extraordinary panorama.

After spending appropriate time at the lake, the route begins the return journey.

The descent requires careful foot placement, particularly on rocky or uneven sections.

The day continues toward Siri Kharka, reducing the elevation after the high point at Tilicho.

This descent is important because the itinerary still has the main Annapurna Circuit ahead, including the Thorong La crossing.

Travelers should resist the temptation to spend excessive energy after reaching the lake.

The return section should be approached as part of a complete trekking day, with sufficient water, food, and controlled pacing.

Day 10: Trek from Siri Kharka to Yak Kharka

4,050 m / 13,287 ft

Distance: 11.1 km (5 hrs walk)

After completing the Tilicho excursion, Day 10 reconnects the journey with the main Annapurna Circuit.

The route from Siri Kharka to Yak Kharka covers approximately 11.1 km and generally takes around 5 hours.

The landscape remains strongly alpine, with sparse vegetation, rocky slopes, broad mountain views, and an increasingly exposed environment.

This day is shorter than the previous lake excursion, allowing the body some recovery before the approach to Thorong Phedi.

The elevation remains high, so trekkers should continue to take altitude precautions.

Yak Kharka is approximately 4,050 m, placing the group firmly within the high-altitude section of the circuit.

The day's walking is not simply about distance. At this elevation, even moderate climbs can require greater effort.

A controlled pace and regular rest breaks can help preserve energy.

The route provides continued opportunities to observe the high Himalayan environment and the changing relationship between vegetation and elevation.

After arriving at Yak Kharka, the afternoon can be used for rest, hydration, food, and preparation for the next day's walk toward Thorong Phedi.

Travelers should check their cold-weather layers and make sure essential items are accessible.

The following day brings another increase in altitude before the major Thorong La crossing.

Lodging: Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
Highlight: Gradual ascent across wooden suspension bridge • Reaching Thorong Phedi base staging settlement • Carbohydrate loading and early evening rest • Briefing for the 4:00 AM Thorong La summit crossing

Day 11 brings the trek closer to the highest point of the entire itinerary.

The route from Yak Kharka to Thorong Phedi at approximately 4,600 m covers around 8.5 km and takes approximately 4.5 hours.

Although this is shorter in distance than several earlier trekking days, the high elevation makes the walk demanding.

The landscape is open, rugged, and alpine.

Vegetation is limited, and the surrounding mountains dominate the scenery.

The trail continues to gain altitude toward Thorong Phedi, which serves as the main staging point before the Thorong La crossing.

Arriving at Thorong Phedi is a significant milestone.

The group is now positioned close to the highest point of the trek, and the following morning will involve the major ascent toward Thorong La at 5,416 m.

The afternoon and evening should be used for rest rather than unnecessary physical activity.

Trekkers can prepare their clothing, headlamp, water, snacks, gloves, warm layers, and other essentials for the early start.

The guide also provides practical information about the next day's route, expected walking duration, weather considerations, and the descent toward Muktinath.

Because the next day is the most demanding of the trek, conserving energy at Thorong Phedi is particularly important.

Day 12: Trek From Thorong Phedi to Muktinath via Thorong-La Pass

5,416 m / 17,769 ft (Pass) → 3,710 m / 12,171 ft (Muktinath)

Hiking / Travel: 8 hours
Distance: 16.4 km (8 hrs walk)
Lodging: Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
Highlight: Pre-dawn headlamp climb up switchbacks to Thorong High Camp • Reaching Thorong La Pass crest (5,416m) with prayer flags • Dramatic views into Mustang and the Dhaulagiri range • Long 1,600m descent to the holy shrine of Muktinath

Day 12 is the defining trekking day of the Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake itinerary.

The route climbs from Thorong Phedi to Thorong La Pass at approximately 5,416 m before descending all the way toward Muktinath at approximately 3,710 m.

The day's total distance is around 16.4 km, with approximately 8 hours of walking.

The day generally begins early.

Starting early provides sufficient daylight for the full crossing and helps the group approach the pass before conditions become more challenging later in the day.

The initial ascent is steady and demanding.

At this elevation, oxygen availability is significantly lower than at the beginning of the trek. Even physically fit trekkers may find the pace slow.

The guide manages the group at a sustainable rhythm, allowing appropriate breaks and encouraging travelers to pay attention to their physical condition.

As the trail continues upward, the surrounding environment becomes increasingly exposed.

Cold temperatures and wind can make the pass particularly demanding, especially during the early morning.

Eventually, the route reaches the summit of Thorong La at 5,416 m.

Standing at the top is one of the major accomplishments of the journey.

Travelers can spend a practical amount of time taking photographs and appreciating the surrounding landscape before beginning the descent.

The descent toward Muktinath is long and significant.

It also produces one of the trek's most dramatic environmental transitions.

The landscape gradually becomes drier, warmer, and more open as the route moves away from the highest part of the Annapurna range.

Muktinath is both a geographical and cultural destination.

The area is an important pilgrimage site for Hindu and Buddhist traditions, and the transition from high-altitude pass to religious and cultural landscape creates a memorable end to the day's journey.

Reaching Muktinath after crossing Thorong La provides a strong sense of completion.

Day 13: Trek From Muktinath to Jomsom

2,720 m / 8,923 ft

Hiking / Travel: 6 hours
Distance: 20.2 km (6 hrs walk)
Lodging: Guesthouse (Private Bathroom)
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
Highlight: Morning visit to sacred Muktinath temple with 108 water spouts • Walking down into the arid trans-Himalayan Kali Gandaki valley • Historic medieval town of Kagbeni and Mustang gateway • Afternoon arrival in the windy mountain airfield hub of Jomsom

After the demanding Thorong La crossing, Day 13 continues the descent from Muktinath toward Jomsom.

The route covers approximately 20.2 km and takes around 6 hours.

The landscape is now very different from the forested lower portions of the Annapurna Circuit.

The route enters the dry environment of the Kali Gandaki Valley and Lower Mustang.

The terrain is more arid, with exposed slopes, rock formations, broad valleys, and strong mountain winds in some sections.

Key Trip Facts & Modern Route Architecture

Our classic Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek is a 16-calendar-day journey crafted for optimal acclimatization, cultural immersion, and wilderness appreciation. Driving past Besisahar to Jagat (1,300 m), trekkers walk on foot through the lower subtropical gorge and pine forests of Tal and Dharapani to Chame, giving your physiology three gentle days to adjust before crossing 3,000 meters.

The standard walking route traverses: Jagat → Dharapani → Chame → Upper Pisang → Ghyaru & Ngawal (high balcony route) → Manang (vital acclimatization) → Yak Kharka → Thorong Phedi / High Camp → Thorong La Pass (5,416 m) → Muktinath. The journey then continues through medieval Kagbeni and the Kali Gandaki riverbed to Jomsom, followed by an aerial mountain flight slicing between Annapurna I and Dhaulagiri directly to Pokhara, before returning to Kathmandu with a dedicated contingency buffer day.

Geographical Structure: Marsyangdi to Kali Gandaki

The geographical genius of the Annapurna Circuit is that it connects two major Himalayan river systems through one continuous high-altitude pass. The Marsyangdi River provides the eastward approach, carving deep through green foothills and dramatic rock walls before broadening into the alpine amphitheater of Manang.

After crossing the high divide at Thorong La Pass (5,416 m), the trail plunges into the arid Kali Gandaki river canyon—the deepest gorge on earth, flanked by the 8,000-meter giants of Dhaulagiri (8,167 m) to the west and Annapurna I (8,091 m) to the east. You witness an entire mountain massif from multiple cardinal directions within a single continuous journey.

What the Trek is Really Like: Daily Rhythm & Terrain

A typical trekking day begins between 6:00 and 7:00 AM. After a hearty breakfast of porridge, eggs, or Tibetan bread with hot tea, you pack your daypack with warm layers, water, and snacks while porters transport the main luggage. You hike for 3 to 4 hours in the crisp morning air, stop at a trailside teahouse for a freshly cooked lunch (often hot dal bhat or noodle soup), and continue 2 to 3 hours to the afternoon destination.

Expect 5 to 7 hours of daily walking on established dirt paths, stone steps, rocky moraines, and occasional suspension bridges. Terrain changes daily: subtropical farmland near Chame, pine forests to Pisang, high cliffside paths through Ghyaru, barren scree slopes up to Thorong Phedi, snowy switchbacks across the pass, and wide river cobblestones in the Kali Gandaki.

Thorong La Pass Crossing: 5,416 m Strategy & Preparation

At 5,416 m (17,769 ft), Thorong La Pass is the highest point of the circuit. The pass day is a true commitment day that begins around 4:00 AM in sub-zero darkness. Headlamps illuminate the steep switchbacks from Thorong Phedi (4,450 m) or High Camp (4,925 m). At this altitude, oxygen pressure is roughly half that at sea level, requiring a slow, rhythmic, synchronized breathing pace.

Reaching the pass marker adorned with colorful prayer flags provides an immense sense of accomplishment. However, the day is only half done: you must descend 1,600 vertical meters over loose rock and scree to Muktinath (3,800 m). Trekking poles and sturdy ankle support are essential to protect knees and quadriceps during this prolonged descent.

Manang Acclimatization Strategy (Day 5 Deep Dive)

Manang sits at approximately 3,500 m and represents the critical physiological bridge of the circuit. Skipping this acclimatization day is the single most common cause of altitude failure at Thorong La. The human body requires 24 to 48 hours to increase red blood cell production and adapt to lower barometric pressure.

On Day 5, we practice the proven 'climb high, sleep low' rule. You make an active daytime hike to Chongkor viewpoint, Gangapurna glacial lake, or the cliffside Praken Gompa (~3,945 m) to expose your body to higher elevations before returning to sleep at 3,500 m. In the afternoon, visit the Himalayan Rescue Association (HRA) clinic for their daily educational altitude lecture.

High Ridge Route: Upper Pisang, Ghyaru & Ngawal

Between Pisang and Manang, trekkers can choose between a lower valley path or the magnificent high ridge route via Upper Pisang, Ghyaru (3,670 m), and Ngawal (3,650 m). While the high route involves a steep 400-meter zigzag ascent from the river, it is widely considered the finest walking day in Nepal.

From the ridge, you gaze straight across at the massive glaciated faces of Annapurna II (7,937 m), Annapurna IV (7,525 m), and Annapurna III (7,555 m). The historic stone villages of Ghyaru and Ngawal showcase medieval Tibetan architecture, ancient chortens, and wind-sculpted prayer flags, providing superior natural acclimatization ahead of Manang.

Muktinath: Sacred Pilgrimage & Temple Complex

Sitting at 3,800 m on the western flank of Thorong La, Muktinath ('Place of Liberation') is one of the Himalaya's most sacred pilgrimage sanctuaries, venerated for millennia by both Hindus and Buddhists. For Hindus, Muktinath is one of the 108 Divya Desams dedicated to Lord Vishnu, featuring 108 sacred brass water spouts shaped like bull heads where pilgrims bathe to cleanse spiritual sins.

For Tibetan Buddhists, the site is known as Chumig Gyatsa ('Hundred Springs') and is sanctified by Guru Rinpoche (Padmasambhava). Nearby stands the Jwala Mai temple, where an eternal blue natural gas flame burns continuously over a sacred underground water spring, symbolizing the mystical unity of earth, water, and fire.

Kagbeni: Medieval Gateway to Upper Mustang

Perched on the confluence of the Kali Gandaki and Jhong rivers at 2,800 m, Kagbeni is a living medieval fortress town that feels frozen in time. Its tightly packed, whitewashed mud-brick houses, covered tunnels, and intricate alleyways were designed to withstand fierce valley winds and ancient invaders.

Kagbeni marks the official southern boundary of the ancient Kingdom of Lo (Upper Mustang). Just past the village police checkpoint, a restricted-area permit is required to continue north to Lo Manthang. Exploring Kagbeni's 500-year-old red Kag Chode Thupten Sampheling Gompa and watching apple trees grow against barren desert cliffs is an unforgettable cultural contrast.

Thakali Culture & Marpha Apple Orchards

South of Jomsom lies Marpha, widely hailed as the most picturesque village in the Kali Gandaki. Marpha is the cultural heartland of the Thakali people, an indigenous community who historically dominated the trans-Himalayan salt and grain trade routes between Tibet and India.

Marpha is famed for its spotless flagstone streets, flat earthen roofs stocked with firewood, and bountiful apple orchards introduced by Japanese botanist Ekai Kawaguchi and local horticultural projects. Trekkers can indulge in fresh apple pies, locally distilled apple brandy (Marpha Marpha), apple jams, and authentic Thakali dal bhat—universally considered the most flavorful meal in the Himalaya.

Tatopani Natural Hot Springs & Deep Descent

Descending from Jomsom through Tukuche and Ghasa brings you to Tatopani at 1,190 m—a staggering 4,200 vertical meters below the summit of Thorong La. The sudden return of dense subtropical foliage, banana trees, flowering poinsettias, and humid, oxygen-rich air creates profound physical rejuvenation.

Tatopani ('hot water' in Nepali) is famous for its natural geothermal mineral pools situated right beside the rushing Kali Gandaki River. Soaking in these soothing 38°C to 42°C sulfur-rich thermal waters relieves tired leg muscles, eases joint tension, and marks a celebratory milestone after completing the cold high pass.

Ghorepani Rhododendron Forests & Poon Hill Sunrise

The climb from Tatopani up to Ghorepani (2,860 m) represents the circuit's second major mountain rise, passing through the world's largest natural rhododendron forest. In springtime (March and April), entire hillsides bloom in breathtaking crimson, pink, and white blossoms against snow-capped peaks.

From Ghorepani, a pre-dawn climb leads to Poon Hill (3,210 m / 10,531 ft). Standing at the summit tower as the sun rises over Dhaulagiri (8,167 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m), Annapurna South (7,219 m), and the iconic sacred peak of Machhapuchhre (Fishtail) provides the quintessential panoramic climax of your Himalayan journey.

Cultural Diversity: Gurung, Manangi, Thakali & Salt Routes

The Annapurna Circuit traverses four distinct ethno-cultural zones within 16 days. In the lower valleys around Chame and Dharapani, you encounter Gurung and Magar communities rooted in agriculture, terraced farming, and deep martial traditions in the famed British and Indian Gurkha regiments.

Ascending toward Manang, the culture shifts to Manangi (Nyeshang) communities closely tied to Tibetan Buddhism, trans-Himalayan commerce, and seasonal trade. West of Thorong La, Muktinath unites Hindu pilgrims and Bhotia Buddhists, while the Kali Gandaki Valley is home to the Thakali people, historic masters of mountain hospitality, caravan inns, and regional culinary arts.

Ecological Traverse: Subtropical to Rain Shadow Desert

Few trails on earth showcase such dramatic ecological diversity in two weeks. Starting in the subtropical river gorges (bamboo, pine, terraced rice paddies), you ascend into temperate oak and blue pine forests around Chame, transitioning into subalpine birch, juniper, and larch forests near Pisang.

Above 3,500 m, the landscape crosses into the trans-Himalayan rain shadow of Manang and Mustang—an arid alpine desert sheltered from the Indian monsoon by the Annapurna wall. After traversing the alpine tundra of Thorong La (5,416 m), you descend into barren lunar canyons before climbing back into lush cloud forests of rhododendron and bamboo around Ghorepani.

Physical Training & Preparation (Endurance, Legs, Downhill)

The Annapurna Circuit requires robust aerobic endurance, muscular stamina, and joint resilience. We recommend a structured 8 to 12-week training regimen prior to departure. Focus on three core pillars:

1. Cardiovascular Endurance: 45 to 60 minutes of brisk hiking, stair climbing, trail running, or cycling 3 to 4 times per week.
2. Leg & Core Strength: Squats, lunges, step-ups with a weighted backpack, and planks to prepare muscles for 6 to 8 hours of continuous load.
3. Eccentric Downhill Conditioning: Descending Thorong La drops 1,600 vertical meters in one morning. Practice downhill stair walking and weighted step-downs to build knee stability and quadriceps endurance.

Altitude Management & AMS Prevention (CDC & NTB Guidelines)

Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) can affect anyone above 2,500 m regardless of physical fitness or age. Both the CDC and Nepal Tourism Board emphasize gradual sleeping ascent: limiting net sleeping elevation gains to 300–500 m per day above 3,000 m and incorporating a mandatory rest day every 1,000 m.

Our guides carry certified pulse oximeters to measure your blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) and heart rate twice daily (morning and evening). Maintain 3 to 4 liters of fluid intake daily, avoid alcohol and sleeping pills, climb at an easy conversational pace ('bistari, bistari'), and report any mild headaches or nausea immediately.

Teahouse Accommodation & High Mountain Food Realities

Accommodations along the Annapurna Circuit are family-operated mountain teahouses and lodges. Rooms typically feature twin beds with foam mattresses, clean sheets, and blankets (bringing your own -10°C to -15°C sleeping bag is essential for high elevations like Yak Kharka and Thorong Phedi). Lower village lodges in Chame, Manang, and Jomsom often offer private attached bathrooms and Wi-Fi, while high-altitude stops use shared facilities.

Meals are freshly prepared using local ingredients. The staple fuel is Nepali Dal Bhat (steamed rice, spiced lentil soup, seasonal vegetable curry, spinach, and pickles), which includes free refills. Menus also feature Tibetan momos, fried noodles, porridge, vegetable soups, garlic soup (renowned for altitude acclimatization), and Western options like pasta, pancakes, and apple pie.

Frequently Asked Questions: Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake (16 Days)

General Information

How long is this Annapurna Circuit Trek package?

∨
This package is 16 calendar days from Kathmandu arrival to final international departure. Days 3–12 encompass the mountain walking journey from Jagat through Manang and across Thorong La Pass (5,416 m) to Muktinath and Jomsom. Day 2 features dedicated UNESCO cultural sightseeing in Kathmandu, Day 13 includes the scenic mountain flight to Pokhara, and Day 15 provides an essential safety buffer in Kathmandu against mountain weather delays.

How high are Thorong La Pass and Manang?

∨
Thorong La Pass stands at 5,416 m (17,769 ft), making it the highest point and physical pinnacle of the Annapurna Circuit. Manang sits at approximately 3,500 m (11,482 ft) and serves as the vital acclimatization base. The highest overnight stop is Thorong Phedi (~4,450 m) or an individually assessed High Camp (~4,925 m).

What is the best season to trek the Annapurna Circuit?

∨
The two optimal seasons are Autumn (September to November) and Spring (March to May). Autumn offers exceptionally clear skies, stable dry weather, and crisp Himalayan panoramas after the monsoon. Spring brings warmer daytime temperatures, longer daylight, and magnificent blooming rhododendron forests between Tatopani and Ghorepani.

How does the Annapurna Circuit compare to Everest Base Camp?

∨
While Everest Base Camp follows a single valley to a mountain foot and returns along the same route, the Annapurna Circuit is a complete circumnavigation of an entire mountain massif. It connects two contrasting river systems (Marsyangdi and Kali Gandaki), crosses a 5,416-meter pass, and provides unmatched ecological diversity—from subtropical valleys to the Tibetan-style rain shadow desert.

What permits are required for the Annapurna Circuit?

∨
Trekkers require two official permits: the Annapurna Conservation Area Project (ACAP) Permit and the Trekkers' Information Management System (TIMS) Card. As per Nepal Tourism Board regulations, foreign trekkers must be accompanied by a certified licensed trekking guide. All permits and official paperwork are fully organized and included in your booking.

How do we travel from Kathmandu to the starting trail?

∨
We provide private 4WD overland transportation from Kathmandu directly to Chame (2,710 m) on Day 2. Driving past the lower road-affected sections preserves valuable vacation time and physical stamina for the pristine alpine sections between Pisang, Manang, and Thorong La.

What are the luggage limits for domestic transport and porters?

∨
Porters carry up to 15 kg (33 lbs) of main luggage per trekker in a waterproof duffel bag provided by us. You only carry a light daypack (25–35 liters) with daily water, warm layers, camera, sun protection, and personal valuables.

How do we return to Kathmandu at the end of the trek?

∨
On Day 12, after watching sunrise from Poon Hill, we descend to Nayapul and take a private vehicle to Pokhara. On Day 13, you travel from Pokhara to Kathmandu via a scenic 25-minute flight or deluxe tourist coach, as confirmed in your package schedule.

What is accommodation like along the Annapurna Circuit?

∨
Accommodation is in family-run mountain teahouses and lodges. Rooms are clean, twin-sharing with foam mattresses, pillows, and blankets. In lower villages like Chame, Manang, and Jomsom, rooms often feature private attached bathrooms. High-altitude stops (Yak Kharka and Thorong Phedi) offer simpler communal facilities. Bringing a 4-season (-10°C to -15°C) sleeping bag is essential.

What meals are served during the trek?

∨
Full board (Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner) with hot tea or coffee is included during all trekking days. Menus feature authentic Nepali Dal Bhat (steamed rice, spiced lentil soup, seasonal vegetable curry, greens, and pickles with complimentary refills), Tibetan momos, fried noodles, garlic soups, porridge, pancakes, and famous Thakali apple pies in Marpha.

Are hot showers and Wi-Fi available at teahouses?

∨
Hot gas or solar showers ($2–$4) and Wi-Fi ($2–$3) are available at most teahouses up to Manang and from Muktinath southward. Connectivity is naturally limited or unavailable at Thorong Phedi and High Camp due to remote high-altitude geography.

How is safe drinking water managed along the route?

∨
Safe drinking water is essential. Teahouses sell boiled drinking water, and ACAP community water stations provide safe filtered water. To minimize single-use plastic waste, we recommend carrying two 1-liter reusable water bottles with chlorine dioxide water purification drops or a Sawyer Squeeze filter.

How do we prevent Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS)?

∨
By following our progressive counterclockwise ascent curve and mandatory Day 5 acclimatization rest in Manang (~3,500 m). Trekkers climb high during day hikes and return to sleep lower, maintaining 3–4 liters of daily hydration, eating high-carbohydrate meals, and ascending at an easy conversational pace ('bistari, bistari').

Do your guides carry safety equipment and medical supplies?

∨
Yes. All our lead guides carry certified digital pulse oximeters to check blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) and pulse twice daily (morning and evening). Guides are certified in wilderness first aid and carry comprehensive medical kits.

What happens in case of an emergency evacuation?

∨
In the rare event of severe altitude illness or traumatic injury, our operations base in Kathmandu coordinates rapid emergency helicopter rescue to specialized medical facilities in Pokhara or Kathmandu through your travel insurance provider.

Should I take Diamox (Acetazolamide)?

∨
Many trekkers carry Diamox (125mg or 250mg) as a preventative aid. Consult your personal physician before departure. Diamox helps the body adapt more quickly to reduced oxygen, but it is not a substitute for gradual acclimatization and hydration.

We require a 20% deposit upon booking confirmation via secure online bank transfer or credit card. The remaining balance can be settled upon arrival in Kathmandu in USD or cash. We offer flexible free date changes up to 30 days prior to departure.

How much personal spending money should I bring?

∨
We recommend carrying approximately $25 to $35 USD per day in Nepali Rupees (NPR) to cover hot showers, Wi-Fi, electronic device charging, personal snacks, and beverages. ATMs are available in Kathmandu, Pokhara, and Jomsom.

How much should I tip the guide and porters?

∨
Tipping is customary in Himalayan trekking and deeply appreciated by the local crews. A common guideline is approximately $100 to $150 USD per trekker, divided among your lead guide, assistant guide, and porter team at the farewell dinner.

What cultural etiquette should I follow at Muktinath and gompas?

∨
Dress modestly with shoulders and knees covered when entering temples and monasteries. Always walk clockwise around Buddhist chortens, mani walls, and prayer wheels. Ask permission before photographing worshippers or monks inside active prayer halls.

What distinct ethnic communities will we encounter?

∨
In the lower Marsyangdi Valley: Gurung and Magar agricultural communities. In Manang: the Tibetan-influenced Manangi (Nyeshang) people with a rich trading heritage. In the Kali Gandaki Valley: the Thakali people, historic masters of Himalayan trade and hospitality.

What environmental principles apply to the circuit?

∨
We strictly practice 'Leave No Trace' mountain ethics. Pack out all non-biodegradable trash, avoid single-use plastic water bottles, use existing toilet facilities, and preserve the delicate alpine flora and fauna within the Annapurna Conservation Area.
