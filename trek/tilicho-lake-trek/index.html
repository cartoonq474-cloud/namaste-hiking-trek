Annapurna Circuit Trek with Tilicho Lake (16 Days)

A legendary 16-day high-altitude journey combining the classic Annapurna Circuit with an unforgettable excursion to turquoise Tilicho Lake (4,919m), crossing Thorong La Pass (5,416m), and scenic flights from Jomsom to Pokhara and Kathmandu.

The Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek weaves two of Nepal's most revered mountain adventures into one continuous 16-day Himalayan voyage. Trek from the lush subtropical valleys of the lower Marsyangdi through traditional Gurung and Tibetan-influenced Manangi settlements, make a dedicated ascent to the turquoise waters of Tilicho Lake (4,919m) under the Annapurna Great Barrier, and conquer the iconic Thorong La Pass at 5,416 meters before descending into the trans-Himalayan desert of Mustang and holy Muktinath. Unlike itineraries with long bumpy return drives, this 16-day program includes panoramic domestic flights from Jomsom to Pokhara through the Kali Gandaki gorge, and from Pokhara to Kathmandu. This ensures maximum trail time, superior acclimatization, and smooth air returns.

Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek Overview

The Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek combines two of the most remarkable high-altitude experiences in the Annapurna region into one continuous 16-day Himalayan journey. The route follows the classic Annapurna Circuit through the Marsyangdi Valley, reaches the high mountain settlements around Manang, makes a dedicated excursion toward Tilicho Lake at 4,919 meters, and then continues toward Thorong La at 5,416 meters before descending through Muktinath and the Kali Gandaki Valley.

Unlike a standard Annapurna Circuit itinerary that may pass through Manang and continue directly toward Yak Kharka and Thorong Phedi, this itinerary adds the high-altitude Tilicho section. The additional excursion takes trekkers from Manang toward Khangsar and Tilicho Base Camp before making the ascent to Tilicho Lake, descending to Siri Kharka, and reconnecting with the circuit at Yak Kharka.

The result is a comprehensive trekking experience that combines green lower valleys, subtropical waterfalls, traditional mountain villages, high alpine scree trails, glacial landscapes, turquoise lakes, dry trans-Himalayan valleys, sacred pilgrimage sites, and scenic mountain flights from Jomsom to Pokhara and Kathmandu.

Complete 16-Day Day-by-Day Itinerary

Detailed route breakdown, walking hours, elevations, authentic trail photography and lodging.

Day 01

Arrival in Kathmandu

20-25 minutes 1,355 m / 4,445 ft 6 km (Transfer) Hotel Breakfast Included
▼
Arrival at Kathmandu Tribhuvan International Airport

Your Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek begins with your arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport in Kathmandu, located at approximately 1,355 m.

A Namaste Hiking Trek representative receives you at the airport and coordinates your transfer to the hotel. After an international flight, this first day is intentionally kept light so you have time to settle into your accommodation, recover from travel, and prepare for the mountain journey ahead.

Once at the hotel, you can organize your luggage and separate the belongings that will be required during the trek from items that can remain in storage. This is also a useful opportunity to check your trekking boots, clothing layers, personal medication, documents, and other essential equipment.

The evening can include a short group meeting and introduction to the trekking guide and other members of the group. The guide can provide an initial overview of the 16-day journey, including the route through the Marsyangdi Valley, the Manang acclimatization day, Tilicho Lake, Thorong La Pass, Muktinath, and Jomsom.

Travelers can also use the evening to become familiar with the Kathmandu environment. A short walk through Thamel can provide an introduction to the city's restaurants, shops, and trekking atmosphere before the journey moves toward the mountains.

This is not a strenuous day, and no trekking is required. The main objective is to arrive comfortably, organize your preparation, and rest before the long journey toward Jagat the following morning.

Day 02

Drive from Kathmandu to Jagat via Besisahar

7-8 hours 1,300 m / 4,265 ft 286 km (7–8 hrs drive) Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
Overland drive to Jagat via Besisahar along Marsyangdi River

The second day begins the journey toward the Annapurna region with a long overland drive from Kathmandu to Jagat via Besisahar.

The route covers approximately 286 km and normally takes around 7 to 8 hours, although actual travel time can vary because of traffic, road conditions, weather, and local circumstances.

As the journey leaves Kathmandu, the landscape gradually changes from the urban Kathmandu Valley toward the middle hills and river valleys of central Nepal. The road provides a visual introduction to the environments that will become more important during the trekking section.

Besisahar serves as an important gateway toward the Annapurna Circuit. From there, the journey continues toward Jagat and the Marsyangdi River valley.

The landscape becomes progressively greener as the road enters the mountain environment. River valleys, terraced hillsides, villages, forested slopes, and waterfalls begin to replace the urban surroundings.

Although this is a road day rather than a trekking day, it plays an important role in the itinerary. It allows the trekking journey to start from a relatively low elevation while gradually moving toward the higher Annapurna region.

Jagat is located at approximately 1,300 m, making it a useful starting point for the gradual ascent ahead.

Upon arrival, you settle into the local accommodation, enjoy a meal, and prepare for the first full trekking day.

The evening is also a good time to make sure your daypack is organized with water, rain protection, snacks, warm layers, and other items you may need during the trail.

Day 03

Trek From Jagat to Dharapani

5 hours 1,960 m / 6,430 ft 12 km (5 hrs walk) Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
Trek from Jagat to Dharapani crossing suspension bridges

The actual trekking journey begins on Day 3 as the route follows the Marsyangdi River valley from Jagat toward Dharapani.

The day's walking distance is approximately 12 km and takes around 5 hours under normal conditions.

The landscape during this section remains relatively green compared with the higher stages of the trek. Forests, agricultural terraces, mountain streams, river crossings, waterfalls, and traditional villages form much of the scenery.

The trail follows the changing contours of the valley, with a mixture of gradual climbs, descents, stone paths, suspension bridges, and uneven mountain terrain.

This first trekking day provides an introduction to the rhythm of the Annapurna Circuit. Rather than immediately climbing into high altitude, the route progresses gradually while giving trekkers time to adapt to repeated mountain walking.

The villages along the way provide opportunities to observe local architecture, agricultural practices, and everyday life. This section also gives travelers their first sustained experience of walking through a Himalayan river valley.

As the route approaches Dharapani, the surrounding mountains become increasingly prominent.

Dharapani sits at approximately 1,960 m, representing a significant increase from Jagat but still well below the highest parts of the itinerary.

After reaching the village, trekkers can settle into the teahouse, recover, eat dinner, and prepare for the longer uphill stages ahead.

Good habits established on this first trekking day, particularly controlled pacing, regular hydration, and careful foot management, can make the remaining trek more comfortable.

Day 04

Trek From Dharapani to Chame

6 hours 2,630 m / 8,628 ft 15 km (6 hrs walk) Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
Trek through pine forests from Dharapani to Chame

Day 4 continues the gradual ascent from Dharapani to Chame.

The route covers approximately 15 km and generally takes around 6 hours.

The landscape changes noticeably as the altitude increases. The lower valley environment gradually gives way to cooler mountain forests and more open terrain.

The trail passes through settlements and forest sections while following the Marsyangdi Valley deeper into the Annapurna region.

Mountain views become increasingly impressive as the surrounding peaks rise above the valley.

The route contains uphill sections as well as descents and uneven surfaces, so travelers should continue maintaining a steady pace rather than attempting to complete the day too quickly.

Chame sits at approximately 2,630 m and is one of the major settlements on the Annapurna Circuit.

The village provides useful trekking facilities and a comfortable stopping point before the route moves to higher elevations.

As altitude increases, trekkers may begin to notice that uphill sections require more controlled breathing and slower movement. This is normal and is one reason the itinerary uses gradual elevation gain.

After arriving in Chame, travelers can rest, enjoy a warm meal, organize their equipment, and prepare for the next day's trek toward Pisang.

Day 05

Trek From Chame to Pissang

6 hours 3,300 m / 10,826 ft 13.9 km (6 hrs walk) Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
Trek from Chame to Upper Pisang beneath Annapurna peaks

The trek from Chame to Pisang is another major stage in the gradual progression toward higher elevation.

The route covers approximately 13.9 km and takes around 6 hours.

As the trail climbs, the surrounding landscape becomes cooler and more distinctly Himalayan.

Forested sections give way to more open mountain terrain, while the views toward the Annapurna range become increasingly expansive.

The route follows the Marsyangdi Valley through changing terrain and traditional settlements.

Pisang is located at approximately 3,300 m, making this an important point in the altitude progression.

Travelers are now entering an environment where hydration, nutrition, pace, and rest become increasingly important.

The higher elevation can make familiar physical activity feel more demanding. Rather than compensating by walking faster during easier sections, travelers should maintain an even rhythm throughout the day.

Pisang is also notable for its traditional settlement environment and mountain scenery.

The village provides an excellent opportunity to observe the transition between the lower valley communities and the more distinct high-altitude culture that becomes increasingly visible around Manang.

After arriving at the teahouse, the evening should focus on recovery and preparation for the following day's longer journey.

Day 06

Trek From Upper Pissang to Manang

6.5 hours 3,500 m / 11,482 ft 16 km (6.5 hrs walk) Guesthouse (Private Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
Panoramic trail from Upper Pisang to Manang with Annapurna range views

Day 6 takes the route deeper into the Annapurna Circuit toward Manang.

The day's distance is approximately 16 km, with a walking time of around 6.5 hours.

The route provides some of the most impressive scenery of the middle section of the circuit.

The mountain environment becomes increasingly open, dry, and expansive as the trail climbs.

The higher route around Pisang and the approach toward Manang provide opportunities for wide mountain views and traditional Himalayan landscapes.

As the trek continues toward Manang, the surrounding peaks become increasingly dominant.

Manang sits at approximately 3,500 m and is one of the most important destinations on the entire itinerary.

It is not only a major trekking settlement but also the principal acclimatization point before the route continues toward Tilicho Lake and the higher stages of the Annapurna Circuit.

The increase from Pisang to Manang is moderate in terms of altitude, but the walking distance makes the day substantial.

Arriving in Manang marks the end of the main continuous ascent from the lower Marsyangdi Valley.

The following day provides an important opportunity to slow down, explore, and adapt to the elevation.

Day 07

Manang Exploration and Acclimatization Day

2-3 hours 3,500 m – 3,800 m 4–6 km (2–3 hrs hike) Guesthouse (Private Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
Acclimatization day hike to Gangapurna Lake in Manang

Day 7 is intentionally different from the surrounding trekking days.

Instead of continuing immediately toward greater elevation, the itinerary includes an exploration and acclimatization day in Manang.

At approximately 3,500 m, Manang provides a suitable environment for spending additional time at elevation before the route moves toward Tilicho Base Camp, Tilicho Lake, and eventually Thorong La.

The day can be used for short walks around the village and surrounding landscape rather than complete inactivity.

Light activity allows travelers to remain mobile while avoiding the unnecessary exertion of a full trekking day.

The guide can recommend suitable walking options based on weather, group condition, and individual fitness.

Manang also provides an excellent cultural experience.

Travelers can explore traditional stone-built structures, Buddhist cultural features, agricultural areas, prayer flags, monasteries, and the distinctive architecture of the high Himalayan settlements.

The village is surrounded by dramatic mountain scenery, including views toward the higher peaks and valleys of the Annapurna region.

The acclimatization day is also an ideal time to evaluate how the body is responding to the altitude.

Travelers should pay attention to hydration, appetite, sleep quality, headache, unusual fatigue, dizziness, nausea, or other symptoms.

The guide can monitor the group and provide practical advice.

The day also provides an opportunity to prepare for the Tilicho section.

Travelers should organize warm clothing, water, snacks, trekking poles, headlamps, and other essential equipment before moving toward the more exposed high-altitude environment.

Day 08

Trek From Manang to Tilicho Base Camp through Khangsar Village

5-6 hours 4,150 m / 13,615 ft 19.1 km (5–6 hrs walk) Guesthouse (Private Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
Trek from Manang to Tilicho Base Camp via Khangsar village

Day 8 begins the dedicated Tilicho Lake section.

The route leaves the main Annapurna Circuit around Manang and continues toward Khangsar Village before ascending toward Tilicho Base Camp.

The total distance is approximately 19.1 km, making this one of the longer and more demanding days of the itinerary. Walking generally takes around 7 to 8 hours.

The landscape changes significantly as the route moves farther from the main circuit.

Vegetation becomes increasingly sparse, while the terrain becomes more rugged and exposed.

Khangsar provides an important cultural stop before the route enters the higher environment leading toward Tilicho.

The trail includes uneven and rocky sections, and careful footing is required.

As the elevation increases toward approximately 4,150 m, the reduced oxygen availability becomes more noticeable.

This is where the acclimatization day in Manang becomes especially important.

Travelers should maintain a slow and steady pace, drink regularly, eat properly, and communicate with the guide if they experience unusual symptoms.

The objective is to reach Tilicho Base Camp without exhausting the body before the following day's high-altitude lake excursion.

Tilicho Base Camp provides the overnight stop before the journey to Tilicho Lake.

The surrounding landscape is markedly different from the green lower Annapurna valleys. The mountains are more barren, the air colder, and the environment more exposed.

After arriving, trekkers should focus on hydration, food, warmth, and preparation for the following day's climb.

Day 09

Hike to Tilicho Lake and Return to Tilicho Base Camp, then Trek to Shree Kharka

6-7 hours 4,919 m / 16,138 ft (High Point) 15 km (6–7 hrs walk) Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
High altitude ascent to turquoise Tilicho Lake at 4919m

Day 9 is one of the major highlights of the entire Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek.

The day's objective is to reach Tilicho Lake at approximately 4,919 m before returning toward the lower section of the route and continuing to Siri Kharka.

The complete day covers approximately 15 km and generally takes around 6 to 7 hours.

The route begins from Tilicho Base Camp and climbs through a high-altitude environment characterized by rugged slopes, exposed terrain, sparse vegetation, and dramatic mountain surroundings.

The ascent is demanding because the trail is already at high elevation.

The cold and reduced oxygen availability mean that trekkers should maintain a deliberate pace.

The approach toward the lake is a major physical achievement.

As the lake comes into view, the landscape changes into a striking high-altitude setting surrounded by massive mountain slopes.

Weather conditions influence visibility and the appearance of the lake. On a clear day, the combination of blue water, surrounding rock, snow, and mountain peaks can create an extraordinary panorama.

After spending appropriate time at the lake, the route begins the return journey.

The descent requires careful foot placement, particularly on rocky or uneven sections.

The day continues toward Siri Kharka, reducing the elevation after the high point at Tilicho.

This descent is important because the itinerary still has the main Annapurna Circuit ahead, including the Thorong La crossing.

Travelers should resist the temptation to spend excessive energy after reaching the lake.

The return section should be approached as part of a complete trekking day, with sufficient water, food, and controlled pacing.

Day 10

Trek from Siri Kharka to Yak Kharka

5 hours 4,050 m / 13,287 ft 11.1 km (5 hrs walk) Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
Descent from Siri Kharka to Yak Kharka alpine pastures

After completing the Tilicho excursion, Day 10 reconnects the journey with the main Annapurna Circuit.

The route from Siri Kharka to Yak Kharka covers approximately 11.1 km and generally takes around 5 hours.

The landscape remains strongly alpine, with sparse vegetation, rocky slopes, broad mountain views, and an increasingly exposed environment.

This day is shorter than the previous lake excursion, allowing the body some recovery before the approach to Thorong Phedi.

The elevation remains high, so trekkers should continue to take altitude precautions.

Yak Kharka is approximately 4,050 m, placing the group firmly within the high-altitude section of the circuit.

The day's walking is not simply about distance. At this elevation, even moderate climbs can require greater effort.

A controlled pace and regular rest breaks can help preserve energy.

The route provides continued opportunities to observe the high Himalayan environment and the changing relationship between vegetation and elevation.

After arriving at Yak Kharka, the afternoon can be used for rest, hydration, food, and preparation for the next day's walk toward Thorong Phedi.

Travelers should check their cold-weather layers and make sure essential items are accessible.

The following day brings another increase in altitude before the major Thorong La crossing.

Day 11

Trek from Yak Kharka to Thorong Phedi

4.5 hours 4,450 m / 14,599 ft 8.5 km (4.5 hrs walk) Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
Trek from Yak Kharka to Thorong Phedi staging post

Day 11 brings the trek closer to the highest point of the entire itinerary.

The route from Yak Kharka to Thorong Phedi at approximately 4,600 m covers around 8.5 km and takes approximately 4.5 hours.

Although this is shorter in distance than several earlier trekking days, the high elevation makes the walk demanding.

The landscape is open, rugged, and alpine.

Vegetation is limited, and the surrounding mountains dominate the scenery.

The trail continues to gain altitude toward Thorong Phedi, which serves as the main staging point before the Thorong La crossing.

Arriving at Thorong Phedi is a significant milestone.

The group is now positioned close to the highest point of the trek, and the following morning will involve the major ascent toward Thorong La at 5,416 m.

The afternoon and evening should be used for rest rather than unnecessary physical activity.

Trekkers can prepare their clothing, headlamp, water, snacks, gloves, warm layers, and other essentials for the early start.

The guide also provides practical information about the next day's route, expected walking duration, weather considerations, and the descent toward Muktinath.

Because the next day is the most demanding of the trek, conserving energy at Thorong Phedi is particularly important.

Day 12

Trek From Thorong Phedi to Muktinath via Thorong-La Pass

8 hours 5,416 m Pass / 3,710 m Muktinath 16.4 km (8 hrs walk) Guesthouse (Shared Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
Crossing Thorong La Pass at 5416m and descent to Muktinath

Day 12 is the defining trekking day of the Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake itinerary.

The route climbs from Thorong Phedi to Thorong La Pass at approximately 5,416 m before descending all the way toward Muktinath at approximately 3,710 m.

The day's total distance is around 16.4 km, with approximately 8 hours of walking.

The day generally begins early.

Starting early provides sufficient daylight for the full crossing and helps the group approach the pass before conditions become more challenging later in the day.

The initial ascent is steady and demanding.

At this elevation, oxygen availability is significantly lower than at the beginning of the trek. Even physically fit trekkers may find the pace slow.

The guide manages the group at a sustainable rhythm, allowing appropriate breaks and encouraging travelers to pay attention to their physical condition.

As the trail continues upward, the surrounding environment becomes increasingly exposed.

Cold temperatures and wind can make the pass particularly demanding, especially during the early morning.

Eventually, the route reaches the summit of Thorong La at 5,416 m.

Standing at the top is one of the major accomplishments of the journey.

Travelers can spend a practical amount of time taking photographs and appreciating the surrounding landscape before beginning the descent.

The descent toward Muktinath is long and significant.

It also produces one of the trek's most dramatic environmental transitions.

The landscape gradually becomes drier, warmer, and more open as the route moves away from the highest part of the Annapurna range.

Muktinath is both a geographical and cultural destination.

The area is an important pilgrimage site for Hindu and Buddhist traditions, and the transition from high-altitude pass to religious and cultural landscape creates a memorable end to the day's journey.

Reaching Muktinath after crossing Thorong La provides a strong sense of completion.

Day 13

Trek From Muktinath to Jomsom

6 hours 2,720 m / 8,923 ft 20.2 km (6 hrs walk) Guesthouse (Private Bathroom) Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner Included
▼
Descent from sacred Muktinath temple to wind-carved Jomsom

After the demanding Thorong La crossing, Day 13 continues the descent from Muktinath toward Jomsom.

The route covers approximately 20.2 km and takes around 6 hours.

The landscape is now very different from the forested lower portions of the Annapurna Circuit.

The route enters the dry environment of the Kali Gandaki Valley and Lower Mustang.

The terrain is more arid, with exposed slopes, rock formations, broad valleys, and strong mountain winds in some sections.

This environmental transformation is one of the most distinctive aspects of completing the full circuit.

The trek follows the valley toward Jomsom, passing through settlements and dry mountain landscapes that reflect the trans-Himalayan character of the region.

The descent to approximately 2,720 m provides a noticeable reduction in altitude compared with the previous day's 5,416 m crossing.

After several days above 4,000 m, the lower elevation can feel considerably more comfortable.

The day's distance is relatively long, but the reduced altitude often makes the physical environment feel different from the high pass section.

Jomsom marks the end of the primary trekking journey.

After arriving, travelers can settle into accommodation, eat, rest, and prepare for the following day's flight to Pokhara.

Day 14

From Jomsom, fly to Pokhara

25 minutes flight and 20 minutes drive 822 m / 2,696 ft Flight (25 min) Hotel Breakfast Included
▼
Mountain flight from Jomsom to Pokhara lakeside

Day 14 marks the transition from trekking to air travel.

After the long trekking journey, travelers leave Jomsom by domestic flight for Pokhara at approximately 822 m.

The flight itself is approximately 25 minutes, followed by the transfer to the hotel.

The aerial journey provides a different perspective on the landscapes traveled through on foot.

The route from Jomsom toward Pokhara crosses a dramatic section of the Himalayan landscape, moving from the dry Kali Gandaki Valley toward the greener lower mountain environment.

Flight operations are dependent on weather and visibility, so the exact departure time may vary.

Travelers should maintain flexibility and follow the instructions of the trekking and transport team.

Once in Pokhara, the atmosphere changes significantly.

The city provides more comfortable accommodation, restaurants, shops, communication services, and opportunities for relaxation than the remote mountain settlements.

After completing Tilicho Lake and Thorong La, travelers can use the rest of the day to recover.

A comfortable meal, rest, and time to organize belongings provide a welcome contrast to the previous nights in high-altitude teahouses.

Day 15

Fly from Pokhara to Kathmandu

25 minutes flight and 20-25 minutes drive 1,355 m / 4,445 ft Flight (25 min) Hotel Breakfast Included
▼
Scenic flight from Pokhara to Kathmandu and celebration

Day 15 begins the final return journey.

The package includes a domestic flight from Pokhara to Kathmandu, with a flight duration of approximately 25 minutes, followed by the transfer to the Kathmandu hotel.

The flight creates another rapid transition between environments.

The traveler has moved from the high-altitude landscapes of Tilicho and Thorong La through Muktinath, Jomsom, and Pokhara, and now returns to the capital.

Once in Kathmandu, the day provides time for rest and final preparation before departure.

Travelers may use the remaining time to explore the city, purchase souvenirs, enjoy a final Nepali meal, or simply recover after two weeks of mountain travel.

The overnight stay in Kathmandu also creates a useful separation between the mountain journey and the international departure.

Keeping this day relatively flexible can provide additional protection against transportation delays affecting the final stages of the itinerary.

Two Distinct High-Altitude Challenges

The defining feature of this itinerary is that Tilicho Lake is treated as a major high-altitude objective rather than an incidental detour. This creates two distinct physical and logistical tests:

  • Tilicho Lake (4,919 m): An excursion away from the circuit into a secluded, vertical glacial cirque. Involves steep switchbacks, exposed scree trail sections, and pre-dawn cold, followed by a descent back to the base camp lodge.
  • Thorong La Pass (5,416 m): A sustained, continuous mountain pass crossing requiring an early pre-dawn ascent of nearly 1,000 vertical meters from Thorong Phedi, followed by an immense 1,600-meter descent to Muktinath in the Mustang kingdom.

Because the body has already spent significant time operating at 4,150–4,919 meters during the Tilicho extension, trekkers arrive at Thorong La with superior physiological acclimatization compared to those following a hurried classic 14-day schedule.

Route Spotlight The Tilicho Lake (4,919m) Excursion: Route, Landslide Zone & Safety

Tilicho Lake is one of the highest accessible glacial lakes in the world, sitting at an elevation of 4,919 meters (16,138 ft) in the high mountains of the Manang region. Nestled directly beneath the staggering 7,134-meter north face of Tilicho Peak and the Annapurna Great Barrier, its waters vary from brilliant turquoise to deep cerulean and frozen white depending on the season.

Navigating the Tilicho Landslide Zone

The trail between Siri Kharka and Tilicho Base Camp crosses the famous Tilicho Landslide Zone. This is an awe-inspiring section where the path is cut across immense slopes of eroded shale, moraine, and fine scree. While dramatic and visually breathtaking, safety protocols are strictly observed:

  • Single-File Movement: Trekkers walk in single file, maintaining steady spacing and continuous situational awareness.
  • Guide Spotters: Our experienced guides position themselves at key vantage points to monitor upper slopes for natural rockfall caused by wind, melting snow, or grazing blue sheep.
  • Pacing and Silence: We move steadily without stopping unnecessarily in exposed gullies. Conversation is kept low to ensure warnings from guides can be heard instantly.
  • Seasonal Assessment: During periods of heavy rain or spring thaw, morning transits are prioritized before solar warming destabilizes loose shale.

The Day 10 Summit Push to the Lake

On Day 10, the excursion starts at approximately 4:30 AM from Tilicho Base Camp (4,150m). Trekkers climb 769 vertical meters over 5.5 kilometers of steep, winding switchbacks. The morning temperature is frequently below -10°C (14°F), requiring full thermal layering, windproof outer shells, thermal gloves, and headlamps.

After 3 to 4 hours of steady climbing, the immense frozen or turquoise expanse of Tilicho Lake reveals itself. We spend roughly 45 to 60 minutes taking photographs and enjoying hot tea before beginning the return walk. The return descent requires careful knee control, as descending fatigue is a common cause of slips. We return to Base Camp or Siri Kharka for an afternoon of deep recovery.

Medical & Safety Altitude Progression, Acclimatization & AMS Safety Guidelines

Operating above 3,000 meters requires understanding how your body adapts to lower atmospheric pressure. At the summit of Thorong La Pass (5,416m), the effective oxygen available per breath is roughly 50% of what is available at sea level. Without adequate time to acclimatize, this deficit causes Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS), which can progress to life-threatening conditions (HAPE or HACE) if ignored.

Symptoms of Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS)

  • Mild AMS: Throbbing frontal headache, mild nausea, loss of appetite, fatigue, dizziness, insomnia.
  • Severe Warning Signs (HAPE / HACE): Persistent coughing with pink frothy sputum, severe breathlessness while resting, loss of motor coordination (ataxia / stumbling walk), confusion, extreme lethargy, slurred speech.

The Cardinal Rule of High-Altitude Safety:

Never ascend with symptoms of altitude sickness. If symptoms worsen despite rest, immediate descent to a lower elevation is mandatory and lifesaving. Reaching Tilicho Lake or Thorong La Pass is never more important than trekker safety.

Our Acclimatization Safeguards

  • Gradual Overland Progression: We spend 6 full days walking up the Marsyangdi Valley before crossing 3,500m.
  • Double Manang Staging: Full acclimatization rest and active hike day on Day 7, plus a post-Tilicho recovery night on Day 11.
  • Climb High, Sleep Low: Tilicho Lake (4,919m) is visited as a daytime excursion; sleeping is done 769 meters lower at Base Camp or Siri Kharka.
  • Twice-Daily Medical Monitoring: Guides carry digital pulse oximeters to check blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) and resting heart rate every morning and evening.
  • Hydration Discipline: Trekkers drink 3.5 to 4 liters of clean water, electrolyte drinks, and warm herbal tea daily.
Training & Fitness Difficulty, Fitness & 12-Week Physical Preparation Guide

The Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek is rated Challenging / Strenuous. While it requires no technical climbing skills (no ropes, harnesses, or technical crampons under standard conditions), it demands sustained cardiovascular endurance, leg power for prolonged descents, and mental grit across 20 consecutive days in rugged Himalayan environments.

The 12-Week Progressive Training Schedule

Weeks 1–4: Aerobic Base
  • 3x weekly brisk walking or jogging (30–45 mins).
  • Stairmaster or hill climbing (20 mins).
  • Squats, lunges, and calf raises (2 sets of 15).
  • Weekend hike of 2–3 hours on natural terrain.
Weeks 5–8: Volume & Load
  • Cardio: 45–60 mins cycling or incline treadmill.
  • Wear daypack with 4–6 kg (8–13 lbs) weight.
  • Focus on eccentric step-downs for knee longevity.
  • Weekend hike: 4–5 hours with 500m+ elevation gain.
Weeks 9–11: Peak Simulation
  • Back-to-back hiking days (Saturday 6 hrs, Sunday 5 hrs).
  • Full pack weight (6–8 kg / 13–18 lbs).
  • Train in actual trekking boots & with trekking poles.
  • Practice nutrition and hydration timing while walking.
Week 12: Taper & Rest
  • Reduce volume by 50%; light walks only.
  • Gentle stretching, foam rolling, and mobility.
  • Focus on hydration, clean nutrition, and 8+ hrs sleep.
  • Arrive in Kathmandu fresh, energized, and uninjured.

Crucial Training Focus: Downhill Strength

Many hikers train exclusively by walking uphill. However, on Day 14 (Thorong La to Muktinath), you will descend 1,600 vertical meters in a single afternoon! Training your quadriceps and glutes with controlled step-downs, box drops, and downhill outdoor walking is essential to prevent severe knee inflammation (patellofemoral pain syndrome) on the trail.

Weather & Timing Best Seasons, High-Altitude Weather & Month-by-Month Analysis

Because this trek traverses both subtropical valleys and arctic high-altitude passes exceeding 5,400 meters, timing your trek during optimal weather windows is essential for clear mountain vistas, safe pass crossings, and accessible lake trails.

1. Autumn (September to November) — The Peak Season

Autumn is universally regarded as the premier season for the Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake. The summer monsoon washes the atmosphere clean of dust and haze, leaving crystal-clear skies, brilliant blue horizons, and crisp visibility across the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri massifs. Daytime temperatures in lower valleys range from 15°C to 22°C, while nights at Tilicho Base Camp and Thorong Phedi dip to -5°C to -10°C.

2. Spring (March to May) — The Season of Flowers & Warmth

Spring is the second peak trekking window. Lower Marsyangdi and Ghorepani forests come alive with vibrant blooming rhododendrons, magnolias, and orchids. Temperatures are milder and days are longer. Tilicho Lake, often frozen solid in early March, gradually thaws into an awe-inspiring turquoise jewel by late April and May. High passes may have remaining winter snowpacks that require microspikes in March.

3. Winter (December to February) & Monsoon (June to August)

  • Winter: Very cold, with temperatures dropping below -20°C at Thorong Phedi. High lodges at Tilicho Base Camp frequently close due to extreme snow, and Thorong La Pass may become impassable. Not recommended for standard groups.
  • Monsoon: Lower Marsyangdi and Pokhara areas receive heavy rainfall, bringing leech activity, muddy trails, and landslides along mountain roads. However, the upper Manang and Mustang sections sit within the rain-shadow and remain relatively dry.
Geography & Ecology Complete Route & Ecological Profile: Subtropical to Trans-Himalayan

Few trekking journeys on the planet encompass the dramatic geographical diversity of the 16-day Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake. Over two weeks, you cross five distinct ecological zones:

  • Subtropical Middle Hills (760m–1,800m): Warm, humid river valleys with terraced paddy fields, banana groves, bamboo thickets, and roaring waterfalls in Besisahar, Jagat, and Tal.
  • Temperate Forest Zone (1,800m–3,000m): Dense mixed forests of blue pine, hemlock, silver fir, and magnificent rhododendrons around Dharapani and Chame.
  • Subalpine Rain-Shadow Zone (3,000m–4,000m): Dry, arid pine and juniper woodlands, carved sandstone canyons, and dramatic open vistas in Upper Pisang and Manang.
  • Alpine Glacial Tundra (4,000m–5,000m): Glacial scree, dwarf junipers, permafrost moraines, and sheer amphitheaters surrounding Khangsar, Tilicho Base Camp, and Thorong Phedi.
  • Arctic High-Altitude Zone (5,000m+): Eternal snowfields, glaciers, and high wind-scoured ridges atop Thorong La Pass (5,416m).
  • Trans-Himalayan Desert (Mustang Plateau): Stark, windswept, ochre-tinted cliffs and deep canyons carved by the Kali Gandaki River in Muktinath, Kagbeni, and Jomsom.
Trail Life Teahouses, Mountain Food Realities & Himalayan Hospitality

The Annapurna Circuit is home to Nepal's most developed teahouse trekking network. Teahouses are family-run mountain lodges providing clean twin-share bedrooms, home-cooked meals, and communal dining rooms heated in the evenings by central wood- or yak-dung-burning stoves.

What to Expect at High Altitude

In lower settlements like Chame, Manang, and Muktinath, teahouses often offer attached private bathrooms, hot gas showers, Wi-Fi, and extensive menus with Italian pasta, yak burgers, and German bakery treats. However, at extreme outposts like Tilicho Base Camp (4,150m) and Thorong Phedi (4,450m), facilities become basic:

  • Rooms feature plywood walls and twin foam beds with quilts; bringing a -15°C rated four-season sleeping bag is strongly recommended.
  • Bathrooms are shared squat or western toilets; running water may freeze overnight during cold months.
  • Charging devices and hot bucket showers require a small nominal fee ($2–$4 USD) payable directly to the lodge owner.

The Power of Dal Bhat

The staple fuel of the Himalaya is Dal Bhat Tarkari—steamed rice, savory lentil soup, spiced vegetable curry, and pickled greens. Not only is it freshly prepared and hygienically cooked, but teahouses provide unlimited free refills of rice, lentils, and curry! A hearty plate of Dal Bhat guarantees the sustained carbohydrates and protein needed for long days on the trail.

Culture & People Cultural Landscapes: Gurung, Manangi, Thakali & Sacred Muktinath

The Annapurna Circuit is as much a profound cultural pilgrimage as a wilderness trek. Along the route, you encounter four distinct Himalayan ethnic cultures, each with unique architectural, linguistic, and spiritual traditions:

  • Gurung and Magar Communities (Lower Marsyangdi & Ghorepani): Renowned for their hospitality, animistic-Buddhist traditions, and proud history in the British Gurkha regiments.
  • Manangi People (Upper Marsyangdi & Tilicho): Indigenous inhabitants of Manang practicing Tibetan Vajrayana Buddhism. Historic cross-Himalayan traders who built fortified stone towns, cliffside hermitages, and ancient monasteries.
  • Thakali Community (Kali Gandaki & Marpha): Renowned throughout Nepal as master innkeepers and traders along the historic Trans-Himalayan Salt Route connecting Tibet to India.
  • Sacred Pilgrimage at Muktinath (3,800m): A holy sanctuary where Hindus bathe under 108 sacred bull-head water spouts to attain liberation (Moksha) and Buddhists honor Guru Rinpoche at the miraculous eternal flame of Jwala Mai.
Flora & Fauna Wildlife, Biodiversity & the Annapurna Conservation Area (ACAP)

Established in 1986, the Annapurna Conservation Area Project (ACAP) is Nepal's largest protected nature reserve, encompassing 7,629 square kilometers. Its extraordinary elevation gradient—from 760m to 8,091m—nurtures world-class biodiversity.

While trekking, keep your binoculars ready for Himalayan blue sheep (Bharal) grazing on high alpine slopes above Khangsar and Thorong Phedi, golden eagles soaring over mountain passes, bearded vultures (Lammergeier), musk deer, and the elusive snow leopard inhabiting the remote cliffs of Tilicho. Lower forests teem with grey langur monkeys, Himalayan black bears, and over 450 species of birds.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the key difference between the standard Annapurna Circuit and the Tilicho Lake version?

The standard Annapurna Circuit typically bypasses Tilicho Lake, heading straight from Manang to Yak Kharka and crossing Thorong La Pass in 14–16 days. The Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake Trek adds a dedicated multi-day excursion from Manang into the high glacial amphitheater of Tilicho Lake (4,919m) before rejoining the circuit. This adds dramatic glacial scenery, an additional high-altitude challenge, superior acclimatization, and extends the journey to 16 comprehensive days.

How difficult is the Tilicho Lake excursion on Day 10?

The Tilicho Lake excursion is physically demanding and rated strenuous. Trekkers climb 769 vertical meters from Tilicho Base Camp (4,150m) to the lake (4,919m) and return the same afternoon, covering approximately 12 km round-trip in 6 to 8 hours. The steep gradient, sub-zero morning temperatures, and thin air require steady pacing, warm layers, and mental focus. However, technical climbing equipment is not required.

Is the landslide zone between Khangsar and Tilicho Base Camp dangerous?

The trail crosses fine scree and moraine slopes where erosion occurs naturally. While the path is well-defined and traversed daily by trekkers, porters, and pack animals, it demands care. Our guides position themselves as lookouts, manage group pacing, and ensure trekkers move in a single-file line without stopping in exposed gullies. Walking sticks and good foot placement make this section safe and exhilarating.

What is the highest point reached on the 16-day trek?

The highest point reached is Thorong La Pass at 5,416 meters (17,769 ft) on Day 14. The second highest point is Tilicho Lake at 4,919 meters (16,138 ft) on Day 10.

Where do we sleep before crossing Thorong La Pass: Thorong Phedi or High Camp?

We prioritize sleeping at Thorong Phedi (~4,450m). While Thorong High Camp (~4,850m) shortens summit morning by an hour, sleeping nearly 5,000 meters high significantly increases the risk of severe AMS, insomnia, and altitude complications. Because our trekkers are already exceptionally acclimatized from Tilicho Lake, the climb from Phedi to the pass is safe, manageable, and far more restful.

Can beginners undertake this 16-day trek?

Yes, dedicated beginners with strong cardiovascular fitness, good endurance, and a positive mindset can successfully complete this trek. Because this 16-day itinerary features built-in acclimatization days in Manang and gradual elevation gain, your body has ample time to adapt. Previous hiking experience with a loaded daypack is highly recommended.

What permits are required for this trek?

Two official permits are mandatory: the Annapurna Conservation Area Project (ACAP) Entry Permit and the Trekkers' Information Management System (TIMS) Card. Both permits are completely arranged and included in our package.

What is the best season to trek Annapurna Circuit with Tilicho Lake?

The two optimal trekking windows are Autumn (late September to late November) and Spring (March to May). Autumn delivers the clearest skies, crisp mountain vistas, and stable pass conditions. Spring offers warmer daytime weather, colorful blooming rhododendron forests in lower valleys, and thawing turquoise lake waters.

Is technical mountaineering equipment required?

No technical climbing gear (ropes, harnesses, ice axes) is needed under normal trekking conditions. However, a lightweight pair of slip-on microspikes / shoe chains is recommended in early spring or late autumn when compact snow or ice may form on high trail switchbacks near Thorong La Pass or Tilicho Lake.

What happens if someone develops altitude sickness (AMS)?

Our lead guides are certified Wilderness First Responders equipped with pulse oximeters and medical kits. At the first sign of mild AMS, we stop ascent, rest, and hydrate. If symptoms persist or worsen, our strict protocol requires immediate descent to lower elevations (such as descending from Tilicho Base Camp to Khangsar or Manang). In severe emergencies, we coordinate rapid helicopter evacuation with international travel insurers.

What kind of accommodation and food should I expect?

You stay in local teahouses (mountain lodges) with twin-bed private rooms. In lower villages, attached bathrooms and hot showers are common. At Tilicho Base Camp and Thorong Phedi, facilities are basic with shared squat/western toilets. Daily meals include hearty Nepali Dal Bhat (lentil soup with rice and veggies), pasta, noodles, potatoes, eggs, momos, porridge, and hot teas.

Is Wi-Fi, electricity, and hot water available along the trail?

Yes, Wi-Fi and solar/hydro-electric charging are available in almost all teahouses up to Manang. At Tilicho Base Camp and Thorong Phedi, satellite Wi-Fi (such as Airzed or Everest Link) and solar device charging are available for a small fee ($2–$4 USD). Hot showers are available across most teahouses, utilizing gas geysers or solar heating.

Can this 16-day itinerary be customized or shortened?

Absolutely! For trekkers with tighter schedules, we can organize private jeep transfers on road-connected sections (e.g. driving directly from Jomsom to Pokhara or flying from Jomsom) to condense the trek to 16–18 days, or customize rest days in Pokhara to suit your party's exact preferences.

What are the luggage and weight limits for porters?

Our company strictly adheres to ethical porter guidelines established by the International Porter Protection Group (IPPG). Each porter carries a maximum of 20–22 kg total (divided between two trekkers, or roughly 10–11 kg per client). You will only carry a lightweight daypack (5–7 kg) containing water, camera, rain layers, and personal valuables.
