Trek Overview

The 17-day Upper Mustang Trek is a remarkable Himalayan journey into the remote trans-Himalayan landscapes of northern Nepal, where dramatic mountain scenery, ancient settlements, Tibetan-influenced culture, Buddhist heritage and the historic walled city of Lo Manthang come together in one of Nepal’s most distinctive trekking experiences.

Unlike many popular trekking routes in Nepal that pass through dense forests, lush valleys and glacial landscapes, Upper Mustang has a distinctly dry and rugged character. The trail moves through wide barren valleys, wind-carved cliffs, exposed ridgelines, deep gorges, dry riverbeds and traditional villages built from stone, mud and earth. Small areas of cultivated farmland appear around settlements, creating a sharp contrast with the surrounding arid terrain.

Located north of the main Himalayan barriers in the rain shadow of the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges, Upper Mustang enjoys much drier conditions than other parts of Nepal. This makes it particularly suitable for trekking during the summer monsoon months (June, July, and August) when clear skies prevail. With high overnight elevations reaching approximately 3,840 m at Lo Manthang and daylight pass crossings around 4,010 m, this complete 17-day journey provides an unhurried, safe, and deeply immersive mountain experience.

Trip Features 16 Unforgettable Moments

16 Highlights of the Upper Mustang Trek

Key landmarks, ancient cultural discoveries, and trans-Himalayan landscapes experienced along the 17-day journey.

Ancient Walled City of Lo Manthang

Enter the medieval fortified capital of the Kingdom of Lo, founded in 1380 AD with intact defense walls and whitewashed mud-brick buildings.

Centenary Royal Palace of the King

Stand before the historic four-story palace of the Mustangi King (Lo Gyalpo) in the heart of Lo Manthang’s labyrinthine alleys.

8th-Century Ghar Gompa (Lo Gekar)

Visit one of the oldest active Tibetan Buddhist monasteries in the Himalayas, famous for intricate painted carved stones linked to Guru Rinpoche.

Chhoser Ancient Sky Caves (Jhong Cave)

Explore multi-story cliffside cave dwellings dating back over 2,500 years, complete with connecting ladders, hearths, and burial chambers.

Namgyal Gompa & Tingkhar Excursion

A dedicated cultural walking day north of Lo Manthang visiting the 'Monastery of Victory' and the authentic traditional settlement of Tingkhar.

Rain-Shadow Summer Monsoon Trekking

Protected behind the Annapurna-Dhaulagiri barrier, Upper Mustang receives under 300mm annual rainfall, providing sunny, clear skies in July and August.

Flaming Red Sandstone Cliffs of Drakmar

Marvel at the vibrant crimson and ochre canyon bluffs of Drakmar, sculpted by mountain winds and pierced by ancient hermit caves.

Great Mani Wall of Ghami

Walk alongside Nepal’s longest continuous stone-carved Buddhist prayer wall, stretching over 300 meters with thousands of carved mantras.

World's Deepest Kali Gandaki River Gorge

Trek through the geological marvel flanked by two 8,000-meter giants—Annapurna I (8,091 m) and Dhaulagiri (8,167 m).

14th-Century Gompas of Lo Manthang

Admire masterwork murals, gilded statues, and ancient mandalas inside Jampa Lhakhang ("God House"), Thubchen Gompa, and Chode Gompa.

Medieval Gateway Village of Kagbeni

Stroll through the 500-year-old mud-brick alleys, prayer-wheel corridors, and historic checkpoint where the restricted kingdom begins.

Sacred Pilgrimage Shrine of Muktinath (3,710 m)

Experience the sacred sanctuary revered by Hindus and Buddhists alike, featuring 108 stone water spouts and an eternal natural gas flame.

Pure Tibetan Culture & Lobas Heritage

Encounter an undisturbed mountain culture where Tibetan customs, language, traditional dress, and Bon religious roots remain vibrant.

Nilgiri, Tilicho Peak & Dhaulagiri Vistas

Witness sweeping mountain views from high arid passes, contrasting stark desert plains against blinding white snow peaks.

Scenic Mountain STOL Flights (PKR–JOM)

Experience breathtaking 25-minute flights connecting subtropical Pokhara with the high desert of Jomsom right through the mountain pass.

Serene Pokhara Lakeside Rest & Recovery

Relax beside the calm waters of Phewa Lake with Annapurna views before heading into the wild and upon completing your desert trek.

Upper Mustang Trek Itinerary: 17-Day Day-by-Day Guide

Our comprehensive 17-day Upper Mustang itinerary follows a carefully engineered progression through the ancient Kingdom of Lo. Incorporating round-trip Pokhara-Jomsom flights, 11 trail walking days, dedicated exploration around Lo Manthang and Tingkhar, and high pass traverses, this classic route guarantees optimal acclimatization, cultural immersion, and trans-Himalayan desert wonder.

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu & Transfer to Hotel

Kathmandu – 1,355 m / 4,445 ft

Transfer time: 20 to 30 minutes
Accommodation: 3-Star Hotel in Kathmandu
Activity: Airport welcome & trek orientation
Meals: Welcome Dinner (D)
Overnight: Kathmandu (1,355 m)

Your Upper Mustang Trek begins with your arrival in Kathmandu, the capital of Nepal and the starting point for your Himalayan journey. After landing at Tribhuvan International Airport, you complete immigration and baggage formalities before continuing to your hotel.

The first day is intentionally kept free from trekking activity. After an international flight, the priority is to settle into the hotel, recover from your journey and prepare mentally and physically for the days ahead.

The transfer from the airport to your accommodation gives you your first introduction to Kathmandu. The capital is a complete contrast to Upper Mustang. The streets are busy, the urban environment is dense and the city brings together historic architecture, modern services, traditional markets and religious landmarks.

Once you arrive at the hotel, there is time to check your trekking equipment and organize your luggage. Items that will be needed every day on the trail should be separated from unnecessary belongings so that the trekking baggage remains practical and manageable.

A short briefing can also be used to review the complete route, expected trekking conditions, daily walking hours, accommodation arrangements, meals, transportation and the practical requirements of trekking in the Upper Mustang region.

Day 2: Kathmandu Sightseeing & Restricted Trekking Preparation

Kathmandu – 1,355 m / 4,445 ft

Activity duration: 4 to 6 hours
Accommodation: 3-Star Hotel in Kathmandu
Activity: UNESCO World Heritage tour & permit issuance
Meals: Breakfast (B)
Overnight: Kathmandu (1,355 m)

Before moving into the mountains, this day provides an opportunity to experience some of Kathmandu's cultural heritage. The exact sightseeing program can be organized around the available time, local conditions and the overall trekking schedule, but the purpose is to introduce you to Nepal's historic and religious environment before the journey moves into Mustang.

Kathmandu's cultural landscape offers a sharp contrast to the remote villages you will encounter later in the itinerary. Historic courtyards, temples, monasteries, traditional architecture and busy urban streets provide a first look at Nepal's diverse cultural heritage.

The second half of the day is focused on trekking preparation.

This is the time to make sure your clothing, footwear, daypack and other essential equipment are ready for the mountain section. Personal medication, documents, permits and other important items should be kept accessible rather than buried inside larger luggage.

The preparation day is particularly useful because the Upper Mustang route becomes increasingly remote after Kagbeni. Once the trek moves deeper into the restricted area, access to a wide range of urban services is limited. Preparation in Kathmandu therefore reduces unnecessary problems later.

Day 3: Drive from Kathmandu to Pokhara

Pokhara – 822 m / 2,697 ft (200 km / 124 mi)

Drive duration: 5 to 6 hours (200 km)
Accommodation: 3-Star Hotel in Pokhara (Lakeside)
Activity: Scenic highway drive along Trishuli & Marsyangdi rivers
Meals: Breakfast (B)
Overnight: Pokhara Lakeside (822 m)

The journey leaves Kathmandu on Day 3 and heads west toward Pokhara, the major gateway for travel into the Annapurna and Mustang regions.

The road journey covers approximately 200 kilometres and generally takes around 5 to 6 hours, although actual travel time can vary with road conditions, traffic, weather and temporary delays.

This day is primarily a transportation day, but it also marks an important geographical transition. Kathmandu is located in the central hill region of Nepal, while Pokhara sits considerably lower and provides access toward the high mountain valleys farther north.

The journey passes through changing landscapes, including river valleys, terraced hillsides, rural settlements and mountain scenery. As the road continues westward, the urban character of Kathmandu gradually disappears and the landscape becomes more open and rural.

Pokhara is an important staging point for the Upper Mustang Trek because the following morning's travel continues toward Jomsom. The town also provides a useful final opportunity to organize personal items before entering the remote trekking region.

Day 4: Fly Pokhara to Jomsom & Trek to Kagbeni

Kagbeni – 2,743 m / 8,999 ft (10 km / 6.2 mi)

Flight: 25 mins | Trek: 4 to 5 hours (10 km)
Accommodation: Mountain Teahouse in Kagbeni
Activity: Kali Gandaki gorge flight & trek to gateway of Upper Mustang
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner (B, L, D)
Overnight: Kagbeni (2,743 m)

The day begins with the flight from Pokhara to Jomsom, a short mountain flight that takes approximately 25 minutes under suitable operating conditions. Flying north along the Kali Gandaki corridor provides an immediate transition from the lower hill environment to the dramatic mountain landscape of Mustang.

Jomsom sits at approximately 2,720 metres, and the surrounding landscape is noticeably drier and more rugged than Pokhara. The mountain environment becomes more exposed, with barren slopes, rocky ridges and strong winds characteristic of the Kali Gandaki valley.

After arriving in Jomsom, the trek continues toward Kagbeni, covering approximately 10 kilometres in around 4 to 5 hours.

This is the first walking stage, so the distance is intentionally manageable compared with the longer trekking days that follow. The objective is to introduce your body to the higher elevation and new mountain environment without immediately imposing a long day of strenuous walking.

The route gradually follows the Kali Gandaki area toward Kagbeni. The scenery changes quickly, with the mountains becoming increasingly dry and the villages taking on a distinctly trans-Himalayan appearance.

Day 5: Trek from Kagbeni to Chele

Chele – 3,050 m / 10,006 ft (15 km / 9.3 mi)

Trek duration: 6 to 7 hours (15 km)
Accommodation: Mountain Lodge in Chele
Activity: Enter restricted Upper Mustang zone & cliffside ascent
Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner (B, L, D)
Overnight: Chele (3,050 m)

The fifth day begins the deeper journey into Upper Mustang as you trek from Kagbeni to Chele.

This is a longer walking day of approximately 15 kilometres, generally taking around 6 to 7 hours depending on pace, trail conditions and rest periods. The route continues to gain elevation, reaching approximately 3,050 metres at Chele.

The morning begins with the distinctive mountain environment of Kagbeni before the route moves farther into the dry valleys of Mustang. The trail passes through rugged terrain with limited vegetation and increasingly dramatic rock formations.

As the route progresses, the contrast between the lower Kali Gandaki region and Upper Mustang becomes clearer. The mountains are more exposed, the climate feels drier and the settlements reflect the traditional architecture of the northern Mustang region.

The walking conditions can be quite different from trails through forested parts of Nepal. Shade is limited on many sections, so sun protection, hydration and a steady walking rhythm are important.

Day 6: Trek from Chele to Gheling

Gheling – 3,570 m / 11,712 ft (17 km / 10.5 mi)

Overnight: Gheling (3,570 m)

The day involves steady walking through increasingly high and exposed terrain. The landscape becomes more expansive as the route moves through valleys and over mountain sections, offering a stronger sense of the remote character of Upper Mustang.

The climb toward Gheling is an important part of the acclimatization progression. Instead of making one rapid ascent to Lo Manthang, the itinerary increases sleeping elevation in stages.

This gradual movement gives trekkers time to adapt while continuing to make meaningful progress toward the main destination.

The trail environment remains predominantly dry and rocky, with dramatic hills and cliffs surrounding the route. Traditional settlements appear as carefully located pockets of human habitation within a much larger barren landscape.

One of the memorable aspects of this section is the contrast between the natural environment and the cultivated areas around the villages. Irrigation and farming make it possible for communities to create green agricultural zones despite the surrounding dry terrain.

Day 7: Trek from Gheling to Charang (Tsarang)

The route continues from Gheling to Charang on Day 7, covering approximately 20 kilometres in around 6 to 7 hours.

This is one of the longer walking days of the outward journey and forms an important stage before the final approach to Lo Manthang.

The trail continues through Upper Mustang's characteristic combination of barren mountain terrain, dry valleys and traditional villages. The route repeatedly rises and falls, creating a full trekking day that requires good pacing rather than a hurried approach.

At this point, the surrounding landscape is unmistakably trans-Himalayan. The lack of dense vegetation creates broad views across exposed ridges and valley walls, while the villages appear as compact communities surrounded by cultivated land.

Walking through the Mustang region also provides a gradual introduction to its cultural landscape. Mani walls, chortens, prayer flags and traditional buildings appear along and around the trail, creating visible connections between the landscape and Buddhist heritage.

The trek covers approximately 19 kilometres and takes around 6 to 7 hours, with Lo Manthang situated at approximately 3,840 metres.

The morning begins from Charang, with the trail continuing through the dry mountain terrain that has characterized the trek since Kagbeni.

As the route advances, the landscape opens into the high valley surrounding Lo Manthang. The sense of entering a more isolated Himalayan environment becomes stronger as the route approaches the historic settlement.

The day's walking requires a steady pace because of both distance and altitude. Although the itinerary does not involve technical climbing, the body is now operating at a significantly higher elevation than in the opening days.

Taking regular breaks and maintaining adequate hydration are important. There is no benefit in rushing the final approach. The objective is to arrive at Lo Manthang with enough energy to enjoy the cultural exploration that follows.

Day 9: Active Cultural Exploration of Namgyal Gompa & Tingkhar

Lo Manthang – 3,840 m+ / 12,598 ft+ (16 km / 9.9 mi)

This day is one of the most important cultural components of the package because Lo Manthang should not be treated simply as an overnight waypoint.

The purpose of the exploration day is to experience the cultural and settlement landscape surrounding the historic town while maintaining enough time to move at a practical pace.

Namgyal Gompa is an important religious site associated with the Lo Manthang area. Visiting the gompa provides an opportunity to observe the Buddhist cultural environment that defines much of Upper Mustang.

The journey continues toward Tingkhar, another traditional settlement area near Lo Manthang. Walking beyond the central town allows a broader view of how communities are distributed around the valley and how agriculture, religious institutions and residential areas fit into the high-altitude environment.

After the cultural exploration around Lo Manthang, Day 10 begins the return journey toward the southern part of Mustang.

Leaving Lo Manthang creates a noticeable shift in the rhythm of the journey. The major destination has been reached and explored, but the trek is not yet finished. The return route provides additional landscapes and settlements that reveal another side of Upper Mustang.

The trail once again enters broad dry valleys and exposed mountain terrain. The return journey should not be viewed as simply retracing the approach. Instead, it follows a different sequence of destinations and offers new scenery.

As the day progresses, the landscape around Drakmar becomes increasingly dramatic.

The route also continues to pass through culturally significant areas. Traditional architecture, Buddhist structures and agricultural settlements appear wherever the geography provides suitable conditions for habitation.

The physical experience remains substantial. Consecutive days of 17 to 20 kilometres require good endurance, and the accumulated fatigue from the earlier part of the trek should be managed carefully.

The air gradually becomes less thin as the trail moves downward, while the surrounding valleys take on a somewhat different appearance. The transition is not sudden, but the decreasing elevation becomes increasingly noticeable.

The route continues through dry and rugged terrain, retaining the distinctive geographic identity of Mustang even as the altitude decreases.

Chhusang is an important settlement in the broader Mustang trekking corridor and provides a practical overnight location before the journey continues toward Muktinath.

The route provides a different perspective on the Mustang landscape as you move toward one of Nepal's major pilgrimage areas.

Muktinath is significant to both Hindu and Buddhist traditions and has long attracted pilgrims from Nepal, India and the wider Himalayan region.

The arrival in Muktinath therefore adds a strong religious dimension to the trekking package.

The route gradually loses elevation toward Jomsom, creating a physical contrast with the ascent toward Muktinath on the previous day.

As you descend, the landscape continues to evolve. The higher, drier Mustang terrain gradually transitions toward the broader Kali Gandaki valley environment around Jomsom.

The route provides time to reflect on the distance already covered. Since leaving Kagbeni, you have crossed high mountain terrain, passed through traditional settlements, reached Lo Manthang, explored its cultural surroundings and then completed the return circuit through the remote landscapes of Upper Mustang.

Once you reach Pokhara, you check into the hotel and have time to rest.

The journey began with the busy urban environment of Kathmandu before moving through Pokhara and Jomsom into the remote restricted region. Accommodation for the Upper Mustang Trek

Accommodation throughout the Upper Mustang Trek reflects the authentic character and architectural heritage of the trans-Himalayan region. During your trek, you will stay in clean, family-operated mountain teahouses and traditional lodges constructed from whitewashed stone, mud-brick, and exposed timber beams.

Rooms are typically arranged on a twin-sharing basis with wooden twin beds, clean mattresses, pillows, and cozy blankets. While extra blankets are provided by lodge hosts, bringing a four-season sleeping bag (rated to -10°C) is essential because night temperatures in Upper Mustang can drop rapidly after sunset, especially in Lo Manthang (3,840 m).

In major settlements such as Kagbeni, Charang, and Lo Manthang, lodges have evolved significantly in recent years. Many now offer attached private bathrooms with Western flush toilets, solar-heated showers, and communal dining rooms heated in the evenings by central wood or dried-dung stoves. In smaller hamlets like Chele, Drakmar, and Sangboche, amenities remain traditional, with shared clean squat or flush toilets and hot water available in buckets upon request for a modest fee ($2–$3).

City Lodging Note: In Kathmandu and Pokhara, our 17-day package includes 3 nights in Kathmandu and 2 nights in Pokhara at hand-picked 3-star standard boutique hotels (with en-suite bathrooms, 24/7 hot showers, Wi-Fi, and breakfast included).

Available Food & Dining in Upper Mustang

Meals in Upper Mustang provide hearty, wholesome nutrition crafted for high-altitude trekking. Lodge menus combine classic Nepalese staples, local Tibetan-Mustangi culinary traditions, and popular international trekker dishes.

The staple of the trek is Dal Bhat Tarkari (steamed rice, spiced lentil soup, seasonal vegetable curry, and savory pickles). Served with unlimited refills of rice, dal, and vegetables, it remains the ultimate power meal for mountain hikers. Other popular dishes include fried noodles (chow mein), vegetable momos (steamed or fried dumplings), thukpa (hearty Tibetan noodle soup), egg fried rice, macaroni pasta, potato rosti, and fresh vegetable stews.

Local specialties are a distinct highlight in the Kingdom of Lo: try fresh Tibetan flatbread with yak butter or honey, Tsampa (roasted barley flour porridge eaten by Himalayan monks), local buckwheat pancakes, and Mustang’s renowned organic apples, apple pies, and apple ciders around Kagbeni and Jomsom.

Hydration & Dietary Requests: Drinking 3 to 4 litres of water daily is crucial in Mustang’s arid, windswept climate. We cater seamlessly to vegetarians, vegans, and gluten-free diets upon advance request.

Best Time to Trek Upper Mustang (The Monsoon Advantage)

Unlike almost all other trekking routes in Nepal that are rendered inaccessible or waterlogged by the summer monsoon, Upper Mustang is one of the world's premier monsoon trekking destinations. It lies in the geographical rain shadow of the immense Dhaulagiri and Annapurna massifs, receiving less than 200 mm of precipitation each year.

Summer Monsoon (June, July, August): While Pokhara and Kathmandu receive torrential downpours, Upper Mustang enjoys bright sunny skies, dry mountain trails, and comfortable daytime temperatures between 16°C and 22°C. The irrigated valley floors turn vibrant emerald green with flowering buckwheat and barley crops, presenting breathtaking visual contrasts against red and ochre canyon walls.

Autumn (September, October, November): The most popular trekking window across Nepal. Crisp, crystal-clear Himalayan skies, exceptional mountain visibility of Nilgiri, Dhaulagiri, and Tilicho Peak, and mild daytime temperatures. Nights in November grow cold, dropping to -5°C in Lo Manthang.

Spring (March, April, May): Excellent trekking conditions with lengthening days, blooming wildflowers, and mild pass crossings. May also hosts the legendary three-day Tiji Festival in Lo Manthang, featuring elaborate masked dances performed by monks to chase away evil spirits.

Winter (December–February): Bitterly cold with heavy snowfall on passes. Most Loba residents migrate down to Pokhara or Kathmandu, and many mountain teahouses close.

Daily routines on the Upper Mustang trail are structured to work in harmony with the unique weather and wind dynamics of the Kali Gandaki valley.

  • 6:30 AM – Morning Wakeup: Hot tea or coffee served in the dining hall, followed by packing your duffel bag for the porters.
  • 7:00 AM – Breakfast: A hearty breakfast of porridge, eggs, Tibetan bread, pancakes, or muesli.
  • 7:45 AM – Trail Departure: Early morning departures are essential. Between 11:00 AM and 2:00 PM, fierce southern winds funnel up the Kali Gandaki canyon. Trekking early allows you to complete most walking hours under calm morning conditions.
  • 12:00 PM – Lunch Stop: Hot lunch enjoyed at a teahouse settlement midway along the daily route (e.g., Samar, Ghemi, or Tsarang).
  • 2:30 PM – 3:30 PM – Arrival at Overnight Lodge: Check into your twin room, wash up, change into comfortable warm evening clothes, and enjoy afternoon tea.

Permits & Restricted Area Regulations for Upper Mustang

Upper Mustang is classified by the Government of Nepal as a Restricted Trekking Area to protect its delicate Tibetan Buddhist heritage and sensitive border environment near Tibet (China). Strict governmental regulations govern entry:

  • Restricted Area Permit (RAP): Costs USD 500 per person for the first 10 days within the restricted zone (north of Kagbeni), plus USD 50 per additional day thereafter. Our 17-day itinerary covers the exact restricted duration needed without permit overruns.
  • Annapurna Conservation Area Project (ACAP): NPR 3,000 (approx. USD 25 per person) required for traversing the Annapurna region between Jomsom, Kagbeni, and Muktinath.
  • Minimum Group Size: Under immigration rules, RAP permits are never issued to solo individual trekkers. A minimum of two registered trekkers is legally mandatory. If you are a solo traveler, Namaste Hiking Trek can easily pair you with another traveler or arrange a ghost permit where allowed.
  • Mandatory Licensed Guide: Independent trekking is strictly illegal in Upper Mustang. Trekkers must be accompanied at all times by a government-licensed Nepalese guide arranged through a registered agency.

Altitude Profile & Acclimatization Strategy

Compared to other flagship Himalayan circuits like Everest Base Camp (5,364 m) or Thorong La (5,416 m), Upper Mustang is moderate in maximum altitude. The principal overnight destination and high sleeping elevation is Lo Manthang at 3,840 m (12,598 ft), while daylight pass crossings such as Nyi La reach 4,010 m (13,156 ft) and Gyu La reaches 4,077 m.

Our 17-day itinerary is purposefully crafted to minimize altitude sickness (Acute Mountain Sickness):

We begin from Jomsom (2,720 m) and Kagbeni (2,743 m), increasing sleeping elevation gradually over 5 days before reaching Lo Manthang. Furthermore, Day 9 is a dedicated active exploration day in Lo Manthang, allowing your body to acclimatize thoroughly while visiting Namgyal Gompa and Tingkhar without packing bags or changing lodges.

Safety Protocols: Our guides carry pulse oximeters to check your blood oxygen saturation twice daily (morning and evening), monitor for early signs of AMS, and carry emergency medical kits and descent contingencies.

Trek Difficulty, Trail Conditions & Fitness Required

The 17-day Upper Mustang Trek is graded as Moderate. It does not require technical mountaineering skills, ropes, crampons, or glacial navigation. The trails consist of broad dirt tracks, packed earth paths, stone steps, canyon ridges, and dry gravel riverbeds.

However, the trek presents distinct physical demands that require solid preparation:

  • Consecutive Walking Days: You will hike for 11 consecutive days on the trail, averaging 5 to 7 hours and 15 to 20 km per day.
  • Arid Climate & Afternoon Winds: The high-desert air is extremely dry, causing faster dehydration. Afternoon headwinds in the Kali Gandaki gorge can tire hikers who do not pace themselves.
  • Pass Crossings: While passes are below 4,100 m, the trail includes steep ascents and descents over loose scree and canyon shoulders (e.g., Taklam La, Nyi La, Lo La).

Training Recommendation: We recommend 6 to 8 weeks of regular cardiovascular training (brisk hiking with an 8 kg daypack, jogging, stair climbing, or cycling) before arrival to ensure an enjoyable and energetic journey.

Cultural Heritage of Lo: Monasteries, Chortens & Traditions

Upper Mustang is one of the last living sanctuaries of traditional Tibetan Buddhist civilization on earth. Historically known as the Kingdom of Lo, the region remained politically and geographically secluded until opening to foreign trekkers in 1992.

Culturally and linguistically, the Loba people share deep ties with Tibet. Throughout your trek, you will witness active spiritual traditions untouched by modern globalization:

  • Ancient Gompas: Visit centuries-old monasteries including Champa Lhakhang (dating to 1420 AD with immense gilded Maitreya Buddha statues), Thubchen Gompa with magnificent 15th-century frescoes, and Namgyal Gompa ("Monastery of Victory") perched dramatically on a high ridge.
  • 8th-Century Ghar Gompa (Lo Gekar): Located near Drakmar, this is one of the oldest Tibetan Buddhist monasteries in existence, established by Guru Padmasambhava even before Samye Monastery in Tibet.
  • Sacred Mani Walls & Chortens: Ghemi features the longest stone Mani wall in Mustang (over 300 meters long), adorned with meticulously carved Buddhist mantras (Om Mani Padme Hum).

The Mysterious Sky Caves of Mustang & Chhoser

One of the most enigmatic archaeological wonders of the world, Upper Mustang contains an estimated 10,000 man-made "Sky Caves" hollowed out of sheer vertical sandstone cliff faces up to 50 meters above the canyon floor.

During our exploration day from Lo Manthang, we visit the legendary Jhong Cave at Chhoser. This five-story cliff fortress features dozens of interconnected chambers carved by ancient inhabitants more than 2,500 years ago. Archaeologists believe these caves served three distinct historical purposes:

  • Prehistoric Burial Sites (1000 BC – 400 AD): Excavations have revealed mummified remains, bronze jewelry, and silk fragments from ancient trans-Himalayan trade routes.
  • Fortified Defensive Sanctuaries (10th – 14th Centuries): When hostile raiders attacked villages, entire communities retreated up rope ladders into these secure cliffside fortresses with food stores and water cisterns.
  • Buddhist Hermitages & Meditation Retreats: Monks and yogis retreated to the high caves for years of silent meditation. Beautiful painted frescoes and sacred texts have been discovered in these elevated sanctuaries.

Essential Cultural Rules & Etiquette in the Kingdom of Lo

The Loba people are exceptionally warm, gracious, and hospitable. Observing traditional cultural etiquette ensures a respectful, mutually enriching experience:

  • Clockwise Movement: Always walk around Buddhist chortens, stupas, mani walls, and shrines in a clockwise direction (keeping the sacred monument on your right hand side). Similarly, spin prayer wheels clockwise.

Frequently Asked Questions: Upper Mustang Trek (17 Days)

When is the best time to trek to Upper Mustang? ∨
Upper Mustang is unique because it lies in the trans-Himalayan rain shadow of Annapurna and Dhaulagiri, receiving less than 200 mm of annual rainfall. This makes Summer Monsoon (June, July, August) one of the best times to visit, with bright blue skies, warm days (18°C–24°C), and lush green oasis crops. Autumn (September to November) offers crystal-clear mountain views and crisp weather, while Spring (March to May) brings pleasant trekking temperatures and the vibrant Tiji Festival in Lo Manthang.
How long does the Upper Mustang Trek take? ∨
Our standard Upper Mustang package is 17 calendar days from arrival in Kathmandu to final international departure. This incorporates 11 active days on the trail, 1 scenic round-trip flight between Pokhara and Jomsom, 1 dedicated exploration day around Lo Manthang and the Chhoser sky caves, and 5 city days (3 nights in Kathmandu and 2 nights in Pokhara) for permit issuance, sightseeing, and overland transit.
What makes Upper Mustang distinct from other Himalayan treks? ∨
Unlike the lush rhododendron forests and glacial moraines of the Annapurna or Everest regions, Upper Mustang features a dramatic arid high-desert landscape reminiscent of the Tibetan plateau. It is home to 2,500-year-old cliffside sky caves, the 14th-century medieval walled capital of Lo Manthang, preserved Sakya and Nyingma Buddhist monasteries, and deep multi-colored canyons formed by wind and the Kali Gandaki River.
What is the highest elevation reached on the 17-day trek? ∨
The highest overnight destination is Lo Manthang at 3,840 m (12,598 ft), where you spend two consecutive nights. During daylight hiking, you will cross high mountain passes including Nyi La Pass (4,010 m / 13,156 ft) and Gyu La Pass (4,077 m / 13,376 ft) above Muktinath. Because all sleeping elevations remain below 3,900 m, the risk of severe altitude sickness is considerably lower than on treks like EBC or the Annapurna Circuit.
Is Upper Mustang suitable for active beginners? ∨
Yes. For physically active hikers with good cardiovascular endurance, Upper Mustang is one of the most accessible multi-week Himalayan journeys. The trails consist of well-trodden packed dirt, stone paths, and gravel riverbeds without technical rock climbing or glacier travel. Consistent hiking practice, comfortable broken-in boots, and trekking poles are recommended.
What permits are required for Upper Mustang, and what do they cost? ∨
Two official government permits are required: the Restricted Area Permit (RAP), which costs USD 500 per person for the first 10-day block within the restricted zone (north of Kagbeni), plus USD 50 per additional day; and the Annapurna Conservation Area Project (ACAP) permit (NPR 3,000 / approx. USD 25). Both permits are fully included in our 17-day package and processed by Namaste Hiking Trek in Kathmandu.
Can I trek Upper Mustang independently without a licensed guide? ∨
No. Under Nepal Department of Immigration regulations, independent solo trekking is strictly illegal in Upper Mustang. Trekkers must travel with a registered government-licensed mountain guide and book through an authorized Nepalese trekking agency. There must also be a minimum of two registered trekkers per permit application.
I am a solo traveler. Can I still book the Upper Mustang Trek? ∨
Yes! While government immigration regulations require at least two names to issue a Restricted Area Permit, Namaste Hiking Trek routinely matches solo travelers with other trekkers on our scheduled group departures. If you prefer a private trek, we can pair your permit with another group permit application to fulfill legal requirements.
How do flights between Pokhara and Jomsom operate? ∨
Scheduled domestic flights operate early in the morning between 6:15 AM and 9:30 AM using 19-seat STOL aircraft (Twin Otters or Dorniers). The flight takes approximately 20–25 minutes, threading between Annapurna I (8,091 m) and Dhaulagiri (8,167 m). Afternoon flights do not operate due to high thermal winds funneling up the Kali Gandaki canyon.
What happens if Jomsom flights are delayed due to mountain weather? ∨
Mountain aviation relies on visual flight rules (VFR). In the event of morning fog or unexpected adverse weather, flights are placed on standby. If cancellations persist, our operations team immediately arranges private 4WD rugged jeeps via the scenic Beni-Jomsom highway, ensuring you reach Pokhara safely without disrupting international flights.
What are the teahouses and lodges like in Upper Mustang? ∨
Accommodations in Upper Mustang are traditional Tibetan-style mountain lodges built from local whitewashed stone, mud-brick, and timber. Rooms feature wooden twin beds with clean foam mattresses, pillows, and warm blankets. Dining rooms are cozy and heated in the evenings by central stoves burning dried yak dung or wood.
Are private rooms and hot showers available? ∨
Yes. In larger settlements like Kagbeni, Charang, and Lo Manthang, lodges have upgraded significantly in recent years, offering rooms with attached Western flush toilets and solar-heated showers. In smaller hamlets like Chele and Drakmar, facilities are more basic with shared clean toilets and hot water available in buckets for a nominal fee ($2–$3).
What types of meals are provided during the trek? ∨
All meals during the trail portion are included in your package. Menus combine hearty Nepalese Dal Bhat (steamed rice, spiced lentil soup, seasonal vegetable curry, pickles) with Tibetan dishes like vegetable momos, thukpa noodle soup, fresh Tibetan flatbread, tsampa porridge, and Western options like pasta, fried rice, porridge, and eggs.
How do I obtain safe drinking water along the route? ∨
To protect Mustang’s fragile ecosystem, single-use plastic mineral water bottles are strongly discouraged. You should bring two reusable 1-litre water bottles (or a hydration bladder). Lodges provide boiled filtered drinking water for a small fee, or you can use water purification tablets or a SteriPEN UV device.
Is electricity and Wi-Fi available in Upper Mustang? ∨
Most villages now have solar power or micro-hydro electricity. You can recharge mobile phones, camera batteries, and power banks in lodge dining areas (often for a nominal fee of NPR 200–400 per charge). Wi-Fi is available in many lodges up to Lo Manthang via local Everest Link or 4G mobile data (NTC/Ncell), though speeds can be variable.
How severe is altitude sickness on the Upper Mustang Trek? ∨
Because the maximum sleeping altitude is 3,840 m (Lo Manthang), serious Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) is much less frequent than on high-pass treks like Thorong La (5,416 m) or Kala Patthar (5,550 m). However, mild symptoms like mild headaches or shortness of breath on pass ascents can occur. Our guides check your blood oxygen saturation daily with a pulse oximeter.
What medical training and equipment does the guide carry? ∨
All Namaste Hiking Trek leaders are certified in Wilderness First Aid and High-Altitude Medicine. They carry a comprehensive first aid kit containing altitude medication (Diamox/Acetazolamide), oral rehydration salts, pain relievers, antiseptics, and pulse oximeters to assess blood oxygen and pulse rates every morning and evening.
How does emergency medical evacuation work in Upper Mustang? ∨
In the rare case of a severe medical emergency or injury, our guide coordinates with our Kathmandu operations office to dispatch an emergency rescue helicopter. Evacuation flights operate from village helipads directly to international medical facilities in Pokhara or Kathmandu. Your travel insurance must explicitly cover emergency medical helicopter evacuation up to 4,200 m.
How should I protect against Mustang’s wind and dust? ∨
The Kali Gandaki valley experiences strong, dry headwinds every afternoon between 11:00 AM and 3:00 PM. A breathable neck gaiter (buff) or dust mask is mandatory to protect your throat and lungs from fine silt dust. High-protection UV sunglasses (Category 3 or 4) with side shields and SPF 50+ sunscreen are also vital in this high-altitude desert environment.
Do I need special vaccinations before visiting Nepal? ∨
Standard travel vaccinations are recommended: Tetanus, Hepatitis A, Typhoid, and routine childhood immunizations. Malaria is not present in Kathmandu, Pokhara, or the high mountains of Upper Mustang. Consult your local travel clinic 6 to 8 weeks before departure.
How much personal spending money should I bring in cash? ∨
We recommend carrying approximately USD 250 to USD 350 equivalent in Nepalese Rupees (NPR 30,000 to NPR 45,000) in cash. This covers personal expenses not included in the package: hot showers, device recharging, hot & cold drinks, Wi-Fi cards, snacks, and tips for your guide and porters. There are no working ATMs beyond Jomsom.
What is the deposit and payment schedule? ∨
To secure your booking and initiate permit processing with the Department of Immigration, we require a 20% advance deposit via credit card or wire transfer. The remaining 80% balance is payable upon your arrival in Kathmandu during our pre-trek briefing, in cash (USD, EUR, GBP, NPR) or by credit card.
What is the tipping custom for the guide and porter? ∨
Tipping is customary in Nepal and represents a heartfelt expression of thanks for exceptional service and care. As a general benchmark, groups tip approximately USD 10 to USD 15 per day for the lead guide, and USD 8 to USD 10 per day for the porter (shared among 2 trekkers). Gratuities are presented on the final trekking evening in Jomsom or Kathmandu.
Are luggage transfers and luggage storage included? ∨
Yes. Excess city clothes, hard suitcases, and non-trekking gear can be securely stored free of charge at our hotel in Kathmandu or Pokhara until your return. During the trek, our porter carries your main duffel bag (up to 10–12 kg per trekker), while you carry a light daypack with your essentials.
What is your cancellation and refund policy? ∨
If you need to cancel your trek due to unforeseen personal emergencies, we offer flexible rescheduling options for up to 12 months. Any non-recoverable government permit fees already issued (such as the USD 500 RAP permit) cannot be refunded once stamped by the Department of Immigration, while remaining services can be credited toward future bookings.
