Pikey Peak Trek

The Pikey Peak Trek is a scenic Himalayan trekking adventure in the lower Everest region of Nepal, offering panoramic mountain views, traditional Sherpa villages, Buddhist heritage, and peaceful walking trails away from the busier routes in the Khumbu. Reaching an elevation of approximately 4,065 meters at Pikey Peak, this trek provides an opportunity to enjoy views of Mount Everest and the surrounding Himalayan range without undertaking the longer Everest Base Camp journey or dealing with domestic Lukla flight delays. Combining an overland road approach from Kathmandu to Dhap Bazaar with five walking days through Jhapre, Pikey Base Camp, Jasmane Bhanjyang, Junbesi, and Phaplu, this 9-day journey showcases centuries-old monasteries like Thupten Chholing, rhododendron forests, authentic Sherpa homestays, and what Sir Edmund Hillary famously declared to be his favorite panoramic view of Mount Everest in all of Nepal.

Today is the highlight of the entire trek. You awake between 4:15 AM and 4:45 AM for the pre-dawn summit push to Pikey Peak (4,065 m). Walking under the beam of headlamps, you ascend steady grassy slopes and rocky ridgelines for approximately 1.5 to 2 hours.

Reaching the summit cairn adorned with colorful Buddhist prayer flags before dawn, you witness one of the most spectacular sunrises in the Himalayas. As the first rays of sunlight strike the snowcapped peaks, a breathtaking 360-degree panorama unfolds before you. Sir Edmund Hillary famously considered this view of Mount Everest (8,848 m) to be his personal favorite in all of Nepal.

On a clear morning, you can see not only Mount Everest, but six 8,000-meter peaks including Kanchenjunga to the far east, Makalu, Lhotse, Cho Oyu, and Manaslu, alongside Thamserku, Ama Dablam, and Langtang Lirung. After savoring the moment and taking photos, you descend to Base Camp for a hearty warm breakfast.

Later in the morning, you continue trekking along the high ridge toward Jasmane Bhanjyang (approximately 3,550 m). This descent allows you to enjoy the changing mountain scenery and settle in for a relaxing evening at a lower altitude.

Optional Visit: Sacred Thupten Chholing Monastery

Trekkers interested in local religious traditions may consider extending their stay around Junbesi to visit Thupten Chholing Monastery. Established in the late 1960s by the revered Tibetan lama Trulshik Rinpoche, this secluded mountain gompa is one of the most important centers of Tibetan Nyingma Buddhism in Nepal, home to over 300 monks and nuns.

Excursion Logistics & Etiquette:

  • Monastery Etiquette: Visitors are welcome to observe late-morning puja chanting and butter-lamp ceremonies. Maintain silence, remove footwear before entering prayer halls, and always request permission before taking photographs.

ROUTE LOGISTICS 4 min read

1. Overland Journey Logistics: Kathmandu to Dhap Bazaar via BP Highway

The standard Pikey Peak Trek begins with a scenic overland journey rather than a domestic mountain flight. Departing early in the morning from Kathmandu, we travel southeast along the smooth, Japanese-engineered BP Highway (Banepa–Bardibas Highway), winding through the mid-hills parallel to the Sun Koshi River.

After crossing the river at Ghurmi, the route climbs north through Okhaldhunga into the Solukhumbu district, reaching the high ridge settlement of Dhap Bazaar (2,850 m). The journey covers approximately 240 km and takes 7 to 9 hours depending on mountain traffic and seasonal road conditions.

Key Overland Advantages:
  • Zero Flight Delay Risk: Completely unaffected by Lukla cloud cover, wind shears, or runway closures.
  • Comfortable 4WD Transportation: Private Mahindra Scorpio 4WD jeeps with seasoned mountain chauffeurs.
  • Natural Acclimatization: A gradual transition from Kathmandu's 1,400 m to Dhap's 2,850 m over the course of a full travel day.

Rest stops are scheduled at riverfront roadside restaurants for clean lunch breaks (fresh dal bhat, seasonal curry, and tea). Salleri pickup or drop-off is a separately agreed variation, while Phaplu serves as the default return hub.

ELEVATION GAIN 4 min read

2. Dhap to Jhapre & Base Camp Ridge Progression (Elevation & Trail Profile)

Once on foot from Dhap Bazaar, the trail follows rolling mountain ridgelines that separate the Dudh Koshi and Likhu Khola valleys. The walking progression is deliberate and steady:

  • Day 2 (Dhap to Jhapre - 2,820 m): Approx. 13 km, 4–5 hours. Gentle ascents and descents along dirt roads and quiet pine forest paths, with early views of Numbur Himal.
  • Day 3 (Jhapre to Pikey Peak Base Camp - 3,640 m): Approx. 12 km, 5–6 hours. An elevation gain of 820 m through ancient rhododendron and fir groves, past high yak pastures (kharkas) to the open saddle below Pikey Peak.
Pacing Strategy:

The climb between Jhapre and Base Camp represents the largest single jump in sleeping altitude (+820 m). Maintain a conservative, conversational walking rhythm ("bistari, bistari") to ensure your red blood cell count adapts comfortably without altitude stress.

SUMMIT HIGHLIGHT 5 min read

3. Pikey Peak Summit Ascent Strategy: Pre-Dawn Sunrise at 4,065 m

The pre-dawn summit push to Pikey Peak (4,065 m / 13,336 ft) is the undisputed climax of this expedition. We wake between 4:15 AM and 4:45 AM depending on the month, drink hot black tea or ginger tea in the dining hall, and commence walking under headlamp illumination.

The trail ascends grassy open slopes and a rocky ridgeline for approximately 425 vertical meters over 1.5 to 2 hours. The trail is non-technical with no exposed drop-offs or scrambles, but early morning temperatures often dip between -5°C and -10°C in autumn and spring with brisk wind chill.

Summit Morning Checklist:
  • Layering: Thermal base layer + fleece + windproof down jacket (800-fill).
  • Extremities: Thermal beanie covering ears, windproof ski gloves or mittens, neck buff.
  • Gear: Headlamp with fresh lithium batteries (keep phone/camera batteries inside an inner pocket close to body heat).
  • Hydration: Thermal flask with 0.75–1.0 L of hot water; water bladder hoses will freeze in sub-zero predawn winds.

Reaching the prayer-flag-draped summit cairn before the sun breaks over the eastern horizon rewards you with one of the greatest natural panoramas on Earth.

Sir Edmund Hillary famously stated that Pikey Peak was his favorite viewpoint in all of Nepal—even surpassing the celebrated Kala Patthar. Because Pikey Peak sits slightly south of the main Himalayan wall, you enjoy an unobstructed, panoramic perspective of the entire central and eastern Himalaya across more than 300 kilometers.

Major Peaks Visible from Pikey Peak (4,065 m):

WATER SAFETY 3 min read

9. Safe Drinking Water Protocols & Plastic-Free Responsible Hydration

Staying adequately hydrated (drinking 3 to 4 liters of fluid daily) is the single most effective physiological defense against altitude sickness. However, untreated tap water or stream water in Nepal carries high risks of giardia, amoebic dysentery, and bacterial infection.

Safe Hydration System:
  • Boiled Water from Teahouses: Purchase boiled water every evening at your lodge (NPR 150–250 per liter). Fill your Nalgene bottles before bed and tuck them into your sleeping bag as a hot-water bottle.
  • Chemical Purification Tablets: Chlorine dioxide tablets (Aquatabs or Katadyn Micropur) or iodine drops effectively neutralize all cysts and bacteria with a 30-minute contact time.
  • UV Light / Micro-Filters: Portable SteriPENs or Sawyer Squeeze filters provide fast purification for cold tap water.
  • Plastic-Free Initiative: Single-use plastic bottled water is discouraged to protect fragile mountain ecosystems with no municipal waste processing.
HYGIENE & TOILETS 3 min read

10. Bathroom Facilities, Toilet Hygiene & Mountain Sanitation Realities

Toilet facilities along the Pikey Peak trail reflect traditional rural Himalayan conditions. Understanding what to expect ensures proper preparation and realistic expectations:

  • Shared Facilities: At Dhap, Jhapre, Pikey Base Camp, and Jasmane Bhanjyang, bathrooms are shared among lodge guests located at the end of the hall or in an external outhouse building.
  • Squat vs. Western Style: Higher elevations predominantly feature ceramic Asian-style squat toilets flushed manually using a water bucket and scoop. Junbesi and Phaplu lodges feature Western sit-down toilets.
  • Sub-Zero Night Freezes: In late autumn and winter, flush water tanks and bucket water freeze solid overnight. Guides coordinate warm bucket water in the morning.
Personal Hygiene Packing Essentials:

Mountain teahouses do not supply toilet paper. Pack 2 full rolls of toilet paper, 2 packs of biodegradable wet wipes, a personal hand sanitizer bottle (minimum 70% alcohol), and small ziplock disposal bags to carry out paper where septic disposal is limited.

ALTITUDE SAFETY 5 min read

14. Altitude Acclimatization Curve & Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) Prevention

Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) can affect anyone above 2,500 meters regardless of physical fitness, age, or past athletic background. Understanding how your body acclimatizes ensures early recognition and prompt remediation:

Altitude Milestones & Risk Analysis:
  • Dhap (2,850 m): Moderate altitude; mild headache or shortness of breath on stairs is normal on Day 1.
  • Jhapre (2,820 m): A stable contour night allowing the respiratory system to normalize.
  • Pikey Base Camp (3,640 m): High altitude sleep; pulse oximeter readings typically drop to 82%–88% SpO2. Guides check vitals twice daily.
  • Pikey Peak Summit (4,065 m): Daytime summit outing. You immediately descend to Jasmane Bhanjyang (3,550 m) and Junbesi (2,700 m), adhering strictly to the golden rule: "Climb high, sleep low."

Report any persistent throbbing headache, loss of appetite, dizziness, or nausea to your guide immediately. Rest, hydration, and immediate descent to lower elevations are the definitive cure for high altitude symptoms.

MEDICAL KIT 4 min read

15. Diamox (Acetazolamide) Protocols & Personal Medical Kit Checklist

Diamox (Acetazolamide) is a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor that acidifies the blood, stimulating your brain's respiratory center to inhale deeper and faster during sleep, thereby accelerating natural altitude acclimatization.

Diamox Protocol Guidelines:
  • Dosage: Standard prophylactic dose is 125 mg taken twice daily, starting 24 hours prior to ascending above 2,800 m (at Dhap Bazaar) and continuing through the summit day.
  • Common Side Effects: Harmless tingling sensations (paresthesia) in fingers, toes, and face; increased urination; altered taste of carbonated beverages.
  • Contraindication: Do not take Diamox if you have a known allergy to sulfa-based medications. Always consult your personal physician before departure.

Personal Medical Kit: Pack ibuprofen/paracetamol, throat lozenges (for dry mountain cough), blister treatment (moleskin/Leukotape), rehydration electrolyte salts, Imodium, Pepto-Bismol, and broad-spectrum antibiotics (Azithromycin or Ciprofloxacin) prescribed by your travel doctor.

Difficulty, fitness and preparation

The Pikey Peak Trek is generally considered a moderate Himalayan trekking adventure, suitable for travelers with reasonable fitness who want to experience mountain scenery, traditional Sherpa villages, and a panoramic summit without undertaking a longer expedition to Everest Base Camp. The route reaches approximately 4,065 meters at Pikey Peak and combines several days of walking with a long overland journey from Kathmandu.

The descent toward Jasmane Bhanjyang and Junbesi can also be tiring, particularly after the summit walk.

The overall difficulty depends on your fitness, previous hiking experience, walking pace, health, weather, and the exact itinerary. Travelers who regularly walk, hike, or participate in other endurance activities may find the route manageable, while those with limited recent exercise should allow sufficient time for preparation.

The nine-day itinerary provides a combination of road travel, trekking, mountain viewpoints, and village exploration. However, the walking days still require preparation, and the long drives can contribute to travel fatigue. Building endurance before departure, choosing appropriate footwear, packing sensibly, and following a steady pace help make the journey more comfortable.

Whether you are a first-time trekker, an experienced hiker, a couple, or a small group, understanding the route's physical demands allows you to select a suitable package and prepare realistically for the journey.

How difficult is the Pikey Peak Trek?

The Pikey Peak Trek is best described as a moderate trek with a demanding summit day. Most of the route follows established trails rather than technical climbing terrain, but the combination of elevation, daily walking, and changing trail conditions requires reasonable physical preparation.

The difficulty is not determined by altitude alone. The distance covered each day, the amount of uphill and downhill walking, the condition of the trail, and the ability to recover between stages all influence how demanding the trek feels.

The body responds differently to altitude, and individual reactions cannot be predicted solely from fitness or previous trekking experience. Even people who exercise regularly may develop symptoms during the journey.

The ascent toward Pikey Peak Base Camp increases the sleeping elevation, followed by the summit excursion the next morning. This makes it important to pay attention to how you feel as the trek progresses.

Junbesi is an established Sherpa village with cultural and religious significance in the Solukhumbu region. Depending on your arrival time, you may have the opportunity to explore parts of the settlement or visit appropriate cultural sites where access is available.

The downhill terrain still requires attention. A long descent can contribute to fatigue, so maintain a comfortable pace and take breaks when needed. Good footwear and suitable clothing also remain important as the temperature and trail conditions change with lower elevation.

Junbesi to Phaplu: completing the trekking route

The final main walking stage continues from Junbesi at approximately 2,700 meters toward Phaplu at around 2,470 meters. The route connects traditional settlements and rural landscapes before reaching a transportation point for the return drive to Kathmandu.

This part of the trek allows you to complete the main walking journey while experiencing the lower Solukhumbu landscape. Villages, agricultural land, and local paths provide a final look at the region before the return to the road network.

After reaching Phaplu, you can rest, organize your luggage, and prepare for the drive the following day. Check the transportation arrangements and expected departure time, particularly if you have onward flights or other fixed commitments.

How acclimatization works

Acclimatization is the body's gradual adjustment to a lower-oxygen environment. After ascending to a higher elevation, the body begins making physiological changes that support breathing and oxygen delivery.

These adjustments take time, which is why a person may feel comfortable at one elevation but develop symptoms after ascending further. The response is individual, and previous experience at altitude does not guarantee the same outcome on a future trip.

For the Pikey Peak Trek, acclimatization should be considered throughout the itinerary rather than only on the morning of the summit ascent.

Several factors influence how you respond to altitude

Rate of ascent: Climbing to a higher sleeping elevation too quickly can increase the likelihood of altitude-related illness. The change from lower settlements to the base-camp area requires particular attention.

Sleeping elevation: Spending the night at a higher altitude exposes your body to reduced oxygen availability for a prolonged period. The overnight stay near Pikey Peak Base Camp is therefore an important part of the itinerary to consider.

Previous altitude experience: A history of tolerating altitude can provide useful background, but it does not guarantee that you will remain symptom-free on another trip.

Individual susceptibility: People differ in how they respond to the same elevation and ascent schedule. Fitness alone is not a reliable predictor.

Physical exertion: Strenuous walking can increase fatigue and make it harder to distinguish ordinary exertion from symptoms that need attention.

Existing health considerations: Some medical conditions or medications may affect the suitability of high-altitude travel. Personal medical advice is important when relevant.

Acclimatization is not achieved simply by reaching a particular destination. It develops over time, and there is no single rest period that guarantees protection against altitude illness.

The appropriate approach is to use a realistic itinerary, monitor how you feel, and allow additional time or descend when symptoms and circumstances require it.

Common symptoms of altitude-related illness

Altitude-related illness can develop after ascending to an elevation where your body has not yet adjusted to the lower availability of oxygen. Symptoms may appear several hours after reaching a higher elevation or following an increase in altitude.

Acute mountain sickness is one form of altitude-related illness. Common symptoms can include headache, nausea, dizziness, unusual fatigue, and reduced appetite. Sleep disturbance can also occur at altitude, although it can have other causes.

During the Pikey Peak Trek, it is important to distinguish ordinary exertion from symptoms that persist, worsen, or appear alongside other warning signs.

Common symptoms to watch for include

Headache that develops after ascending.

Nausea or vomiting.

Dizziness or light-headedness.

Unusual tiredness or weakness.

Reduced appetite.

Difficulty sleeping or disrupted sleep.

A noticeable decline in physical performance beyond what you would normally expect.

These symptoms do not automatically establish a diagnosis, and other conditions can cause similar problems. However, symptoms that develop after an increase in elevation should not be ignored.

If you feel unwell at Jhapre, Pikey Peak Base Camp, or elsewhere on the route, tell your guide promptly. Do not assume that the symptoms are simply caused by hard walking, poor sleep, dehydration, or the cold.

The appropriate response depends on the severity and progression of the symptoms. It may involve stopping further ascent, obtaining medical assessment, remaining at the current elevation under appropriate guidance, or descending to a lower elevation.

Do not continue ascending while experiencing symptoms that may indicate altitude illness unless appropriately qualified medical assessment supports that decision.

Serious altitude warning signs

Some altitude-related conditions can become life-threatening and require urgent action. These include high-altitude cerebral edema (HACE), which affects the brain, and high-altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE), which affects the lungs.

Serious warning signs should never be treated as normal trekking fatigue or something to sleep off without assessment.

Important warning signs include

Confusion or altered mental state: The person may become confused, behave unusually, or struggle to make decisions.

Loss of coordination: Difficulty walking in a straight line, severe unsteadiness, or loss of coordination can indicate a serious problem.

Breathlessness at rest: Significant difficulty breathing while resting, particularly if it is worsening, requires urgent attention.

Chest symptoms or a worsening cough: A persistent or worsening cough, chest tightness, or breathing difficulty at altitude may require urgent medical assessment.

Rapid deterioration: A person who becomes markedly weaker, confused, unusually drowsy, or unable to continue walking safely needs immediate assistance.

If serious symptoms occur, do not continue the ascent toward Pikey Peak. Alert the guide, seek urgent medical assistance, and follow the appropriate emergency plan. Descent to a lower elevation is often a critical part of treating serious altitude illness, but it must be coordinated with suitable assistance and the conditions at the location.

Do not leave a severely unwell person alone or rely on an internet connection to determine what to do. The response should follow established emergency procedures and appropriate medical advice.

Frequently Asked Questions for Pikey Peak Trek

Have more questions about the trek?

Planning & Logistics

How long is the Pikey Peak Trek?

∨
The standard itinerary is 9 calendar days from Kathmandu arrival to final departure. This includes 2 travel days by road (Kathmandu to Dhap Bazaar, and Phaplu back to Kathmandu) and 5 full days of walking on the trail through Jhapre, Pikey Base Camp, Jasmane Bhanjyang, Junbesi, and Phaplu.

Why do we drive instead of fly to Lukla?

∨
Pikey Peak is located in the Lower Everest (Solukhumbu) region, accessible via the scenic paved BP Highway to Dhap Bazaar. Approaching by road avoids the frequent weather delays and flight cancellations associated with Lukla Airport while offering a gradual road ascent and stunning rural hill views.

What permits are required and who arranges them?

∨
The trek requires two permits: the Solukhumbu Rural Municipality Entry Permit and the Trekkers' Information Management System (TIMS) Card. When booking an all-inclusive package with Namaste Hiking Trek, our team handles all paperwork and fees in advance in Kathmandu.

What is the best month to trek Pikey Peak?

∨
October and November in autumn offer crystal-clear mountain visibility, dry trails, and golden fall foliage. March to May in spring features mild temperatures, blooming rhododendron forests, and vibrant Sherpa festivals. Winter (Dec–Feb) is clear but cold, while monsoon (Jun–Aug) brings rain and cloud cover.

Can I trek solo or do I need a licensed guide?

∨
Under Nepal Tourism Board regulations, solo foreign trekkers must be accompanied by a licensed mountain guide. Beyond legal compliance, our local Sherpa guides provide indispensable navigation, cultural insights, emergency wilderness response, and lodge coordination.

How do I confirm my booking and pay the deposit?

∨
You can reserve your departure date by paying a 20% refundable deposit via credit card, bank wire transfer, or online payment gateway. The remaining 80% balance is payable upon your arrival in Kathmandu before departing for the trail.

What are the mountain teahouses like on this route?

∨
Teahouses on the Pikey Peak route are family-run Sherpa lodges. Rooms are clean, twin-sharing with foam mattresses, pillows, and warm blankets (sleeping bags recommended). Common dining halls are heated in the evening with wood stoves, providing a cozy atmosphere for meals.

What food and drinks are available on the trail?

∨
Menus feature nutritious, freshly prepared mountain staples including Dal Bhat (steamed rice, lentil soup, vegetables), Sherpa stew (Syakpa), Tibetan bread, Tsampa porridge, fried rice, noodle soups, potatoes, eggs, pancakes, and hot herbal teas.

Can I charge my camera, phone, and power bank?

∨
Yes. Most lower lodges have hydroelectric or solar electricity in the dining hall. At higher camps (Pikey Base Camp), power is solar-dependent. Lodges may charge a small fee ($2–$4 USD) to recharge devices. Carrying a 10,000–20,000 mAh power bank is strongly advised.

Is Wi-Fi or mobile cellular reception available?

∨
Ncell and Nepal Telecom (NTC) 4G coverage is available in Kathmandu, Dhap, Junbesi, and Phaplu. Along the high ridge and at Pikey Base Camp, signal can be intermittent. Some teahouses offer satellite or cellular Wi-Fi vouchers for a small fee.

How do I obtain safe drinking water along the route?

∨
Never drink untreated tap or stream water. We encourage using reusable bottles with water purification tablets (Aquatabs / chlorine dioxide) or UV purifiers (SteriPEN). Boiled filtered water can also be purchased from teahouse kitchens for a small charge.

Are hot showers available in the lodges?

∨
Gas geyser or solar-heated bucket showers are available at lodges in Dhap, Jhapre, Junbesi, and Phaplu (typically NPR 300–500 / $3–$4 USD). At Pikey Base Camp (3,640 m), running water is limited and wet wipes are recommended.

How physically difficult is the Pikey Peak Trek?

∨
The trek is rated Moderate (Grade 3). You walk 5 to 7 hours daily covering 10–16 km with an average elevation gain of 500–800 m. The toughest section is the pre-dawn summit climb from Base Camp (3,640 m) to Pikey Peak (4,065 m), taking 2 to 2.5 hours on an uphill trail.

Can beginners complete this trek successfully?

∨
Yes. Pikey Peak is an excellent entry-level Himalayan trek for reasonably fit beginners. No technical mountaineering skills, ropes, or crampons are required. Regular cardiovascular training (hiking, stairs, jogging) 4–6 weeks prior ensures comfort and endurance.

What is the risk of altitude sickness at 4,065 meters?

∨
Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) can occur above 2,500 m, but our itinerary features gradual elevation gains (Dhap 2,850m • Jhapre 2,920m • Base Camp 3,640m). We touch 4,065 m briefly for sunrise before descending to sleep lower at Jasmane Bhanjyang (3,550m), following the 'climb high, sleep low' rule.

What medical facilities exist along the Solukhumbu route?

∨
Small health posts operate in Junbesi, and the Phaplu District Hospital offers physician care, x-rays, and basic emergency treatment. Our guides carry comprehensive first aid kits, pulse oximeters for daily oxygen checks, and are trained in high-altitude medicine.

What happens if I get injured or cannot continue walking?

∨
For minor illness or fatigue, our guide can arrange a horse or pony in villages like Jhapre or Junbesi, or arrange road vehicle evacuation via jeep from Phaplu or Salleri. In a severe medical emergency, 24/7 helicopter evacuation to Kathmandu is coordinated immediately.

What kind of travel and evacuation insurance do I need?

∨
You must obtain comprehensive travel medical insurance that explicitly covers high-altitude trekking up to 4,500 meters (15,000 ft) and emergency helicopter search, rescue, and repatriation. Bring a paper copy of your policy and emergency insurer hotline.

What essential clothing and gear should I bring?

∨
Key essentials include a 3-layer clothing system (merino base layers, fleece mid-layer, wind/waterproof hardshell), a warm down jacket (rated to -10°C), broken-in trekking boots, -10°C sleeping bag, trekking poles, polarized sunglasses, and a 300+ lumen headlamp.

How much cash in Nepali Rupees should I carry?

∨
There are no ATMs along the trekking trail between Dhap and Phaplu (ATM available in Salleri/Phaplu only). We recommend withdrawing NPR 15,000 to 20,000 ($120–$160 USD) in Kathmandu for personal expenses such as hot showers, device charging, snacks, and tips.

What is the customary tipping amount for guides and porters?

∨
Tipping is customary in Nepal to show appreciation for hard work. We recommend budgeting roughly $10–$15 USD per day for your guide and $7–$10 USD per day for your porter, usually handed in an envelope at the end of the trek in Phaplu or Kathmandu.

What cultural etiquette should I follow when visiting monasteries?

∨
Always walk clockwise around Buddhist stupas, chortens, and mani stone walls. Remove your shoes and hat before entering monastery prayer halls (like Thupten Chholing), ask permission before taking photos of monks, and maintain a quiet, respectful demeanor.

How much luggage can my porter carry for me?

∨
We adhere strictly to IPPG (International Porter Protection Group) ethical weight limits. Porters carry a maximum of 20–25 kg total, typically shared between 2 trekkers (10–12 kg per trekker in the main duffel bag). You carry only your personal daypack with water and layers.

How cold does it get during the summit sunrise?

∨
During the 4:00 AM ascent to Pikey Peak (4,065 m), temperatures typically range between -5°C and 0°C (23°F to 32°F) in spring and autumn, and can drop to -12°C (10°F) in winter with windchill. Wearing a warm down jacket, thermal gloves, and windproof pants is essential.
