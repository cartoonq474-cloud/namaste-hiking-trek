01 Trip Overview & Key Facts

Pikey Peak Trek Overview

The Pikey Peak Trek is a scenic Himalayan adventure in the lower Everest region of Nepal, offering panoramic mountain views, traditional Sherpa villages, Buddhist heritage, and peaceful walking trails away from the busy routes in the Khumbu. Reaching an elevation of approximately 4,065 meters (13,337 feet) at Pikey Peak, this trek provides an exceptional opportunity to enjoy views of Mount Everest and the surrounding Himalayan range without undertaking the longer Everest Base Camp journey or facing the unpredictability of Lukla flights.

This comprehensive nine-calendar-day itinerary includes arrival in Kathmandu, an overland road approach to Dhap Bazaar (2,850 m), five scheduled walking days through Jhapre, Pikey Peak Base Camp, Jasmane Bhanjyang, and Junbesi to Phaplu, the road return, and final departure. Phaplu serves as the standard vehicle pickup point, while Salleri remains a separately agreed variation. No domestic flights are required on the standard route, making the itinerary remarkably dependable and cost-effective.

Pikey Peak (4,065 m) is the conditional Day 5 daytime viewpoint reached via an early-morning summit climb. The highest scheduled sleep is at Pikey Peak Base Camp around 3,640 meters on Day 4, followed by a lower overnight at Jasmane Bhanjyang around 3,550 meters after the summit outing. The trail descends through the tranquil Sherpa heartland of Junbesi (2,700 m) with an optional cultural excursion to the renowned Thupten Chholing Monastery.

Why Choose a Nine-Day Pikey Peak Trek?

The nine-day timeframe strikes an optimal balance between scenic exploration, gradual altitude acclimatization, and practical travel logistics. The main benefits include:

Manageable Walking Days

Daily walking stages average 4 to 6 hours, allowing hikers to maintain a steady, enjoyable pace without exhausting physical pressure.

Gentle Acclimatization Curve

With only two nights above 3,500 meters and immediate descent to 2,700 m after the summit, the risk of serious altitude sickness is significantly reduced.

Zero Flight Cancellations

By utilizing overland Jeep transport along the BP Highway, your holiday avoids the frequent weather delays and baggage stress of Lukla flights.

Deep Cultural Immersion

Spend quality time in centuries-old Sherpa settlements like Junbesi and visit Thupten Chholing Monastery, enjoying warm authentic hospitality.

02 Day by Day Plan

9-Day Pikey Peak Trek Itinerary

Route Sequence: Kathmandu (1,400 m) → Dhap Bazaar (2,850 m) → Jhapre (2,920 m) → Pikey Base Camp (3,640 m) → Pikey Peak Summit (4,065 m) → Jasmane Bhanjyang (3,550 m) → Junbesi (2,700 m) → Phaplu (2,469 m) → Kathmandu (1,400 m).
DAY 1

Arrival in Kathmandu (1,400 m / 4,593 ft) & Trek Preparation

▾
Max Altitude: 1,400 m / 4,593 ft Accommodation: Kathmandu Hotel (Thamel) Meals: Welcome Dinner

Upon arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport (TIA) in Kathmandu, our airport representative will warmly welcome you with traditional khata scarves and transfer you to your hotel in Thamel via private vehicle. The rest of the day allows you to rest, recover from international flights, and explore the bustling streets, cafes, and outdoor gear shops of Thamel.

In the late afternoon or evening, you will meet your licensed Sherpa trekking guide for a comprehensive pre-trek briefing. We will inspect your personal equipment, ensure appropriate warm layers for the summit sunrise, finalize TIMS and local permit documentation, and answer any logistical questions before enjoying a welcome dinner of authentic Nepali cuisine.

DAY 2

Scenic Overland Drive: Kathmandu to Dhap Bazaar (2,850 m / 9,350 ft)

▾
Max Altitude: 2,850 m / 9,350 ft Drive Time: ~8–9 hrs (~240 km) Accommodation: Teahouse / Lodge in Dhap Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

We depart Kathmandu early in the morning by private 4WD Scorpio Jeep or comfortable overland vehicle, traveling southeast along the scenic BP Highway. The route follows the beautiful Sun Koshi River valley, passing terraced fields, vibrant subtropical villages, and riverside settlements like Ghurmi. After crossing the river, the road begins ascending through Okhaldhunga into the higher mid-hills of the lower Solukhumbu region.

As the elevation rises, terraced farmlands give way to pine and rhododendron forests. We arrive in Dhap Bazaar (approx. 2,850 m) in the late afternoon. Dhap is a bustling ridge settlement that serves as the gateway to the Pikey Peak trail. This first night above 2,800 m gives your body its initial exposure to altitude, so we advise staying well-hydrated and enjoying an early night.

DAY 3

Trek from Dhap Bazaar to Jhapre (2,820–2,920 m / 9,580 ft)

▾
Max Altitude: 2,920 m / 9,580 ft Walking Time: 4–6 hrs (~13 km) Net Change: +70 m (gentle undulation) Accommodation: Teahouse / Lodge in Jhapre Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

After a hearty breakfast, we begin the walking portion of our expedition. The trail leaves Dhap along a wide ridge trail offering panoramic views of the northern Himalayan peaks, including the prominent white pyramid of Numbur Himal. The terrain features gentle ascents and descents through pine woodlands, open pastures, and small traditional homesteads.

We pass several Buddhist chortens and stone mani walls inscribed with sacred mantras before arriving at the serene ridge village of Jhapre (approx. 2,920 m). Jhapre features an old Buddhist monastery, apple orchards, and peaceful teahouses. In clear afternoon weather, you can catch your first glimpse of the Everest range rising in the far distance above the ridges.

DAY 4

Trek from Jhapre to Pikey Peak Base Camp (3,640 m / 11,942 ft)

▾
Max Altitude: 3,640 m / 11,942 ft Walking Time: 5–6 hrs (~14 km) Net Ascent: ~720 m Accommodation: Teahouse / Lodge near Pikey Base Camp Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Today is the main climbing stage of the trek, ascending approximately 720 meters. We start from Jhapre climbing steadily through ancient moss-draped rhododendron, hemlock, and fir forests. In the spring months (March–May), these slopes burst into intense shades of red, pink, and white blossoms. Along the way, we pass long mani walls and occasional summer yak pastures known as kharkas.

As we cross the alpine tree line, the landscape opens dramatically into rolling grassy meadows with panoramic vistas on both sides of the ridge. We reach Pikey Peak Base Camp (approx. 3,640 m) by early afternoon. The lodges here are simple, warm, and situated right beneath the summit ridge. After a hot lunch, you can take a gentle acclimatization walk nearby. We recommend preparing your summit daypack, headlamp, and warm layers before an early dinner.

DAY 5 • SUMMIT

Pre-Dawn Climb to Pikey Peak Summit (4,065 m) for Sunrise; Trek to Jasmane Bhanjyang (3,550 m)

▾
Summit High: 4,065 m / 13,337 ft Overnight Alt: 3,550 m / 11,647 ft Walking Time: 5–7 hrs (~11 km) Accommodation: Teahouse in Jasmane Bhanjyang Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

This is the crowning highlight of the journey. We awake around 4:00 AM, equip our headlamps and warmest down layers, and begin the steep, steady hike (1.5 to 2 hours) up the grassy switchbacks to the summit of Pikey Peak (approx. 4,065 m / 13,337 ft). As dawn breaks, you will stand at the prayer-flag draped stone cairn to witness one of the most magnificent sunrise spectacles on earth.

The first rays of sunlight illuminate the snow-clad summits of Mount Everest (8,848 m), Kanchenjunga (8,586 m), Lhotse (8,516 m), Makalu (8,485 m), Cho Oyu (8,188 m), and Shishapangma (8,027 m), alongside Thamserku, Mera Peak, Chamlang, Gaurishankar, and the Langtang Himal. This is the very vista that Sir Edmund Hillary proclaimed his absolute favorite in Nepal.

After ample time for photography, soaking in the panoramic grandeur, and hanging personal prayer flags, we descend back to base camp for a well-deserved hot breakfast. We then pack our gear and follow a scenic ridgeline descent toward Jasmane Bhanjyang (approx. 3,550 m) or Lhamuje, enjoying views of forested valleys and rolling Himalayan foothills.

DAY 6

Trek from Jasmane Bhanjyang to Junbesi (2,680–2,700 m / 8,858 ft) & Optional Thupten Chholing Monastery

▾
Destination Altitude: ~2,700 m / 8,858 ft Walking Time: 5–6 hrs (~12 km) Net Descent: ~850 m Accommodation: Teahouse / Lodge in Junbesi Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Leaving the high ridge behind, we embark on an extended descent through tranquil fir, oak, and dense rhododendron forests. The air warms perceptibly and oxygen levels increase as we descend into the sheltered, fertile Junbesi valley. Along the trail, mountain streams power stone-enclosed prayer wheels that spin continuously day and night.

We arrive at Junbesi (approx. 2,700 m), widely regarded as one of the oldest, cleanest, and most culturally authentic Sherpa villages in Solukhumbu. After lunch, hikers with sufficient energy can make an optional round-trip walking excursion (approx. 3–4 hours) up the valley to Thupten Chholing Monastery. Founded in the late 1960s by the venerable Kyabje Trulshik Rinpoche, this monastery is home to hundreds of Tibetan Buddhist monks and nuns, and serves as a major regional center for meditation and sacred ceremonies.

DAY 7

Trek from Junbesi to Phaplu (2,469 m / 8,100 ft)

▾
Destination Altitude: ~2,469 m / 8,100 ft Walking Time: 3–5 hrs (~10 km) Net Descent: ~230 m Accommodation: Mountain Lodge / Hotel in Phaplu Meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner

Our final day on the trail is a relaxed, gentle walk. We follow the Junbesi Khola downstream, crossing suspension bridges and passing through traditional rural farmland, apple orchards, and stone-fenced pastures. The trail climbs gently past Salung and Benighat, offering pleasant valley views before entering Phaplu (approx. 2,469 m).

Phaplu features a local airstrip, a well-equipped hospital founded by Sir Edmund Hillary's Himalayan Trust, and comfortable mountain lodges. In the evening, we celebrate the successful completion of the Pikey Peak Trek with our dedicated Sherpa guide and porters. This provides a traditional opportunity to thank the crew and share a celebratory meal and customary gratuities.

DAY 8

Scenic Overland Drive: Phaplu to Kathmandu (1,400 m / 4,593 ft)

▾
Destination Altitude: 1,400 m / 4,593 ft Drive Time: ~8–10 hrs (~270 km) Accommodation: Kathmandu Hotel (Thamel) Meals: Breakfast & Farewell Dinner

Following breakfast, we board our private 4WD Jeep for the overland drive back to Kathmandu. The journey passes through Salleri, the district headquarters of Solukhumbu, before winding down through the hills toward Okhaldhunga and rejoined the paved BP Highway along the Sun Koshi River.

We make lunch and refreshment stops along the highway, arriving back in Kathmandu by late afternoon or early evening. After checking into your hotel, you can enjoy a long-awaited hot shower, relax, or pick up local handicrafts. In the evening, Namaste Hiking Trek hosts a celebratory farewell dinner where you can reminisce about your Himalayan summit sunrise.

DAY 9

Final International Departure from Kathmandu

▾
Activity: Hotel Checkout & Airport Transfer Transfer: Private Vehicle Meals: Breakfast

Enjoy your final breakfast at the hotel. Depending on your flight departure schedule, you may have free time for last-minute souvenir shopping in Thamel or a visit to Kathmandu's historic Durbar Square or Swayambhunath (Monkey Temple).

Approximately three hours prior to your international flight departure, our representative will transfer you to Tribhuvan International Airport (TIA) in a private vehicle. As you board your flight home, you will carry unforgettable memories of the 360-degree sunrise over Mount Everest and the sacred Sherpa heartlands of Solukhumbu.

06 Logistics, Culture & Safety

Essential Pikey Peak Trip Details

Everything you need to know about trekking in the lower Everest and Solukhumbu region. Click on any topic below for detailed logistics, cultural etiquette, accommodation standards, and safety guidelines.

1. Solukhumbu Local Area Entry Permits & TIMS Card ▾

Trekking to Pikey Peak requires two primary official permits: the Solukhumbu Rural Municipality Entry Permit (approx. NPR 3,000 / $25 USD) and the Trekkers' Information Management System (TIMS) Card (approx. NPR 2,000 / $17 USD). Depending on the vehicle entry route, a Gaurishankar Conservation Area permit may also be coordinated.

Namaste Hiking Trek manages and purchases all necessary trekking permits and paperwork on your behalf prior to departure. You will only need to provide a clear digital copy of your passport and two recent passport-size photographs upon booking.

2. Overland Route Logistics: 4WD Jeep vs. Lukla Flights ▾

Unlike classic Everest Base Camp itineraries that depend on flights into Tenzing-Hillary Airport in Lukla, the Pikey Peak Trek is approached entirely by road. We travel southeast from Kathmandu along the newly paved, smooth BP Highway following the Sun Koshi River before ascending the mid-hill ridge to Dhap Bazaar (approx. 240 km, 8–9 hours). The return journey from Phaplu to Kathmandu covers approx. 270 km (8–10 hours).

This overland approach guarantees that your trip will never be grounded or postponed by cloud cover, wind, or Lukla runway closures. We utilize sturdy, well-maintained 4WD Mahindra Scorpio Jeeps driven by experienced mountain drivers, ensuring comfort and safety on both paved highways and rural gravel sections.

3. Mountain Teahouse Accommodation Standards along the Ridge ▾

Accommodations along the Pikey Peak circuit are traditional, family-run Nepalese mountain teahouses and lodges. Rooms typically feature two twin wooden beds fitted with foam mattresses, clean bedsheets, pillows, and a heavy blanket. Due to sub-zero temperatures at Pikey Base Camp (3,640 m) and Jasmane Bhanjyang (3,550 m), carrying a personal sleeping bag rated to -10°C is essential.

Bathroom facilities are generally shared among guests on each floor, with a mix of Western seated flush toilets and Asian-style squat toilets. Lower villages such as Junbesi and Phaplu feature larger lodges with occasional attached bathrooms and hot gas showers.

4. Dining, Food Choices & Traditional Sherpa Nutrition ▾

Teahouse menus offer a hearty blend of Nepali staples, Sherpa mountain dishes, and international comfort foods. The premier trekking meal is Dal Bhat Tarkari (steamed rice, yellow lentil soup, spiced vegetable curry, greens, and pickle), providing unlimited free refills of clean carbohydrates and protein.

You will also have opportunities to savor authentic Sherpa specialties, including Syakpa (thick Sherpa potato and vegetable noodle soup), Tsampa (roasted barley flour porridge), Tibetan fried bread with honey, and freshly steamed vegetable momos. We strongly recommend sticking to wholesome vegetarian meals at higher altitudes for optimal digestion and food hygiene.

5. Drinking Water Safety, Boiled Water & Purification ▾

Never drink untreated tap or stream water in Nepal. Bottled mineral water is sold in lower villages (NPR 100–250), but single-use plastic bottles create severe ecological waste on remote mountain ridges where garbage collection is non-existent.

We recommend carrying two 1-liter reusable Nalgene bottles. You can purchase boiled filtered water from teahouses in the evening (which also doubles as a bed-warmer inside your sleeping bag) or fill up from village taps and treat the water using chlorine dioxide water purification tablets (Aquatabs) or a UV SteriPEN.

6. Electricity, Device Charging & Mobile Phone Connectivity ▾

Teahouses in Dhap, Jhapre, Junbesi, and Phaplu are connected to local micro-hydro electricity or the national power grid. However, at Pikey Base Camp and Jasmane Bhanjyang, lodges rely predominantly on solar power systems. Teahouses charge a small nominal fee (approx. NPR 200–400 / $2–$4 USD) to charge phones, cameras, or power banks in the communal dining hall.

Mobile reception from Ncell and Nepal Telecom (NTC) is surprisingly good along high open ridgelines (such as Jhapre, Pikey Peak, and Dhap), allowing 4G mobile data. In deep forested valleys, signal drops intermittently. We strongly advise purchasing a local 4G tourist SIM card in Kathmandu.

7. Hot Showers, Bathroom Facilities & Sanitation ▾

Hot showers are available at most lodges for an extra fee of NPR 300 to NPR 500 ($3–$5 USD), provided via gas geysers or a bucket of solar-heated water. At Pikey Peak Base Camp (3,640 m), where freezing night temperatures drop water pipes below zero, we recommend skipping showers and using biodegradable wet wipes for hygiene.

Bring your own toilet paper and hand sanitizer. Teahouse plumbing cannot handle paper, so always dispose of toilet paper in the provided waste bins next to the toilet.

8. Physical Difficulty, Terrain Profile & Fitness Training ▾

The Pikey Peak Trek is graded Moderate (Grade 3/5). It requires no technical mountaineering gear, crampons, or ropes. Daily walking times range from 4 to 6 hours on well-established stone staircases, packed dirt ridge paths, and forested trails. The only demanding section is the Day 4 climb to base camp (+720 m) and the early Day 5 summit push (+425 m).

Preparation should focus on aerobic stamina (jogging, cycling, swimming, or brisk walking for 45 minutes 3–4 times per week) combined with stair climbing and training walks carrying a 5–6 kg daypack. Fit beginners, active seniors, and families with teenage children frequently complete this trek with complete comfort and success.

9. Pikey Peak Summit Day Strategy & Sunrise Timings ▾

On Day 5, we wake up at approximately 4:00 AM to begin the ascent by 4:30 AM under the glow of headlamps. The trail ascends steady grassy switchbacks directly behind base camp, gaining about 425 vertical meters over 1.5 to 2 hours.

Temperatures on the summit ridge at dawn frequently drop between -5°C and -12°C depending on the season, with gusty morning winds. You must wear thermal base layers, a fleece, your heavy down jacket, windproof gloves, a warm beanie, and a neck gaiter. Carrying a thermos of hot tea or ginger water in your daypack is a wonderful luxury while waiting for the sun to breach the horizon.

10. 360-Degree Mountain Panorama: Hillary's Favorite Viewpoint ▾

When Sir Edmund Hillary was asked which summit provided the best panoramic view in Nepal, he selected Pikey Peak over Kala Patthar and Gokyo Ri. The reason is geography: because Pikey Peak sits farther south at 4,065 m, it offers an unobstructed, perspective-rich panoramic horizon rather than being hemmed in by steep adjacent valley walls.

From the summit cairn, on a clear morning, you can gaze upon an astonishing 360-degree sweep of snow peaks spanning six of the world's fourteen 8,000-meter giants: Kanchenjunga (8,586 m), Makalu (8,485 m), Lhotse (8,516 m), Mount Everest (8,848 m), Cho Oyu (8,188 m), and Shishapangma (8,027 m), alongside Thamserku, Chamlang, Mera Peak, and Numbur Himal.

11. Acclimatization Curve & Altitude Safety Protocols ▾

Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) can theoretically occur above 2,500 meters. However, the altitude risk on Pikey Peak is very mild compared to higher Khumbu expeditions. The highest sleeping altitude is 3,640 meters (Pikey Base Camp) for only one night, and the summit climb to 4,065 meters is completed early in the day followed by an immediate descent to 3,550 m and 2,700 m.

Your guide carries a digital pulse oximeter to check your blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) and pulse every morning and evening. Trekkers should drink 3 to 4 liters of water daily, maintain a steady "pole-pole" (slow and steady) walking pace, and report any mild headaches or nausea to their guide immediately.

12. Medical Emergencies & Evacuation Protocol ▾

All Namaste Hiking Trek guides hold certified wilderness first-aid credentials. If a trekker experiences persistent altitude illness or physical injury, the golden rule of Himalayan medicine is immediate descent. The trail from Pikey Base Camp allows rapid downhill walking to lower elevations within 2 hours.

Furthermore, Phaplu features a full district hospital founded by Sir Edmund Hillary with trained medical doctors. In serious life-threatening medical emergencies, our Kathmandu operations control center coordinates direct helicopter evacuation to premier hospitals in Kathmandu within 1 to 2 hours of notification.

13. Travel & High-Altitude Medical Evacuation Insurance ▾

Comprehensive travel insurance is mandatory for all trekkers joining our expeditions. Your policy must explicitly include medical treatment, high-altitude trekking up to 4,500 meters, and emergency helicopter search & rescue evacuation.

Ensure your policy does not have hidden exclusions for overland trekking or non-technical hiking. Popular reputable insurance providers for Nepal include World Nomads, Ripcord Rescue Travel Insurance, Allianz Global Assistance, and TuGo. Please carry a printed copy of your policy and emergency claim assistance phone numbers.

14. Junbesi Cultural Heritage & Traditional Sherpa Life ▾

Nestled in a lush green valley at 2,700 meters, Junbesi is often described as the cultural cradle of the Solu Sherpa community. Unlike commercialized trekking hubs, Junbesi has preserved its traditional dry-stone Sherpa homes with carved wooden window frames, slate roofs, and tranquil family apple orchards.

A crystalline mountain river, the Junbesi Khola, runs through the village, flanked by ancient chortens and willow trees. Sir Edmund Hillary established one of his earliest Himalayan Trust schools here in 1961, and local Sherpa hospitality in Junbesi remains one of the warmest highlights of the entire trek.

15. Sacred Thupten Chholing Monastery Excursion ▾

Located a scenic 1.5-hour walk up-valley from Junbesi at 3,000 meters, Thupten Chholing Gompa is an active spiritual center of the Nyingma tradition of Tibetan Buddhism. It was established in 1968 by Kyabje Trulshik Rinpoche, who fled Tibet with hundreds of monks and nuns.

Today, over 400 resident monks and nuns practice meditation and traditional chanting ceremonies here. Visitors are welcome to respectfully observe afternoon pujas (prayer chanting accompanied by long horns, drums, and cymbals), view intricate butter sculptures, and leave modest donations for the monastic community.

16. Seasonal Weather Patterns: Spring Rhododendrons vs. Autumn Clarity ▾

The two prime trekking windows are Spring (March to May) and Autumn (September to November). Spring features mild daytime hiking temperatures (10°C to 18°C), longer daylight, and magnificent floral spectacles as wild rhododendrons, magnolias, and orchids paint the lower slopes in vivid reds and pinks.

Autumn delivers the clearest mountain views of the year. Freshly washed by summer monsoon rains, the atmosphere is crystal clear with deep azure skies and stable weather, making October and early November ideal for high-resolution panoramic photography of Mount Everest.

17. Responsible Travel & Leave No Trace Principles ▾

We are deeply committed to sustainable and culturally sensitive trekking. We follow Leave No Trace principles: all non-biodegradable waste is packed out, and we discourage single-use plastic water bottles in favor of refillable systems.

When walking past Buddhist stupas, chortens, and carved mani stone walls, always walk clockwise (keeping the sacred structure to your right side). Always request permission before taking close-up portraits of local Sherpa elders or monks in monasteries.

18. Guide and Porter Welfare & Ethical Labor Practices ▾

Our mountain crew is the heartbeat of Namaste Hiking Trek. We strictly enforce the guidelines of the International Porter Protection Group (IPPG). Our porters carry a maximum weight limit of 20 kg (shared between two clients), receive fair living wages exceeding industry averages, and are provided with warm mountain clothing, suitable footwear, and comprehensive medical insurance.

We never overload our porters, and in the event of illness on the trail, they receive the exact same high-priority medical care and evacuation coverage as our guests.

19. Local Currency, ATM Unavailability & Daily Cash Estimates ▾

There are no functional ATM cash machines along the trail between Dhap Bazaar, Jhapre, Base Camp, and Junbesi. ATMs in Phaplu and Salleri are frequently out of cash or offline due to regional power outages.

You must withdraw sufficient Nepalese Rupees (NPR) in Kathmandu prior to departure. We recommend budgeting approx. NPR 25,000 to 35,000 ($200–$270 USD) in cash per person to comfortably cover hot showers, battery charging, boiled drinking water, snacks, souvenirs, and crew tipping.

20. Tipping Guidelines for Sherpa Guides, Porters & Drivers ▾

Tipping is customary in the Nepal trekking industry and serves as a heartfelt expression of gratitude for the tireless dedication and safety provided by your mountain crew. Tipping is customarily presented on the final evening of trekking in Phaplu.

As a general guideline:

  • Lead Trekking Guide: $10 to $15 USD per day from the group.
  • Trekking Porter: $8 to $10 USD per day from the group.
  • Overland 4WD Driver: $10 to $15 USD total per vehicle journey.
Total gratuity per trekker in a small group generally averages $100 to $140 USD for the 9-day trek.

21. Luggage Storage in Kathmandu & Duffel Weight Limits ▾

You do not need to carry your non-trekking travel clothes, formal wear, or excess luggage onto the mountain trail. Our partner hotel in Kathmandu provides 100% free, secure luggage storage while you are trekking in Solukhumbu.

Namaste Hiking Trek provides a heavy-duty, water-resistant 80-liter company duffel bag during your pre-trek briefing. Pack your trekking gear into this duffel bag (maximum 10 kg per trekker, as one porter carries two bags up to 20 kg total). You will carry your personal daypack (30–38L) with daily water, camera, rain jacket, sunscreen, and valuables (weighing approx. 4–6 kg).

11 Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Got questions about the 9-day Pikey Peak Trek? Here are comprehensive answers to the 24 most common questions asked by our trekkers, organized into 4 helpful categories.

● Planning & Logistics

How long is the Pikey Peak Trek? ▾

The standard comprehensive itinerary is 9 calendar days. This includes Day 1 arrival in Kathmandu, Day 2 overland scenic drive to Dhap Bazaar, 5 walking days through Jhapre, Base Camp, Jasmane Bhanjyang, Junbesi, and Phaplu (Days 3–7), Day 8 return overland drive to Kathmandu, and Day 9 airport departure.

Why do we drive instead of fly to Lukla? ▾

Pikey Peak is located in the lower Solukhumbu region and is directly accessible by road via Dhap Bazaar and Phaplu. Driving via private 4WD Scorpio Jeep along the scenic BP Highway avoids the notorious weather delays, cancellations, and luggage restrictions of Lukla flights, while significantly reducing overall package costs.

What permits are required and who arranges them? ▾

You require a Solukhumbu Rural Municipality Local Area Entry Permit and a TIMS (Trekkers' Information Management System) card. Namaste Hiking Trek arranges and pays for all permits in Kathmandu before your trip commences. You simply provide your passport details and photos.

What is the best month to trek Pikey Peak? ▾

The prime months are April (for vibrant rhododendron blooms and warm spring weather) and October/November (for unrivaled post-monsoon atmospheric clarity, deep blue skies, and crystal-clear mountain panoramas).

Can I trek solo or do I need a licensed guide? ▾

Under Nepal Tourism Board regulations, foreign trekkers are required to have a licensed guide in mountain regions. Even for solo travelers, having a dedicated Sherpa guide enhances safety, takes care of lodge arrangements, provides cultural translation in Sherpa villages, and ensures swift assistance if needed.

How do I confirm my booking and pay the deposit? ▾

You can confirm your trip by paying a 20% advance deposit via credit card or international bank transfer. The remaining 80% balance is payable upon arrival in Kathmandu via cash or card. We offer free date rescheduling up to 30 days before departure.

● Trail Experience & Accommodation

What are the mountain teahouses like on this route? ▾

Teahouses are clean, simple, and family-operated. Rooms offer twin beds with foam mattresses and blankets. The central dining room is heated in the evenings by a wood or yak-dung stove and serves as the social hub for dining, warming up, and socializing with fellow hikers.

What food and drinks are available on the trail? ▾

You will enjoy nutritious meals including Dal Bhat, Sherpa stew (Syakpa), Tibetan bread, vegetable momos, fried noodles, porridge, pancakes, and eggs. Hot drinks include black tea, milk tea, lemon ginger honey tea, and hot chocolate.

Can I charge my camera, phone, and power bank? ▾

Yes. Most lodges have charging stations in the dining room powered by solar panels or local hydro-generators. Teahouses charge a small fee ($2 to $4 USD) per device. We advise carrying a 20,000 mAh power bank to keep your phone and camera charged.

Is Wi-Fi or mobile cellular reception available? ▾

Some lodges offer Wi-Fi scratch cards for NPR 300 to 500. However, 4G cellular data via Ncell or Nepal Telecom (NTC) SIM cards works surprisingly well along high ridges (Dhap, Jhapre, and Pikey Peak). Signal is limited in narrow forested gorges.

How do I obtain safe drinking water along the route? ▾

You can purchase boiled drinking water from teahouse kitchens (NPR 150–250 per liter) or fill reusable bottles from tap water and treat it with water purification tablets (Aquatabs) or a UV SteriPEN. We discourage single-use plastic bottles to protect the environment.

Are hot showers available in the lodges? ▾

Gas-heated showers or hot water buckets are available at lodges in Dhap, Jhapre, Junbesi, and Phaplu for NPR 300–500 ($3–$5). At Pikey Base Camp (3,640 m), hot showers are rarely available due to freezing pipes, so wet wipes are recommended.

● Fitness, Altitude & Safety

How physically difficult is the Pikey Peak Trek? ▾

The trek is graded Moderate (Grade 3/5). It involves 4 to 6 hours of walking per day on stone steps and dirt trails. It is significantly gentler than Everest Base Camp or Circuit treks, making it an excellent introduction to Himalayan trekking.

Can beginners complete this trek successfully? ▾

Yes, beginners with reasonable cardiovascular fitness can complete this trek. Doing regular walking, jogging, or stair-climbing for 4 to 6 weeks before your trip is sufficient preparation.

What is the risk of altitude sickness at 4,065 meters? ▾

The risk of severe Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) is low. You only sleep at base camp (3,640 m) for one night and visit the 4,065 m peak for sunrise, followed by an immediate descent to 3,550 m and 2,700 m. Your guide monitors oxygen levels with a pulse oximeter twice daily.

What medical facilities exist along the Solukhumbu route? ▾

Guides carry comprehensive wilderness first aid kits. For medical needs, Phaplu houses a full district hospital founded by Sir Edmund Hillary with trained medical doctors and emergency facilities.

What happens if I get injured or cannot continue walking? ▾

Your guide will assess your condition. If necessary, you will be escorted down to lower elevation or to the nearest road-accessible junction (Dhap or Phaplu). In severe emergencies, emergency helicopter evacuation is dispatched directly from Kathmandu.

What kind of travel and evacuation insurance do I need? ▾

You must hold insurance that specifically covers high-altitude hiking up to 4,500 meters, emergency medical expenses, and helicopter evacuation. Providers like World Nomads and Ripcord are commonly used.

● Packing, Currency & Culture

What essential clothing and gear should I bring? ▾

Key items include a warm down jacket (-10°C rated), 3-season sleeping bag, broken-in hiking boots, thermal base layers, a 300+ lumen headlamp, trekking poles, sunglasses, and a 30–38L daypack. (See our interactive packing checklist above).

How much cash in Nepali Rupees should I carry? ▾

We recommend carrying NPR 25,000 to 35,000 ($200–$270 USD) in cash per person. There are no reliable ATMs along the trekking route, and teahouses do not accept credit cards.

What is the customary tipping amount for guides and porters? ▾

In Nepal, tipping is customary for trekking staff. Standard guidance is $10–$15 per day for the lead guide and $8–$10 per day for porters, pooled from the group. Overall, budget around $100–$140 USD total per trekker.

What cultural etiquette should I follow when visiting monasteries? ▾

Always walk clockwise around monasteries, stupas, and mani walls. Remove your shoes and hats before entering prayer halls, avoid taking photos during meditation ceremonies without asking, and modest dress is appreciated.

How much luggage can my porter carry for me? ▾

In accordance with ethical porter standards, our porters carry up to 20 kg total (10 kg per trekker) in our complimentary duffel bags. You carry your own daypack (approx. 4–6 kg) with daily water, snacks, camera, and rain gear.

How cold does it get during the summit sunrise? ▾

At 4,065 meters at dawn, temperatures frequently drop to between -5°C and -12°C (23°F to 10°F), often accompanied by cold mountain wind. Wearing thermal base layers, a fleece, your heavy down jacket, warm gloves, and a beanie will keep you cozy.
