Trek Overview

The 14-day Everest Base Camp Trek is the ultimate bucket-list adventure in the Nepalese Himalayas. Beginning with a scenic mountain flight to Lukla, the route follows the historic footprints of Sir Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay Sherpa along the Dudh Koshi river.

Highlight features include acclimatization days in Namche Bazaar, visiting Tengboche Monastery under the shadow of Ama Dablam, standing on Khumbu Glacier at Everest Base Camp (5,364m), and watching the sunrise over Mt. Everest from Kala Patthar (5,545m).

Everest Base Camp Trek Highlights

These aren’t promises pulled from a brochure — they’re the moments our 600-800 trekkers each year keep mentioning in their reviews, in the office when they get back, and in messages months later.

Stand at Everest Base Camp (5,364 m). The walk from Gorakshep is 6 to 7 hours of slow going across rocky glacial debris, with the Khumbu Icefall directly above and Nuptse, Lhotse, and Pumori closing in on three sides. You’re standing where every summit climber sleeps the night before their push.
Climb Kalapatthar (5,545 m) for the actual Everest view. Sunrise or late afternoon — your guide reads the cloud pattern that week and times it for whichever gives the sharper photo. From the summit you see Everest, Nuptse, and Changtse aligned across the horizon.
Fly into Lukla on a 25-minute mountain flight. Tenzing-Hillary Airport (2,860 m) has a 527 m sloped runway built into the side of a hill. It’s a remarkable approach. Bring something to grip with both hands if you’re nervous about turbulence.
Acclimatize the right way. Two full rest days, one at Namche Bazaar (3,440 m) and one at Dingboche (4,410 m). Our guides carry a pulse oximeter and check your oxygen saturation twice daily from Namche onward — this is what most of our reviews mention, that they always felt monitored without feeling rushed.
Spend time at Tengboche Monastery (3,860 m). The largest monastery in the Khumbu, with Ama Dablam framed directly behind it. Afternoon puja is open to visitors when the monks are in residence — your guide will let you know if the timing works for your group.
Walk the Khumbu Glacier moraine. Between Lobuche and Gorakshep, you trace the edge of one of the highest glaciers in the world. On a quiet morning you can actually hear it shifting underneath you.
Cards, momos, and stories in the teahouse dining room. This isn’t in any brochure, but it’s what guests like Sagar’s, Shikhar’s, and KP’s groups bring up most often — the evenings playing cards with your guide in the wood-stove-heated common room, learning a Nepali phrase or two.
Cross the high suspension bridges over the Dudh Koshi. The bridges between Phakding and Namche — strung with hundreds of prayer flags, swaying just enough to make you watch your feet — are one of the trek’s most photographed sections for a reason.
Spot Himalayan tahr and the danphe pheasant. Sagarmatha National Park is genuine wildlife habitat. Early starts above Tengboche give you the best chance of seeing tahr on the slopes and the danphe (Nepal’s iridescent national bird) in the rhododendron forest.
Make it back as a group. Our guides and porters are paid for the full trip whether you finish or not, so they have no reason to push you when you shouldn’t go on — and every reason to help you reach base camp safely. In the last two seasons, the great majority of our trekkers have completed the route. The few who didn’t were turned around early by altitude, not by anything that could have been pushed through.

Phakding to Namche Bazaar

We trek through pine forests along the Dudh Koshi, crossing five suspension bridges (including the high Hillary Suspension Bridge). We enter the Sagarmatha National Park at Monjo, then face a final, challenging steep uphill climb to Namche Bazaar, the historical capital of the Khumbu region.
Distance 11 km / 6.8 miles
Meals B, L, D
Elevation Gain +830 m
Day 04

Acclimatization in Namche Bazaar

📍 Acclimatization 3,440 m ▼
An essential rest day to acclimatize to high altitude. We take a scenic morning day-hike to Syangboche and the famous Everest View Hotel (3,880m) for a stunning panoramic view of Everest, Lhotse, Ama Dablam, and Thamserku before returning to Namche for coffee and exploration.
Hike Duration 3-4 hours
Target Max Alt 3,880 m
Meals B, L, D
Day 05

Namche Bazaar to Tengboche

⏱️ 5-6 Hrs 3,867 m ▼
We trek on a winding scenic trail with views of Everest and Ama Dablam, descending to Phunki Thenga for lunch. From there, we climb steeply through rhododendron forests to Tengboche, home to the largest Buddhist monastery in the Khumbu region, offering unmatched mountain views.
Distance 9.5 km / 6 miles
Overnight Teahouse Lodge in Tengboche
Meals B, L, D
Day 06-08

Trek to Dingboche, Lobuche & Everest Base Camp

⛰️ Summit EBC 5,364 m ▼
We trek past the timberline to Dingboche (4,410m) for a second rest day, then continue via Thokla Pass memorials to Lobuche (4,940m). On Day 8, we trek along the lateral moraine of Khumbu Glacier to Gorak Shep, drop our heavy packs, and hike to Everest Base Camp (5,364m) under the massive Khumbu Icefall.
Key Milestones Thokla Pass, EBC Landmark

Sunrise Kala Patthar Summit & Descend to Pheriche

A pre-dawn, chilly climb to Kala Patthar summit (5,545m), the highest point of our journey. This spot provides the absolute best view of Mt. Everest, Nuptse, and Lhotse bathed in golden sunrise. We return to Gorak Shep for hot breakfast, and descend down to Pheriche.

Trek Trip Details

Altitude & AMS (Acute Mountain Sickness) Safety

The trek reaches a maximum altitude of 5,545m at Kala Patthar. AMS is a real concern. Our itinerary includes two dedicated acclimatization days (Namche Bazaar and Dingboche). Our guides carry oximeters to test your oxygen levels daily, and we carry emergency bottled oxygen. Always hike slowly ("Bistari, Bistari" in Nepali), stay hydrated (drink 4L of water daily), and inform your guide immediately if you experience headache or nausea.

Physical Fitness & Preparation

This trek is rated Challenging. You do not need technical mountaineering skills, but you must have excellent cardiovascular endurance. We recommend starting stair-climbing, jogging, or weekend hiking with a weighted daypack 2-3 months prior to arrival.

Required Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is mandatory for this trek. Your policy MUST explicitly cover hiking up to 6,000 meters and cover helicopter evacuation (emergency rescue flight). We will collect a copy of your insurance policy and emergency contact details before departing Kathmandu.

Permits & Regulations

For this trek, we arrange the Sagarmatha National Park entry permit and the Khumbu Pasang Lhamu rural municipality local permit. The costs of these permits are fully included in your package pricing.

Expedition Route Information

The route commences with a flight into Lukla, following the Dudh Koshi river up to Namche Bazaar. The path then continues through Tengboche, Dingboche, Lobuche, and Gorak Shep, concluding at the Khumbu Glacier base camp.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if the flight to or from Lukla is delayed?

▼
Lukla airport is weather-dependent. Delays can occur, especially in summer/early autumn. We build a buffer day in the schedule, but in case of long delays, we can help coordinate shared helicopter flights (subject to availability and additional fees, often claimable on travel insurance).

What kind of accommodation is available along the route?

▼
We sleep in traditional mountain teahouses (lodges). These are clean but simple. Rooms are typically twin-sharing, unheated, with common toilets. Hot water showers and Wi-Fi are available for small local fees ($3-$5 USD).

Can I charge my phone and camera batteries on the trek?

▼
Yes, you can charge devices in the teahouse dining rooms for a small hourly fee ($2-$5 USD). We highly recommend bringing a high-capacity power bank (20,000mAh+) and keeping your batteries warm in your sleeping bag at night since the cold drains them quickly.
