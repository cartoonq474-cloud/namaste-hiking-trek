Mardi Himal Trek

The Mardi Himal Trek is an 8-day Himalayan journey in Nepal's Annapurna Conservation Area, beginning with travel from Kathmandu to Pokhara and continuing into the tranquil foothills and high ridges beneath Mardi Himal. The route combines terraced hillsides, traditional mountain settlements, dense rhododendron and oak forests, open ridgelines, and dramatic close-up views of Mardi Himal, Machhapuchhre (Fishtail), Annapurna South, and Hiunchuli. The 8-day format provides ample time to experience the complete progression from lower foothills around Pokhara to Kande, Australian Camp, Forest Camp, Low Camp, and Badal Danda toward High Camp (3,580 m). From High Camp, an early morning excursion reaches the Upper Viewpoint (4,200 m) and conditional Base Camp area (4,500 m) before descending through pristine forest via Siding and Lumre back to Pokhara and Kathmandu with full safety, certified mountain guides, and comprehensive acclimatization.

8 DAYS MARDI HIMAL RIDGE TREK ITINERARY

Mardi Himal Trek Overview

Mardi Himal Ridge Trek at a Glance

Approach: Scenic flight/drive Kathmandu → Pokhara, scenic drive to Kande (1,770 m)
Ascent Profile: Gradual rise from subtropical forests to alpine ridge at High Camp (3,580 m)
Summit Outing: Upper Viewpoint (4,200 m) & Base Camp (4,500 m) with front-row Machapuchare view
Exit Route: Direct descent via Siding village (1,750 m) to Lumre roadhead and Pokhara

The Mardi Himal Trek is an exceptional 8-day journey that leads directly up the crest of a high, narrow spur extending south from the sacred peak of Machapuchare (the "Fishtail", 6,993 m). Opened relatively recently compared to older Annapurna circuits, this route offers trekkers an intimate mountain sanctuary experience with fewer crowds, pristine ancient rhododendron forests, and dramatic 360-degree Himalayan amphitheater views.

Unlike valley floor walks where towering peaks remain distant until the final days, the Mardi Himal trail climbs onto the open alpine ridgeline at Badal Danda (3,250 m). From that point onwards, you walk high above the clouds with Annapurna South (7,219 m) and Hiunchuli dominating the western skyline, while Machapuchare rises dramatically right before your eyes. Our carefully paced 8-day schedule provides two nights at High Camp (3,580 m), giving you optimal rest before ascending to Upper Viewpoint (4,200 m) and Base Camp (4,500 m).

Panoramic Ridge

Smart Safety

Sleep low, climb high! By sleeping at 3,580 m and venturing to 4,200 m / 4,500 m on day 5, altitude sickness risks are dramatically lower than on 5,000 m+ circuits.

The 4 Distinct Ecological & Landscape Zones

One of the most remarkable features of Mardi Himal is traversing four distinct ecosystems in just eight days — from warm terraced farmland to alpine tundra:

ZONE 1 • 1,770 m – 2,100 m

Subtropical Foothills & Terraces

Kande to Pothana. Stone stairways, terraced rice paddies, bamboo groves, and panoramic views over the Pokhara Valley.

ZONE 2 • 2,100 m – 3,000 m

Temperate Montane Forest

Deurali, Forest Camp & Low Camp. Ancient moss-draped oaks, giant tree rhododendrons, ferns, and elusive Himalayan wildlife.

ZONE 3 • 3,000 m – 3,600 m

Subalpine Open Ridge

Badal Danda & High Camp. The tree line ends. Dramatic crest walking with dwarf juniper, alpine pasture, and cloud inversions.

ZONE 4 • 3,600 m – 4,500 m

High Alpine Tundra & Moraine

Upper Viewpoint & Base Camp. Glacial scree, dramatic cliff faces, frozen morning winds, and Machapuchare's southern flank.

Mardi Himal Trek Itinerary: Day by Day Details

Our 8-day Mardi Himal Trek itinerary is built around a progressive ascent into the Annapurna Conservation Area, starting from Kathmandu and Pokhara, walking through Kande, Australian Camp, Forest Camp, Low Camp, and Badal Danda before reaching High Camp at 3,580 m (11,745 ft). From High Camp, trekkers embark on an early morning excursion toward the Upper Viewpoint at 4,200 m (13,779 ft) and the conditional Base Camp area at 4,500 m (14,763 ft) before descending via Siding.

The itinerary includes five scheduled trekking days plus intercity travel between Kathmandu and Pokhara. Walking times each day are approximate and may vary depending on trail conditions, weather, group fitness, and altitude adaptation.

The circular descent via Siding provides an alternative exit corridor through pristine oak and bamboo woodlands, preventing the need to retrace steps along the Kande trail and ensuring a dynamic, varied mountain journey.

Before Your Mardi Himal Trek Starts

Your scheduled start date is your departure date from Kathmandu to Pokhara. During your pre-trek briefing in Pokhara, your licensed guide will go through the route details, daypack packing, weather expectations, permit verifications, and high-altitude emergency procedures.

Day 1: Kathmandu to Pokhara

Pokhara – approximately 800 m / 2,624 ft

Route: Kathmandu → Pokhara
Trek: approximately 6–7 hours drive or 25-minute flight
Trek Distance: approximately 200 km / 124 miles (Highway / Flight)
Max Elevation: 800 m / 2,624 ft
Accommodation: 3-Star Hotel in Pokhara (Lakeview)
Meals: Breakfast (Hotel), Lunch and dinner own choice in Pokhara
Overnight: Pokhara

The Mardi Himal Trek begins with the scenic journey from Kathmandu to Pokhara, the principal gateway to the Annapurna trekking region. Pokhara provides the ideal starting point for the mountain section of the itinerary, offering a range of hotels, restaurants, trekking equipment shops, and transport connections allowing trekkers to complete their final preparations before heading toward the trailhead.

The journey can be completed by tourist road vehicle or by a 25-minute domestic flight depending on your preference and operating conditions. Road travel provides captivating views of Nepal's changing hill landscape, terraced valleys, and the Trishuli and Marshyangdi rivers, while flying offers a panoramic mountain transit directly along the Ganesh Himal, Manaslu, and Annapurna massifs.

After arriving in Pokhara, the remaining time is available for relaxing beside Phewa Lake and preparing for the following day's trek. Your trekking guide will meet you for a comprehensive route briefing, check personal equipment, confirm permit registrations, and verify daypack packing. Because Pokhara sits at 800 m, this first night provides a restful, lower-altitude foundation before beginning the gradual ascent into the Annapurna foothills.

Day 2: Pokhara to Kande and Trek to Forest Camp

Kande – 1,770 m / 5,807 ft | Forest Camp – 2,500–2,600 m / 8,202–8,530 ft

Route: Pokhara → Kande → Australian Camp → Pothana → Deurali → Forest Camp
Trek: approximately 6–7 hours walking
Trek Distance: approximately 14–16 km / 8.7–9.9 miles
Max Elevation: 2,550 m / 8,366 ft (Forest Camp)
Accommodation: Mountain Teahouse / Trekking Lodge
Meals: Breakfast, lunch and dinner
Overnight: Forest Camp (Kokar)

After breakfast, a private vehicle transfers you from Pokhara toward Kande (approx. 45-minute drive to 1,770 m), the starting point for the main walking section of the Mardi Himal Trek. This approach avoids walking through road-connected suburbs and immediately places trekkers on clean mountain paths.

From Kande, the trail begins with a steady uphill climb through stone steps, terraced farmland, and pine-oak woods toward Australian Camp (2,060 m). Australian Camp is an iconic lower ridge viewpoint that presents breathtaking early morning panoramas of Machhapuchhre (Fishtail) and the Annapurna range on clear days.

From Australian Camp, the trail descends gently toward Pothana (1,900 m), an authentic Gurung settlement with teahouses and an ACAP checkpoint. The trail then ascends through tranquil forests to Deurali (2,100 m), where trail junctions branch toward Ghandruk and Mardi Himal. Leaving Deurali, the route leaves the main tourist trail and dives into an increasingly quiet, moss-draped forest dominated by rhododendron, oak, and birch.

The final section leads through continuous woodland up to Forest Camp (locally called Kokar, ~2,550 m). This is one of the longer walking days of the itinerary, establishing the steady elevation rhythm needed for the higher ridge. Overnight in a cozy wilderness teahouse at Forest Camp.

Day 3: Forest Camp to Low Camp

Forest Camp – 2,550 m / 8,366 ft | Low Camp – 3,000 m / 9,842 ft

Route: Forest Camp → Rest Camp → Low Camp
Trek: approximately 5–6 hours walking
Trek Distance: approximately 5–7 km / 3.1–4.3 miles
Max Elevation: 3,000 m / 9,842 ft (Low Camp)
Accommodation: Mountain Teahouse / Trekking Lodge
Meals: Breakfast, lunch and dinner
Overnight: Low Camp

The third day continues deeper into the Mardi Himal trekking corridor as the trail climbs through rhododendron, magnolia, and mixed Himalayan cloud forest toward Low Camp. The lush canopy provides refreshing shade during morning sunlight, while the trail follows natural pathways featuring mossy tree roots, stone steps, and winding forest ridges.

As elevation increases from 2,550 m toward 3,000 m, the vegetation gradually changes. The trees become shorter, lichen drapes from the branches, and occasional breaks in the canopy reveal dramatic glimpses of the soaring sheer south face of Machhapuchhre. During spring (March to May), the blooming scarlet, pink, and white rhododendron blossoms transform the route into a natural botanical wonderland.

You will pause for tea or lunch at Rest Camp before completing the final ascent to Low Camp (approx. 3,000 m). Low Camp marks the vital ecological transition where the dense forest begins yielding to subalpine shrubs and open ridgelines.

Low Camp at 3,000 m to High Camp at 3,580 m represents about 580 m of sleeping-altitude gain. Because this 8-day schedule has no separate rest day, our guide monitors your pacing, hydration, and pulse oximeter readings carefully. Enjoy an evening meal in the heated communal dining hall as the sun sets over the Annapurna valleys.

Day 4: Low Camp to Badal Danda and High Camp

Low Camp – 3,000 m / 9,842 ft | High Camp – 3,580 m / 11,745 ft

Route: Low Camp → Badal Danda → High Camp
Trek: approximately 5–6 hours walking
Trek Distance: approximately 7–8 km / 4.3–5.0 miles
Max Elevation: 3,580 m / 11,745 ft (High Camp)
Accommodation: Mountain Teahouse / Trekking Lodge
Meals: Breakfast, lunch and dinner
Overnight: High Camp

Day 4 brings the trek onto the open, windswept Mardi Himal ridge. Leaving Low Camp, the trail climbs steadily through dwarf rhododendrons and alpine scrub before breaking out entirely above the tree line at Badal Danda (Cloud Ridge, approx. 3,200–3,300 m).

Badal Danda is one of the most visually stunning locations in the Himalayas. With the forest behind you, the trail now follows a narrow, panoramic spine. To your left, Annapurna South (7,219 m) and Hiunchuli (6,441 m) plunge dramatically into the Modi Khola valley; to your right, the sacred fluted spire of Machhapuchhre (6,993 m) rises so close you can count the avalanche chutes on its ice walls.

After a warm lunch at Badal Danda, you continue along the undulating ridgeline toward High Camp (approx. 3,580 m). The terrain is exposed to high mountain winds and rapidly shifting mist, making windproof layers essential. High Camp is perched directly on the ridgeline and serves as the principal high-altitude base for the entire Mardi Himal route.

Arriving at High Camp by early afternoon allows plenty of time to rest, acclimatize, hydrate, and enjoy the sunset as golden alpenglow illuminates Fishtail and Mardi Himal. You will have an early dinner and brief guide discussion before turning in early for tomorrow's pre-dawn alpine excursion.

Day 5: High Camp to Upper Viewpoint and Mardi Himal Base Camp Area

High Camp – 3,580 m | Upper Viewpoint – 4,200 m | Base Camp – 4,500 m / 14,763 ft

Route: High Camp → Upper Viewpoint (4,200 m) → Base Camp Area (4,500 m) → High Camp
Trek: approximately 7–9 hours round trip
Trek Distance: approximately 10–14 km / 6.2–8.7 miles round trip
Max Elevation: 4,500 m / 14,763 ft (Peak Outing)
Accommodation: Mountain Teahouse / Trekking Lodge
Meals: Breakfast, trail lunch/snacks, hot lunch at High Camp, and dinner
Overnight: High Camp

Day 5 is the climax, highest elevation point, and most physically demanding day of the Mardi Himal Trek. Your day begins with a pre-dawn wake-up call around 4:00 AM, warm tea, and headlights on as you set out along the dark, frosted ridge toward the sunrise viewpoints.

The trail climbs steeply above High Camp along an exposed, rocky ridge with occasional narrow traverses. As dawn breaks, the eastern sky explodes in shades of violet, amber, and gold, illuminating the jagged summits of Machhapuchhre, Mardi Himal (5,587 m), Annapurna South, Hiunchuli, Gangapurna, and Annapurna I (8,091 m).

After approximately 3 to 4 hours of deliberate, steady ascent, you reach the Upper Viewpoint at approx. 4,200 meters. From this vantage point, Machhapuchhre stands right in front of you—so close it feels within arm's reach. For many trekkers, this expansive panorama represents the full fulfillment of their Himalayan ambition.

Conditioned upon weather stability, snow conditions, time, and guide assessment, parties can continue along the higher alpine ridge toward the Mardi Himal Base Camp area at approx. 4,500 meters. The base-camp area reaches right beneath the vertical ice walls of Mardi Himal and Fishtail. Reaching the Base Camp area is a conditional continuation and must never be confused with climbing Mardi Himal summit (5,587 m), which is an alpine mountaineering expedition.

After soaking in the 360-degree high-altitude panorama and capturing celebratory photos, you carefully retrace your steps along the ridge, descending back to High Camp for a hot lunch, warm drinks, and a deeply satisfying second night of rest at 3,580 m.

Day 6: High Camp to Siding Village

High Camp – 3,580 m / 11,745 ft | Siding – 1,750 m / 5,741 ft

Route: High Camp → Badal Danda junction → Lower Forest Route → Siding Village
Trek: approximately 6–7 hours walking
Trek Distance: approximately 10–12 km / 6.2–7.5 miles
Max Elevation: 1,750 m / 5,741 ft (Siding)
Accommodation: Traditional Village Teahouse / Homestay in Siding
Meals: Breakfast, lunch and dinner
Overnight: Siding Village

After breakfast at High Camp, you begin the major descent from the high alpine zone back toward the warm, lush valley floor. Rather than simply retracing the entire Kande ascent route, the 8-day itinerary follows an alternative exit route toward Siding Village, creating a dynamic circular journey with fresh landscapes.

The trail begins by descending along the upper ridge past Badal Danda toward the Low Camp junction. Here, the trail branches off eastward, plunging into pristine, rarely visited rhododendron, oak, and bamboo forests. The descent is steep and sustained, dropping over 1,800 vertical meters. Trekking poles and controlled foot placement are essential to cushion your knees and leg muscles.

As the altitude drops, the temperature rises pleasantly, and the air becomes noticeably denser and oxygen-rich. Passing through serene moss-draped groves, cascading mountain streams, and isolated farmsteads, you eventually emerge into the traditional Gurung and Brahmin settlement of Siding (approx. 1,750 m).

Siding is an authentic rural village largely unaffected by commercial tourism. Staying overnight in a local village teahouse or community homestay provides an intimate window into everyday Nepali hill farming life, organic local meals, and genuine hospitality.

Day 7: Siding to Lumre and Drive to Pokhara

Siding – 1,750 m / 5,741 ft | Pokhara – 800 m / 2,624 ft

Route: Siding → Kalimati → Lumre → Pokhara
Trek: approximately 3–4 hours walking + 2 hours drive
Trek Distance: approximately 3–5 km walk + 45 km drive
Max Elevation: 800 m / 2,624 ft (Pokhara)
Accommodation: 3-Star Hotel in Pokhara (Lakeview)
Meals: Breakfast, lunch and dinner own choice in Pokhara
Overnight: Pokhara

On Day 7, you wake up to the gentle sounds of village birds and cowbells in Siding. Following a hearty breakfast of fresh eggs, Tibetan bread, and mountain tea, you embark on the final walking leg of the expedition toward Lumre.

The trail follows a scenic, gentle downhill course along the banks of the Mardi Khola river basin. You cross traditional suspension bridges, pass water-powered mills, and traverse terraced rice and millet paddies. The route winds past Kalimati village, where schoolchildren greet you with bright smiles and cheerful 'Namastes'.

At Lumre (approx. 1,200 m), you meet your private 4WD jeep or transport team. Completing the walking segment, you board the vehicle for the scenic 2-hour drive back along the Yamdi Khola highway to Pokhara.

Arriving in Pokhara by early afternoon, you check back into your comfortable lakeside hotel. Treat yourself to a long-awaited hot shower, fresh clothes, and a celebratory meal at one of Lakeside's charming restaurants overlooking Phewa Lake. The evening is yours to enjoy a relaxing boat ride or celebrate your Mardi Himal summit triumph with your guide.

Day 8: Pokhara to Kathmandu

Pokhara – 800 m / 2,624 ft | Kathmandu – 1,400 m / 4,593 ft

Route: Pokhara → Kathmandu
Trek: approximately 6–7 hours drive or 25-minute flight
Trek Distance: approximately 200 km / 124 miles (Highway / Flight)
Max Elevation: 1,400 m / 4,593 ft (Kathmandu)
Accommodation: Hotel in Kathmandu (optional extra arrangement)
Meals: Breakfast at Pokhara Hotel
Overnight: Kathmandu

The final day brings your 8-day Himalayan journey to its official conclusion. After breakfast at your hotel in Pokhara, you board your arranged transportation back to Kathmandu.

Travelers opting for tourist coach or private vehicle will travel along the scenic Prithvi Highway, tracing the turbulent waters of the Trishuli River and climbing over the Thankot Pass into Kathmandu. Travelers who prefer the domestic flight will enjoy a swift 25-minute flight with spectacular aerial views of the entire Annapurna, Manaslu, and Langtang mountain ranges before touching down at Tribhuvan International Airport.

Upon arrival in Kathmandu, our team assists with hotel transfer or onward travel arrangements. The Mardi Himal Trek officially concludes here. We advise travelers departing Nepal on international flights to maintain a suitable buffer time or onward-travel contingency in case of road delays or seasonal flight rescheduling.

1. Where Does the Mardi Himal Trek Route Start? (Pokhara & Kande)

4 min read

The walking section of the Mardi Himal Trek begins at Kande (1,770 m), a road-accessible mountain settlement situated on the Pokhara–Baglung highway. Trekkers are transported by private vehicle from Pokhara to Kande (approximately 45 minutes to 1 hour), directly bypassing suburban roads and entering established mountain trails.

Pokhara (800 m) serves as the primary logistical and operational gateway for the trek. The city is connected to Kathmandu by air (25-minute flight) and road (Prithvi Highway, 6–7 hours). Pokhara is where equipment checks, permit verifications, guide briefings, and luggage separation take place before departure.

The transition from Kande into the foothills avoids the dusty vehicular tracks found in other regions, placing hikers immediately on stone-paved stairs winding through terraced slopes and traditional Gurung homesteads.

2. Kande to Australian Camp, Pothana & Deurali (Lower Foothills)

5 min read

The first walking section from Kande climbs steadily through mixed subtropical woodland and cultivated agricultural terraces toward Australian Camp (2,060 m). The trail consists predominantly of traditional stone steps. Australian Camp is situated on a broad grassy knoll that delivers expansive introductory views of Machhapuchhre (6,993 m), Annapurna South (7,219 m), and Hiunchuli (6,441 m) on clear mornings.

From Australian Camp, the path descends gently through a shaded pine-rhododendron forest toward Pothana (1,900 m). Pothana features picturesque stone lodges, teashops, and the first official ACAP / police checkpoint where trekking permits and TIMS cards are logged.

Beyond Pothana, the trail ascends gradually through temperate broadleaf forests to reach Deurali (2,100 m). Deurali represents a critical trail junction: one path heads west toward Ghandruk and the Annapurna Sanctuary, while the northern path enters the secluded Mardi Himal ridgeline corridor. The Kande → Australian Camp → Pothana → Deurali sequence establishes a gradual physiological warm-up without extreme altitude stress.

3. Deurali to Forest Camp: Entering the Deep Rhododendron Forest

4 min read

Leaving Deurali, the character of the trek shifts dramatically. The trail leaves villages, terraced fields, and vehicular noise completely behind, entering an unbroken wilderness forest dominated by Rhododendron arboreum, Himalayan oak, birch, and bamboo.

The path follows natural ridgelines with exposed mossy tree roots, leaf litter, and occasional stone steps. During spring (March to May), the forest explodes in vibrant hues of crimson, scarlet, and pale pink as the rhododendrons bloom. In the monsoon and post-monsoon transition, the forest is humid and lush, occasionally requiring vigilance for forest leeches.

Forest Camp (locally called Kokar, ~2,550 m) is an isolated wilderness accommodation clearing nestled deep in the forest. It consists of a cluster of friendly teahouses offering wood-stove dining rooms and basic bedrooms. This section provides a calm, meditative walking environment sheltered from strong ridge winds.

4. Forest Camp to Low Camp: The Forest-to-Ridge Transition

5 min read

The trail between Forest Camp and Low Camp (3,000 m) continues to climb steadily through the upper cloud forest. The distance is moderate (5–7 km), but continuous vertical gain requires an even, controlled walking pace.

As elevation crosses 2,800 meters, the forest canopy noticeably thins and changes composition. Tall oak and rhododendron trees give way to dwarf rhododendron species, stunted birch, and alpine scrub. Gaps in the forest allow dramatic, up-close views of the soaring vertical face of Machhapuchhre rising directly ahead.

Low Camp sits at approximately 3,000 meters and marks the vital physiological threshold where the body begins adjusting to lower air pressure. Sleeping at 3,000 m allows the cardiovascular system to produce more red blood cells before the higher ridge ascent.

5. Low Camp to Badal Danda & High Camp: The Alpine Ridge Walk

6 min read

The section from Low Camp through Badal Danda (3,200–3,300 m) to High Camp (3,580 m) is widely considered the most visually spectacular portion of the trek. Leaving Low Camp, the trees disappear within 45 minutes, and the trail steps directly onto a narrow, open alpine ridgeline.

Badal Danda (literally 'Cloud Ridge') lives up to its name: low valley clouds often drift beneath the trail like an undulating ocean of white mist, while snow-clad giants stand crystalline against the cobalt sky. To the west, the massive walls of Annapurna South and Hiunchuli drop sheer into the Modi Khola valley; to the east, the sacred pinnacle of Machhapuchhre looms astonishingly close.

The trail continues along the windswept grassy crest toward High Camp (approx. 3,580 m). High Camp is the highest scheduled overnight settlement on the route, situated in a shallow saddle directly beneath the upper ridge. The environment here is exposed, with temperatures regularly dropping below freezing at night and before dawn.

6. Upper Viewpoint (4,200 m) vs. Mardi Himal Base Camp (4,500 m)

5 min read

A crucial distinction must be made between High Camp, the Upper Viewpoint, and Mardi Himal Base Camp:

  • High Camp (3,580 m): The highest scheduled lodge accommodation and starting base for Day 5.
  • Upper Viewpoint (~4,200 m): Reached after 3 to 4 hours of pre-dawn ridge climbing. This is the primary objective of the Mardi Himal Trek, providing an unobstructed 360-degree panorama of Machhapuchhre, Annapurna South, Hiunchuli, and Annapurna I.
  • Mardi Himal Base Camp Area (~4,500 m): Located 1.5 to 2 hours further along a narrower, exposed rocky crest directly beneath the triangular south wall of Mardi Himal. Continuation to Base Camp is strictly conditional upon group fitness, trail stability, weather, and guide assessment.

Important Clarification: Neither the Upper Viewpoint nor Base Camp involves climbing the summit of Mardi Himal (5,587 m). Reaching the summit is a technical mountaineering expedition requiring climbing permits, ice axes, crampons, and fixed ropes.

7. The Descent: High Camp to Siding Village & Lumre Exit

4 min read

Instead of retracing the ascent trail back through Kande, the 8-day itinerary descends from High Camp past Low Camp junction and branches east toward Siding Village (1,750 m). This circular route design offers fresh scenery, different trail conditions, and an authentic cultural experience.

The descent from High Camp (3,580 m) to Siding (1,750 m) covers approximately 1,830 vertical meters through steep, pristine oak and bamboo woodlands. Trekking poles are highly recommended to reduce joint impact. As you lose altitude, temperatures warm rapidly, and oxygen levels increase, providing an immediate sense of physiological relief.

Siding is an authentic rural Gurung and Brahmin community untouched by large-scale trekking commercialization. From Siding, a gentle 3–5 km walk along the Mardi Khola river valley reaches Lumre (1,200 m), where a pre-arranged 4WD vehicle transfers the group back to Pokhara (approx. 2 hours).

8. Physical Difficulty, Trail Terrain & Technical Requirements

6 min read

The Mardi Himal Trek is graded Moderate to Challenging. It does not require technical climbing skills, mountaineering experience, or ropes under standard seasonal conditions. However, it requires good cardiovascular endurance, strong leg muscles, and mental preparation for sustained elevation changes.

Terrain Characteristics:

  • Lower Sections (Kande to Deurali): Paved stone steps, village trails, and dirt paths with frequent undulating climbs and descents.
  • Middle Sections (Deurali to Low Camp): Forest pathways with mossy tree roots, loose rocks, and occasional mud after precipitation.
  • Upper Ridgeline (Badal Danda to High Camp): Open grassy crests, rocky steps, and narrow dirt tracks exposed to mountain winds.
  • Summit Outing (High Camp to 4,200 m / 4,500 m): Steep rocky switchbacks, scree, and exposed ridges that may have patches of snow or early-morning ice.

The biggest physical challenges are the pre-dawn alpine push on Day 5 (7–9 hours round trip at 3,580–4,500 m) and the long 1,830 m descent on Day 6.

9. Altitude Progression, Acclimatization & Medical Safety

5 min read

Altitude safety on the Mardi Himal Trek relies on the principle of progressive ascent. The route gains sleeping elevation in structured stages: Pokhara (800 m) → Forest Camp (2,550 m) → Low Camp (3,000 m) → High Camp (3,580 m). Crucially, trekkers sleep two consecutive nights at High Camp, using Day 5 as an active daytime excursion to 4,200 m / 4,500 m before returning to sleep at 3,580 m ('climb high, sleep low').

Symptoms to Watch: Trekkers should monitor for signs of Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS), including throbbing headache, dizziness, nausea, fatigue, and loss of appetite. Mild symptoms are common and usually resolve with rest, deep breathing, and hydration (3–4 liters of fluids daily).

Safety Protocols: Our licensed guides carry portable fingertip pulse oximeters to check blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) and heart rate every morning and evening. In the event of worsening symptoms, immediate descent along the ridge is the standard protocol. Helicopter evacuation can be coordinated within hours if required.

10. Best Seasons, Weather Patterns & Monthly Climate Breakdown

5 min read

Himalayan weather on the Mardi Himal ridge varies dramatically with season and altitude. The temperature differential between subtropical Pokhara and the sub-zero alpine ridge at 4,500 m requires careful wardrobe planning.

  • Spring (March to May): Prime trekking season. Temperatures at lower elevations reach 18°C–24°C, while High Camp daytime temperatures range from 5°C–12°C (-4°C at night). Rhododendron forests are in full bloom from late March to late April. Long daylight hours and stable mornings provide excellent trekking.
  • Autumn (September to November): The most popular season. Post-monsoon atmospheric clearing delivers razor-sharp mountain visibility, dry trails, and deep blue skies. Daytime temperatures are pleasant (12°C–18°C lower, 2°C–8°C High Camp). Nights at High Camp drop to -6°C in November.
  • Winter (December to February): Clear skies and fewer crowds, but bitterly cold temperatures. High Camp regularly plunges to -10°C to -15°C at night. Snow and ice can cover the upper ridge between Badal Danda and Base Camp, requiring microspikes.
  • Monsoon (June to August): Heavy monsoon rains bring muddy trails, cloudy skies, and forest leeches in the lower elevations. While the alpine meadows are lush, mountain views are frequently obscured.

11. Teahouse Accommodation, Dining & Mountain Living

4 min read

The Mardi Himal route operates as a traditional teahouse trek. Lodges are family-run mountain inns constructed from local timber and stone. Rooms are typically simple and clean, furnished with two single beds, foam mattresses, pillows, and clean blankets. Because teahouse rooms are unheated, a 3- to 4-season sleeping bag is highly recommended for cold nights at High Camp.

Meals & Dining: Teahouse dining halls are the social center of the lodge, heated in the evening by a central kerosene or wood stove. Menus feature freshly prepared dishes including the national staple Dal Bhat (steamed rice, yellow lentil soup, spiced vegetable curry, and spinach), which is nutritious, filling, and offered with unlimited second helpings. You can also order vegetable noodles, fried rice, potato dishes, pasta, porridge, and Tibetan bread.

Amenities & Connectivity: Hot gas showers are available at Forest Camp, Low Camp, and High Camp for a modest fee (approx. NPR 300–500). Solar or generator electricity allows phone and power bank charging in dining areas (NPR 200–400). Wi-Fi cards and cellular connectivity (NTC and Ncell) operate across several open ridge points.

12. ACAP Permits, TIMS Regulations & Mandatory Guide Policy

4 min read

The Mardi Himal Trek lies entirely within the Annapurna Conservation Area (ACAP), Nepal's largest protected nature sanctuary spanning 7,629 sq km. To trek in this region, all international travelers must hold valid official permits:

  • Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP): Cost NPR 3,000 per person for non-SAARC foreigners (NPR 1,000 for SAARC nationals).
  • TIMS Registration: Trekkers' Information Management System card costing NPR 2,000 per person (NPR 1,000 for SAARC nationals).

Mandatory Guide Rule: As of April 2023, the Nepal Tourism Board (NTB) strictly mandates that all foreign trekkers exploring Nepal's national parks and conservation areas must be accompanied by a government-licensed trekking guide arranged through a registered Nepal trekking agency. Independent trekking without an accredited guide is illegal. Namaste Hiking Trek manages all permit filings, TIMS paperwork, and assigned certified guides seamlessly as part of your package.
