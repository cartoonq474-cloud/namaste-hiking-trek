Trek overview
The Everest Three High Passes Trek is a complete high-altitude trekking circuit through the Khumbu region, combining the three major mountain pass crossings of Renjo La, Cho La and Kongma La with the landscapes and destinations that make the Everest region one of Nepal's most sought-after trekking areas. The route links the Gokyo Valley, Thame Valley, Khumbu Valley and Imja Valley, creating a more extensive Himalayan journey than a conventional Everest Base Camp itinerary.
This trek is designed for travelers who want more than a single destination. Instead of following the standard Everest Base Camp trail and returning along a similar route, the Three High Passes itinerary crosses high mountain terrain between different valleys and combines remote trail sections with major Everest-region highlights.
What is the Three High Passes circuit?
The circuit connects different Khumbu valleys through three named passes above 5,300 m. It combines long ascents and descents with Sherpa settlements, Buddhist heritage, glacial landscapes and two major viewpoint hikes. The detailed 21-day itinerary below is the package route used throughout this page.
The experience is therefore significantly different from a normal teahouse trek at moderate altitude. The Three High Passes Trek requires preparation for long walking days, substantial elevation gain and loss, high-altitude exposure, cold temperatures and changing trail conditions. Some sections can involve snow, ice, rocky terrain and glacial landscapes, particularly around the high passes.
For travelers looking for a challenging Everest-region itinerary with multiple valleys, high passes, Gokyo and the Everest Base Camp area included in one journey, the Three High Passes route provides a comprehensive trekking framework.
How is it different from the classic Everest Base Camp trek?
The Three High Passes Trek is built around variety.
A conventional Everest Base Camp trek focuses primarily on reaching the Everest Base Camp area and returning through the Khumbu Valley. The Three Passes route adds a much broader circuit.
This plan adds Kongma La, Cho La, Renjo La and the Gokyo/Thame-side circuit to the Everest Base Camp visit. It involves more high-altitude stages and more complex route decisions than a straightforward return along the main EBC corridor.

Everest Three High Passes Trek highlights
Cross Kongma La, the highest of the three named passes
Kongma La, at approximately 5,535 metres, is the highest of the three named passes.
The crossing connects the Lobuche and Chhukung sides of the route and takes the trek into highly exposed alpine terrain.
The surrounding landscape includes glaciers, moraine and high mountain slopes.
Kongma La is often treated as one of the major objectives of the complete circuit because the pass requires a sustained high-altitude effort followed by a long descent.
The experience can vary dramatically according to weather.
On clear days, the high terrain provides expansive views across the surrounding peaks and valleys.
After snowfall or under cloud, visibility can decrease substantially and the trail can become more demanding.
A flexible package should therefore allow the guide and group to assess conditions before committing to the crossing.
Connect the Everest Base Camp and Gokyo sides over Cho La
Cho La is the second major pass in the Three High Passes circuit.
The route over Cho La connects the Gokyo side of the region with the Everest Base Camp corridor near the Lobuche area.
The approach is a major trekking highlight because the landscape becomes increasingly alpine as the route rises above the settled valleys.
Depending on season and current trail conditions, trekkers may encounter snow and ice around sections of the crossing.
The crossing requires careful movement, appropriate clothing and footwear, and an understanding that the terrain can be substantially more difficult than ordinary village trekking.
Cho La is therefore one of the days where the Three Passes package demonstrates its difference from a normal Everest Base Camp itinerary.
The route is not merely a longer walk. It requires the trekker to move between valleys through a high mountain environment.
Cho La glacier and the changing terrain
The terrain around Cho La can include glacial and snow-covered sections depending on seasonal conditions.
This creates a visual transition from the rocky terrain around the lower valley to a colder, more exposed high-altitude environment.
For trekkers, the changing surface is part of the attraction but also part of the preparation requirement.
Conditions can vary according to:
Season
Recent snowfall
Temperature
Visibility
Trail maintenance
Weather patterns
The appropriate traction equipment should therefore be based on actual conditions and professional local assessment.
The glacier environment around Cho La also emphasizes the importance of moving at a controlled pace and maintaining adequate spacing within the group.
Cross Renjo La between Gokyo and the Lungden side
Renjo La is one of the three principal high passes of the trek and provides a spectacular transition between the Gokyo and Thame sides of the Khumbu.
The approach to Renjo La gradually leaves the settled lower valley behind and enters more exposed high-altitude terrain. As the trail rises, vegetation becomes increasingly sparse and the surrounding mountains dominate the landscape.
Crossing Renjo La is not simply a viewpoint stop. It is a complete high-altitude trekking day involving a sustained ascent, a high pass crossing and a long descent toward the next settlement.
The route around Renjo La becomes particularly memorable because it connects naturally with the Gokyo Valley.
From the pass and surrounding high ground, trekkers can experience wide views across the Everest region, with the actual panorama depending on weather and visibility.
A well-designed Three Passes package uses Renjo La as more than an isolated challenge. The crossing becomes part of the larger circuit linking the upper Khumbu with Gokyo.
Explore the Gokyo Lakes
The Gokyo Lakes are among the most recognizable landscapes in the Everest region.
The turquoise glacial lakes sit within the high-altitude Gokyo Valley and create a strong visual contrast with the surrounding rocky slopes, glaciers and snow-covered peaks.
The lakes also carry religious significance for both Hindu and Buddhist communities.
The Gokyo wetland system includes a series of lakes rather than one isolated body of water, making the valley a landscape to experience through several viewpoints and walking sections.
For trekkers, the lakes provide opportunities to:
Walk around the Gokyo settlement
Explore the lakeside landscape
Photograph the surrounding mountains
Observe changing light across the valley
Combine the lake area with Gokyo Ri
Continue toward Cho La or Renjo La depending on the itinerary
The visual character of Gokyo can vary significantly according to season and weather. Clear conditions can reveal an expansive mountain panorama, while cloud, snow or changing light can create a completely different atmosphere.
Climb Gokyo Ri when conditions allow
Gokyo Ri reaches approximately 5,357 metres and is one of the major high-altitude viewpoints associated with the Three Passes Trek.
The climb begins from Gokyo and follows a steep route toward the viewpoint.
Although the distance is relatively manageable compared with a complete pass-crossing day, the elevation makes the ascent demanding.
The summit area provides an elevated perspective over the Gokyo Lakes and surrounding Himalayan peaks.
A Gokyo Ri morning can therefore become one of the main visual highlights of the package.
Trekkers often begin early so that they can complete the climb and return to Gokyo with sufficient time for breakfast or onward activities.
The climb should remain subject to weather, acclimatization and individual condition. It is a high-altitude viewpoint rather than an obligatory requirement for every traveler.
Reach Everest Base Camp from Gorak Shep
For packages that include it, Everest Base Camp becomes one of the major destination highlights of the Three Passes Trek.
The route to Base Camp passes through the high-altitude settlements of Lobuche and Gorak Shep before continuing toward the base camp area.
At the Base Camp section, the landscape is dominated by glaciers, moraine, high mountain walls and the surrounding peaks of the Khumbu.
For many trekkers, reaching Everest Base Camp provides the recognizable milestone associated with Everest trekking.
Climb Kala Patthar for the mountain panorama
Kala Patthar reaches approximately 5,545 metres and is one of the highest and most widely recognized viewpoints in the Everest region.
The climb is usually made from Gorak Shep as an early-morning excursion.
The ascent is demanding because of both the gradient and altitude.
The reward is a broad mountain panorama toward Everest and neighboring Himalayan peaks when conditions are clear.
Including Kala Patthar in the package provides another high-altitude viewpoint that complements Gokyo Ri.
The two viewpoints provide different perspectives:
Gokyo Ri focuses strongly on Gokyo Lakes, the Gokyo Valley and the surrounding western Himalayan panorama.
Kala Patthar provides a closer perspective toward Everest and the surrounding peaks above the Khumbu Glacier.
Return through the Thame-side valley
The Thame Valley provides another distinctive component of the Three Passes journey.
Compared with the main Everest Base Camp corridor, Thame represents a quieter side of the Khumbu and is particularly important for travelers interested in the cultural and historical character of the region.
The route toward Thame passes through traditional mountain settlements and landscapes associated with Sherpa life, livestock and historic trade routes.
For the Three Passes circuit, Thame is especially relevant because it forms part of the route around Renjo La.
Including Thame means the package does not simply connect the best-known Everest trekking villages. It also reaches into the upper Khumbu and provides a broader perspective on the region.
Sherpa settlements and Buddhist heritage
The route combines the commercial hub of Namche with Tengboche Monastery, Dingboche, Chhukung, high teahouse settlements and the communities on the return from Lungden. Cultural visits depend on access and local practice, and mountain views depend on visibility.

Everest Three High Passes Trek itinerary: 21 days
This itinerary follows a progressive acclimatization pattern. The route begins in Kathmandu, flies into Lukla and gradually gains elevation through Phakding, Namche Bazaar, Tengboche and Dingboche before entering the high-altitude sections.
The first major acclimatization stage is in Namche Bazaar, followed by a high-altitude acclimatization hike from Dingboche toward Nangkartshang Hill. The route then continues to Chhukung before crossing Kongma La at approximately 5,535 metres.
After Kongma La, the itinerary joins the Everest Base Camp route and reaches Gorak Shep and Everest Base Camp. The following day includes the ascent of Kala Patthar at approximately 5,545 metres before continuing to Dzongla.
The route then crosses Cho La at approximately 5,420 metres, reaches Dragnak or Thangnak and continues into the Gokyo Valley. A dedicated Gokyo day provides time for Gokyo Ri at approximately 5,357 metres and exploration of the Gokyo Lakes.
The final major crossing is Renjo La at approximately 5,360 metres, followed by the descent toward Lungden, Namche Bazaar and Lukla.

Hotel and teahouse expectations
Accommodation is an important part of the Everest Three High Passes Trek package because the route moves through both well-developed Khumbu settlements and remote high-altitude villages where lodging is simple and limited. The 21-day itinerary uses a combination of Kathmandu hotels and local teahouses or mountain lodges along the trekking route, with accommodation standards changing naturally as the trail gains elevation and enters the three-pass circuit.
In Kathmandu, travelers stay in a conventional hotel with substantially more facilities than are available in the mountains. Once the trek begins from Lukla, accommodation changes to the traditional Himalayan teahouse system.
A teahouse is generally a small locally operated mountain lodge providing a bed, dining area and basic guest facilities. In larger settlements such as Namche Bazaar, accommodation can be comparatively comfortable and may offer private rooms, hot showers, charging and internet access. In remote settlements such as Thangnak, Dzongla and Lungden, facilities are more basic because these locations have fewer buildings, lower service capacity and more difficult supply conditions.
The highest part of the itinerary is also where accommodation becomes the simplest. Around Lobuche and Gorak Shep, trekkers should expect cold rooms, basic furnishings and shared facilities to be more common than hotel-style comforts.
The purpose of accommodation on the Three Passes Trek is practical. You need a secure place to sleep, eat, recover and prepare for the next day's route. On a demanding circuit that reaches above 5,500 metres, good preparation and realistic expectations about mountain lodging are more important than expecting luxury facilities.

Why is this a demanding high-altitude trek?
The Everest Three High Passes Trek is physically demanding because of its elevation profile, terrain and length.
The most significant challenges are:
Three high passes above 5,300 metres
Maximum elevations around 5,500 metres
Long trekking days
Steep uphill sections
Long downhill sections
Rocky and uneven trails
Snow and ice under some conditions
Cold temperatures
Reduced oxygen at altitude
Consecutive days of physical effort
Long ascents, descents and consecutive mountain days
The route combines three different demands.
Elevation
The passes rise above 5,300 metres, where the body works under reduced oxygen availability.
At these elevations, walking pace naturally decreases for many travelers. Even a moderate slope can require significantly more effort than the same slope at lower altitude.
Altitude illness is also a separate consideration from fitness. Being physically fit can improve exercise capacity but does not prevent altitude illness. The rate of ascent and individual response remain important factors.
Terrain
The route is not consistently smooth or gently graded.
You may walk over:
Stone steps
Rocky paths
Uneven ground
Loose gravel
Moraine
Snow
Ice
Steep slopes
Long descents
Foot placement becomes increasingly important on high-pass days.
Duration
A long day becomes considerably harder when it follows several previous trekking days.
For example, the Kongma La crossing does not happen at the beginning of the trip. It arrives after the group has already walked from Lukla through Namche, Tengboche, Dingboche and Chhukung.
The same cumulative fatigue applies later when crossing Cho La and Renjo La.
Fitness for the planned stages
A useful preparation benchmark is the ability to comfortably complete long hill walks without being exhausted for the following day.
Before departure, you should ideally be capable of:
Walking for several hours continuously
Climbing sustained uphill sections
Descending for extended periods
Walking on uneven terrain
Carrying a daypack for multiple hours
Completing back-to-back hiking days
What previous trekking experience helps?
Previous high-altitude trekking experience is useful but is not the only indicator of readiness.
Someone without Himalayan experience can prepare adequately through structured hiking and fitness training.
However, previous altitude experience can provide insight into how your body reacts to elevation.
It is important not to confuse previous success at one altitude with guaranteed performance at another. The rate of ascent, maximum sleeping elevation and individual response can all be different.
Your previous experience should therefore be treated as useful information rather than a guarantee.

Leg strength is particularly important because the Three Passes route involves repeated uphill and downhill movement.
Useful exercises can include:

Lunges

Hip-strength exercises
Step-ups are especially practical because the route contains repeated climbing on stone steps and uneven slopes.
Downhill preparation is equally important.
Many trekkers focus heavily on climbing ability while neglecting the muscles required to control the descent.
A long downhill section can produce substantial muscular fatigue even when the actual elevation gain is over.
Why downhill training matters
The Three Passes Trek is not simply a sequence of climbs.
Every major pass requires a descent.
After crossing Kongma La, the route descends toward Lobuche.
After Cho La, the route descends toward Dragnak or Thangnak.
After Renjo La, the route descends toward Lungden.
The body therefore needs to tolerate repeated downhill loading.
Useful preparation includes controlled step-down exercises and long hill descents.
During training, do not automatically stop every time your legs become tired. Instead, gradually teach them to maintain controlled movement while fatigued.
This should always be done within reasonable limits and without creating an injury.

Sleeping elevation and daytime objectives
Sleeping altitude is especially important because the body remains exposed to reduced oxygen while resting for many hours.
Current high-altitude travel guidance recommends controlling the increase in sleeping elevation once above approximately 3,000 metres and incorporating additional acclimatization time as sleeping altitude rises.
The 21-day itinerary follows this principle through staged overnight elevations and dedicated acclimatization days.
This is one reason the itinerary should not be compressed unnecessarily.
Acclimatization and recovery are different
Acclimatization is the body's biological adjustment to altitude.
Recovery is the restoration of energy after physical exertion.
The Three Passes itinerary needs both.
A trekker can be adequately acclimatized but physically exhausted after a long pass crossing.
Conversely, a trekker can be physically strong but poorly acclimatized after ascending too quickly.

Sherpa culture and religious sites
The Everest Three High Passes Trek is a journey through the heart of the Khumbu, where high-altitude trekking is closely connected with Sherpa culture, Tibetan Buddhist traditions, mountain agriculture, trade, religious practice, and village life. The route crosses historic settlements and traditional mountain landscapes rather than passing through an empty wilderness. Namche Bazaar, Tengboche, Dingboche, Chhukung, Lobuche, Dzongla, Gokyo, Thangnak, Lungden, and the Thame Valley each reveal different aspects of life in the Everest region.
The cultural experience becomes especially meaningful on a Three Passes itinerary because the route links several valleys and settlements that have developed distinct relationships with trade, pastoralism, mountaineering, tourism, and Buddhist practice. The walk from the lower Khumbu into the upper valleys gradually changes the landscape from forested trails to alpine settlements, while monasteries, prayer walls, chortens, mani stones, prayer flags, and traditional houses remain visible throughout the route.
A package trek should therefore include more than transportation between overnight stops. Time at monasteries, respectful interaction with local residents, an understanding of village customs, and appreciation of the mountain environment all form part of the experience.
Respect mani walls, prayer wheels, chortens and monasteries; follow local instructions and ask before photographing people or ceremonies. The itinerary passes through living communities. Visits and access depend on local practice, and a cultural stop should not imply that a ceremony or monastery interior is always available.

Everest Three High Passes Trek FAQs
How long is this Three High Passes Trek?
This draft is 21 calendar days from Kathmandu arrival to departure. Days 3–18 are the sixteen scheduled mountain walking/hiking days, including two dedicated acclimatization hikes and the Gokyo Ri outing.
Which passes are crossed, and in what order?
The detailed itinerary crosses Kongma La on Day 10, Cho La on Day 13 and Renjo La on Day 16. It reaches the EBC area between the first and second crossings, then Gokyo before Renjo La.
What is the highest point in this itinerary?
Using this source’s approximate figures, Kala Patthar is the highest planned viewpoint at 5,545 m. Kongma La is the highest named pass at 5,535 m. Gorak Shep is the highest overnight base at 5,180 m.
How difficult is the Everest Three Passes Trek?
It is a demanding high-altitude trekking route.
The difficulty comes from the combination of:
Three passes above 5,300 meters
Long walking days
Steep climbs and descents
High sleeping elevations
Variable weather
Snow and ice on some sections
Remote terrain
Cumulative fatigue
The route requires significantly more preparation than a normal multi-day low-altitude trek.
Can beginners do the Three Passes Trek?
A well-prepared beginner can undertake the route, but it should not be treated as an introductory trek.
A beginner should build cardiovascular endurance, leg strength, downhill ability, and consecutive-day hiking experience before departure.
Do you need previous high-altitude trekking experience?
Previous experience is useful but not an absolute requirement.
What matters more is adequate preparation, appropriate acclimatization, realistic pacing, and the ability to manage prolonged high-altitude walking.
Previous success at altitude does not guarantee that the same person will respond identically on another trip.
Which acclimatization days are included?
The dedicated days are Namche on Day 5 and Dingboche on Day 8. Gokyo Ri on Day 15 returns to the same sleeping base but remains a demanding viewpoint hike, not a full rest day.
What should I do if altitude symptoms develop?
Tell the guide promptly and do not continue ascending when unwell. Worsening symptoms or serious deterioration require urgent assessment and appropriate descent/medical care.
Can one of the passes be skipped?
The route may need to change if a crossing is unsuitable. A locally assessed alternative can add days or remove an objective; no bypass is promised in every condition. Confirm the staffing and cost implications.
Can the Three Passes Trek be done in reverse?
Yes.
A customized itinerary can use a reverse sequence, potentially crossing Renjo La before Cho La and Kongma La.
The acclimatization profile must still be planned carefully.
Can you add extra acclimatization days?
Yes.
Private itineraries can add additional nights where practical.
Extra acclimatization and recovery time can be useful for travelers who prefer a slower schedule.
What is the best season for the Three Passes Trek?
Spring and autumn are generally the main trekking seasons for the route.
The exact choice depends on the traveler's tolerance for cold, snow, rainfall, crowd levels, and changing mountain conditions.
What guide arrangements should be confirmed?
NTB’s published revised TIMS route list specifically names the Three Passes Trek under its licensed-guide and agency-issued TIMS provisions. Confirm the actual staffing, documents and current trip requirements with the operator.
Which trekking documents should be confirmed?
Confirm agency-issued TIMS, Sagarmatha National Park entry and Khumbu local registration. Also have the operator check restricted-area applicability against the actual trail and arrange any additional activity permissions.
Does the itinerary need a restricted-area permit?
Official listings identify Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Ward 5. The operator must check this route’s actual trail against the designation and current instructions. This draft does not establish a blanket automatic requirement or exemption.
Are the Three Passes technical climbs?
The standard trekking route is not normally classified as a technical mountaineering expedition, but the high passes can involve steep, snowy, icy, rocky, and exposed terrain. The exact conditions vary significantly by season.
Do I need crampons or other traction equipment?
The equipment decision depends on the actual snow/ice, trail and group skills. Confirm it with the guide for the departure. A generic checklist or possession of traction equipment is not a guarantee that a crossing is suitable.
Is helicopter evacuation available?
Helicopter evacuation is an emergency service used when medical or safety conditions require rapid evacuation and suitable flight operations are possible.
Nepal Tourism Board advises trekkers to coordinate emergency helicopter evacuation through their local trekking agency.
Does the trekking package include helicopter evacuation?
That depends on the package.
Emergency coordination may be part of the trekking service while the actual helicopter cost remains subject to insurance, the circumstances of the evacuation, and the rescue provider.
The booking terms should state this clearly.
What travel-insurance cover should I check?
Confirm cover for the actual trekking elevations and activities, medical care and medically necessary evacuation, plus applicable disruption benefits. Review authorization, exclusions and excesses directly with the insurer.
What baggage allowance is included?
The source gives a 10–15 kg main-bag planning illustration, not a confirmed package allowance. Obtain the exact personal limit, bag-weight basis, porter ratio, combined load and excess policy in writing.
How many hours do you walk each day?
Walking time varies.
Some stages are around three to five hours, while demanding pass-crossing days can require approximately seven to eight hours or more depending on conditions and group pace.
What type of accommodation is available?
The route primarily uses teahouses and mountain lodges, with more conventional hotel accommodation in Kathmandu.
Room facilities vary by settlement and altitude.
What food is available?
Teahouses commonly serve dal bhat, noodles, pasta, rice, momos, soup, potatoes, eggs, bread, and other meals.
Menu variety becomes narrower at higher elevations.
Can people with food allergies trek?
Potentially, but allergy management requires advance preparation.
Tell the guide and accommodation staff clearly what must be avoided, and carry suitable personal emergency medication where prescribed.
What happens if the Lukla flight is canceled?
The itinerary may need to be delayed or adjusted.
Possible solutions include rescheduling the fixed-wing flight, using an alternative transportation arrangement where available, adding accommodation, or modifying the trekking schedule.
The exact financial treatment depends on the booking terms and operating conditions.
How much cash should I prepare?
Calculate personal spending from the actual inclusions and carry a separate contingency reserve. The source’s daily cash range is illustrative. Do not assume that remote ATM, card or online access will be available when needed.
Is Wi-Fi available?
Wi-Fi is available in many major Everest-region settlements and trekking stops, including locations along the Three Passes route. Service quality, cost, and reliability vary.
What does the Three Passes Trek cost?
There is no single universal price because package costs vary by itinerary length, group size, service level, guide and porter arrangements, accommodation, flights, meals, permits, and whether the trip is private or shared.
The exact package quotation should therefore be evaluated from its complete inclusions.
What should I confirm before paying?
Confirm the operator, Kongma La-first route, acclimatization, all overnight/services, guide and porter arrangements, baggage, permits, flight airport/transfers, insurance responsibility, delay/route-change costs, deposit/refund terms and the full dated price.
